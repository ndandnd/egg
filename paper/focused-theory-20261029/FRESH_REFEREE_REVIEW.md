# Fresh skeptical referee review

Source-only review, 29 October 2026. I read the six requested sources and the directly relevant common-loss, matching/support, common-transition, complete-graph and strict-curvature proofs. I did not inspect numerical results, raw data, journals, implementations or certificates, and ran no experiments or compilation. No external literature search was necessary to resolve the mathematical questions below. Accordingly, empirical/certificate assertions are unverified here.

## Verdict

The central two-batch physical projection appears exact under its unusually explicit laws. I found no substantive SOC, connector-time or matching-dominance counterexample within those laws. This is a credible restricted structural result, but the principal rejection risk is significance and novelty, not the central proof. It should not be sold as general bus scheduling, realistic battery aggregation, or a new pricing theorem. The current source largely respects these boundaries.

## Exactness and possible hidden constraints

**No proved central flaw.** For a fixed matching the paired, A-only and B-only endpoint intervals are correct: their sums give the stated lower inventory bound and upper headroom bound; their positive lower endpoints give the compulsory purchase requirement R. At any permitted total Q, the constructive allocation with total positive increment max(R,Q) is valid because every interval contains zero unless it has a compulsory positive lower endpoint. This yields a physically feasible one-direction baseline.

The remaining purchase/export totals are exactly a battery-neutral cycle, with residual exports alpha*delta times residual purchases. At any initial SOC one of the two cycle orientations permits depth at least B/2. Repeating finitely many chunks returns to the same SOC before baseline exchange. This avoids both simultaneous charge/export and a closure-only/Zeno argument. Gross throughput, rather than net load, bounds the serial connector time; all vehicles' common availability makes serialization sufficient. Endpoint feasibility plus nonnegative subsequent consumption makes intermediate service SOC feasible. Terminal refill is individually nonnegative and can also be serialized.

**The minimum-r matching does not silently optimize a second matching-dependent quantity in this model.** At fixed cardinality, total consumption, fleet size, total headroom, inventory lower bound, fee and access are constant. Only R changes. Decreasing R therefore enlarges the complete (C,X) polygon at identical loads and fee. This is exact set containment, stronger than monotone economic dominance. Edge-dependent energy breaks precisely the fixed-total premise, as the counterexample correctly demonstrates.

The proof depends materially on universal common-window access, adequate common acceptance power, zero switching/setup delay, no minimum duration, no nonlinear wear, and settlement depending only on aggregate periods. A reviewer may object to these assumptions' importance, but importing one of those omitted constraints is not a counterexample to the stated theorem. The directional-power and affine-wear corollary correctly retains gross quantities in the cost image and supplies a finite, conservative action bound.

## Setup-time quantifiers

**Minor formal omission, with a direct repair:** Proposition `prop:setupslack` says an anchor z_m “has h(z_m) <= 1-sigma_m” but does not explicitly require **z_m in W_m^0**. Its proof needs that membership to preserve every other halfspace by contraction. Add it in the proposition. If “anchor” is understood to imply membership, this is a statement-precision issue; interpreted literally without membership, the proposition is false. For example, its own saturated-paired-layer discontinuity example could select the infeasible zero point as an alleged slack anchor, incorrectly deriving continuity.

**With feasible anchors, the substantive quantifiers and bounds are correct.** Every nonempty outer layer needs a positive-slack anchor; epsilon is the maximum ratio over those layers, and the assertion is restricted to epsilon <= 1. The common epsilon contracts each component enough, including in a hull mixture. Convexity bounds an anchor mixture's objective by M. Both nonnegative value increments lie in the same interval [0, epsilon(M-CH_0)], so their difference needs no factor two. Infima avoid unjustified compactness/attainment of the positive-setup set. This is a fixed-instance, sufficiently-small-tau result, not uniform robustness over fleet families. The discontinuity example convincingly shows why a mere nonempty setup-free model does not suffice. The text correctly avoids claiming minimum-r sufficiency with setup.

## Concrete grounds for rejection

1. **Theory novelty/significance objection (scope judgment, not demonstrated prior-art subsumption).** The central reduction combines interval aggregation, standard cardinality minimum-cost matching, a simple finite time-sharing construction, and planar halfspace counting. A theory referee could judge the synthesis useful but too elementary and too dependent on two simultaneous batches to constitute a substantial advance. The key question is whether retaining assignment-dependent cost creates a reusable structural insight beyond a deliberately tractable toy class. No newly established hardness or broad algorithmic speedup supports that case. I have not established that an existing theorem already states this exact joint projection.
2. **Scheduling objection (scope limitation).** The common windows remove the very release-time, travel-time and resource-contention structure that makes electric-bus scheduling difficult. A small polygon description after computing standard matching values does not solve the operational generalization. The separate third-batch boundary explains the restriction but does not increase its practical reach.
3. **Energy/pricing objection (scope and significance).** Once a cost-labelled nonconvex feasible set is supplied, support failure follows classical convex-hull pricing theory. The graph-complete positive-gap construction uses compulsory abstract edge tasks and a binding fleet cap; every perfect matching being feasible does not make those tasks a road-realistic model. Its local relative neighborhood establishes mathematical persistence, not prevalence, and does not preserve arbitrary changes in physics or access. These objections survive strong convexity and strict feasibility margins.
4. **Public-case evidence (not independently assessed here).** A model-specific positive separation with synthetic coefficients can demonstrate existence beyond common windows. It cannot establish calibrated economic importance. The source properly distinguishes the two energy laws and does not claim an exact hull optimum. None of my concerns requires retracting those narrower claims.

## What an ICML reviewer would require

The supplied contribution is mathematical optimization and energy-market modeling, with no claimed learning result. Source correctness and an internal exact certificate alone do not establish ICML fit. A serious ICML submission would need a substantive learning question and contribution: for example, learned uncertainty or consumption feeding an assignment/pricing decision, a principled decision or support guarantee under estimation error, and evaluation against meaningful predictive/decision baselines on held-out operational instances. Merely adding a predictor to this theorem or using ML to propose schedules would not establish such novelty. Alternatively, the work should target a theory, optimization or energy venue and justify the significance of the restricted structural theorem on those venues' terms.

**Recommendation to the author:** explicitly add feasible-anchor membership; retain the narrow exactness claims; center the paper on assignment-dependent joint cost/load compression and the assumptions that make it work. The remaining acceptance problem is demonstrating why that compression matters enough, not accumulating more author-run qualification cases.
