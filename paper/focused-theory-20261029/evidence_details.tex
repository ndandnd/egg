\section{Implementation qualifications and retained evidence}
\label{app:evidence}
The analytical claims concern the stated ideal models. Exact rational
implementation comparisons use independently formulated physical enumeration
and analytical formulas; agreement is an internal cross-check, not external
replication. The connector/fee qualification contains twenty fixed cases,
and the support-extension qualification fourteen fixed cases, with explicit
boundary and corruption controls. These cases qualify implementations;
the proofs establish the continuous-domain statements.

The heterogeneous adequate-resource specialization matched independent raw
SOC enumeration and threshold formulas on all eight fixed cases; eighteen
negative controls and a smoke anchor passed, including tie and continuous-
response checks. The fixed single-shared-connector extension separately
matched raw SOC enumeration and serial witnesses against sorted-threshold
formulas on all fourteen prospectively fixed cases, with twenty-four negative
controls and a smoke anchor passing. These include physical collinear-threshold
and clipped lower-boundary optima, assignment ties and response continua.
Both are internal qualifications, not external replication; the eight-case
run concerns the separate $n$-connector resource policy.

The common-loss extension matched independent raw inventory/sign-polytope
enumeration on all ten fixed rational cases, retaining every assignment and
infeasible cardinality. Its checks include all projected vertices, exact
full-segment attainment and physical replay, rather than a sampled energy
grid. The hand-anchor control overlaps one case and is not an additional
independent observation. Finite implementation agreement does not prove the
continuum theorem; the constructive proofs are separate.

The complete finite-cycle polygons received a distinct eight-case exact
qualification. All 34 allowed partial duty matchings and 20 cardinality
layers were retained, including resource-infeasible layers. Independently
constructed raw bus polygons, Minkowski sums and resource clipping agreed
with the five-halfspace formula. All 85 case vertex schedules passed one
strict serial-action/SOC/terminal replay. Both polygon containments were
checked, with one fixed matching attaining an entire candidate polygon.
The control anchor and all twelve deliberately corrupted alternatives had
their expected outcomes; the anchor deliberately overlaps one case and is
not an additional independent observation. The cases cover common losses,
forbidden edges, zero/tight budgets and one common transition task. Their
finite implementation agreement is neither a scalability comparison nor
evidence about real-world cycling costs or public-timetable V2G.

Archived outcome and resource records distinguish exact arithmetic from
tolerance-qualified native computations. This evidence summary reads stored
qualification results without rerunning the calculations. The public
positive-gap result uses a source-bound feasible mixture and an independently
reconstructed exact 303-node coverage certificate, with 353 classical native
proposal calls, followed by a retained-partition refinement with 72 new nodes
and 102 new calls. The latter tightly encloses the physical value without
supplying a new hull lower. Neither a finite plan bank nor a native status
alone establishes the conclusion. Failed strict replays and earlier
unsuccessful certification stages remain archived with their costs.

We also packaged the original binary-model evidence with a standalone standard-library
Python checker. One fresh isolated process rechecked both complete physical
points, the fixed mixture, the all-fleet exclusions and the combined 375-node
proof tree, reproducing the stronger lower bound without a solver.
The complete local construction-and-check phase took about 33 seconds.
This author-run command qualification is not an independent external
replication. Source/data redistribution rights remain under review; the
package is currently a local review artifact, not a claimed public release.
The source/input identities and trust boundary are in the accompanying
certificate index.
