\title{Assignment-Dependent Charging and Marginal-Price Support in Electric-Bus Fleets}
\author{Nathan Cho}
\date{Research draft, 29 October 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Service assignments change both an electric-bus fleet's charging opportunities
and its vehicle cost. We derive an exact joint period-load--vehicle-cost projection
for heterogeneous services in two batches with common charging windows,
conversion efficiencies, a fixed successor graph and full replenishment.
When finite repeated charge/discharge actions are allowed without switching
or cycling penalties, one minimum-requirement matching value per cardinality
determines a polygon with at most five vertices. A constructive serial
realization respects individual inventories and the shared gross connector
limit. Retaining these complete polygons preserves projected planning and
price-response values even for negative prices; monotone supply costs admit
a smaller efficient frontier with at most two segments per cardinality.
The lossless specialization gives a scalar own-price support criterion.
Matching, conversion accounting and price-support machinery are classical.
Our physical reduction also has a precise limitation:
edge-dependent compulsory transition consumption can retain a positive support
gap on a complete assignment graph even when every perfect matching is feasible.
The obstruction persists with strongly convex separable supply cost and in a
relative open numerical neighborhood under fixed timing and access. Separately
checked rational certificates establish a positive gap on one 37-service
timetable under explicitly distinguished binary and exact-additive consumption
models. These are model-specific existence results with synthetic coefficients,
not evidence of operational prevalence or calibrated savings.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
An electric-bus operator assigns mandatory services to vehicles and decides
when to recharge them. Continuous charging offers flexibility, but a vehicle
cannot cover a fraction of a service. Assigning two services to the same bus
can therefore impose an intermediate energy requirement that disappears when
two buses are used. A tariff may reward the resulting change in charging
without compensating the operator for its additional vehicle.

We ask a precise question. A supplier has a convex incremental cost for
serving aggregate fleet demand. A tariff is calculated from a candidate
fleet's load and posted before the operator chooses its complete schedule.
Holding that tariff fixed, does the candidate minimize the operator's bill
plus its intrinsic operating cost? This is an own-load price-support test.
Its participant is one operator controlling the complete fleet. It is not a
strategic model in which that operator anticipates a new tariff after each
alternative it considers.
The support test is classical; our contribution is the physical set
description that makes its application explicit for a restricted fleet class.

The difficulty need not be a complicated load-only feasible set. Assigning
services to the same bus can impose an early recharge requirement while
reducing the number of paid vehicles. A fixed-population charging aggregate
does not retain that tradeoff. We identify a restricted physical class in
which it can be described exactly: heterogeneous two-batch services, common
charging windows and conversion efficiencies, a fixed successor graph, shared
gross connector use and full terminal replenishment. A minimum-requirement
matching at each cardinality suffices to construct the joint energy--vehicle-cost
set. With losses and unrestricted finite early direction changes, this set is
a union of polygons having at most five vertices each. Their constructive
realization uses sequential actions, never simultaneous charging and exporting.
Standard convexification then gives planning and own-price support decisions.
For monotone objectives, removing dominated cycling actions leaves at most two
efficient segments per cardinality. Keeping the full polygons avoids that
economic restriction, but relies on ideal switching and period-aggregate
settlement. Gross connector use counts both purchases and exports; full
terminal replenishment prevents free net use of initial battery inventory.

We delineate why this reduction works. A common transition-energy task can
be included because total energy stays fixed within a matching cardinality.
Edge-dependent tasks break that property. A complete-graph construction
admits every perfect matching yet retains a positive gap, even with strongly
convex supply cost and strict feasibility margins. Its relative-neighborhood
statement keeps the discrete topology, charging access and ideal common-window
physics fixed. The example rules out several simple explanations for the
obstruction without claiming generic routing hardness, exponential hull
complexity or empirical robustness. V2G, paid idle fleets and ownership
examples clarify further consequences; their complete arguments are retained
in the relevant sections and appendices.

The public-timetable analysis serves a different purpose. Exact physical
lower bounds and independently admitted complete-plan mixture uppers establish
existence beyond the two-batch class. The original binary-consumption
certificate concerns its declared movement graph. A separately defined
exact-additive law, fresh target certificates and a dominance argument cover
finite walks over specified paired arcs and continuous waiting. These distinct
models share synthetic economics and do not establish empirical road
completeness or operational prevalence. Small exact qualification suites and
the original-model portable verifier address implementation trust, not
generalization or third-party replication.

\section{Implications and limits}
The obstruction concerns one posted-tariff institution with a complete-fleet
participant. It is a possibility under characterized conditions, not an
inevitable failure of marginal tariffs, a strategic tariff-reset equilibrium
or a convergence claim. Convex-hull pricing and general support duality are
established theory. The physical contribution is the explicit assignment-dependent
projection under the stated timing, resource, loss and action assumptions.

The analytical models omit stochastic delays, state-dependent degradation,
temperature, taper and switching time. A common affine-throughput wear
extension remains an ideal cost model, not a battery-aging calibration.
Common windows and efficiencies enable the finite
reduction; arbitrary timetables, vehicle-dependent losses, restricted cycling
and intrawindow price variation require further analysis. Full polygons can
handle negative period prices; the smaller efficient frontier cannot discard
larger loads under a nonmonotone objective. The nominal reserve/loss neighborhood
does not validate those abstractions empirically. The public case uses varied
times and movement energy, but remains charge-only within fixed declared
movement modes and charging windows. Its certificate does not prove
completeness for arbitrary retiming or unlisted access. A separately defined
exact additive law admits a wider result: under its explicit triangle
and charging-access premises, a separate certificate covers arbitrary finite
walks in the paired-arc model. Neither law certifies all empirical road options.
Synthetic coefficients and one base timetable leave calibrated magnitude
and prevalence open.

\section{Conclusion}
Service assignments change the joint load--vehicle-cost set even when its
load-only projection is simple. For the stated common-window class,
cardinality-matching values determine the complete load--cost projection
with common losses and ideal finite cycling. The lossless specialization
gives an exact scalar support classification; monotone objectives admit a
smaller efficient frontier. Gross
connector use, full replacement energy and constructive serial attainment are
essential to that description. A separately checked public-timetable certificate establishes
the same qualitative obstruction in a richer declared charge-only model.
These results identify when continuous charging flexibility does and does
not remove assignment-induced price-support failure, while leaving empirical
significance and more general scheduling classes open.

\section*{Evidence and reproducibility}
Independent physical enumeration qualifies the finite analytical
implementations; constructive proofs establish their continuum statements.
The full common-loss polygons separately matched raw bus-level enumeration
on eight fixed cases, including finite-cycle replay and common transition
energy; earlier one-direction checks have a narrower scope.
Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} preserves all case/control denominators and
their scopes. The public result uses classical native proposals followed by
exact physical admission and rational coverage certificates. One fresh
standalone standard-library Python process rechecked the original binary-model
proof without a solver in a construction-and-check phase of about 33 seconds.
That exported checker does not certify the separate exact-additive model.
This is internal author-run verification, not external replication. The
certificate index binds sources, inputs, exact constants and command.
Earlier failures and costs remain archived. The exported package stays local
pending redistribution review; no public release or substantive learning
contribution is claimed.
