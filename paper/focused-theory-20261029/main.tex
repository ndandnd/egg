\title{Assignment-Dependent Charging and Marginal-Price Support in Electric-Bus Fleets}
\author{Nathan Cho}
\date{Research draft, 29 October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We characterize when a complete electric-bus fleet minimizes its operating
cost and electricity bill at the marginal supply prices associated with
its own load. For heterogeneous services in two simultaneous batches,
a declared successor graph and fixed shared charging resources, we derive
the exact joint load--vehicle-cost frontier with full battery replenishment.
Cardinality-matching thresholds determine where a convexified optimum has
a physical implementation. The load-only set is an interval; its discrete
vehicle-cost layers cause the obstruction. A bidirectional extension accounts
for gross connector throughput and signed electricity settlement. Vehicle-to-grid
operation can preserve or remove a positive gap, and identical paid idle
buses are redundant under a stated fleet cap. Exact examples and parameter
regions distinguish useful flexibility from mere enlargement of the duty set.
The matching and price-support machinery is classical; the contribution is
the complete physical classification under these explicit assumptions.
For one public 37-service timetable, an independently checked rational
branching certificate separates every physical fleet from a feasible
convexified plan, proving a positive gap of at least $0.008790$ in the stated
objective units. This small certified separation resolves existence under
the declared model, not practical magnitude. Synthetic coefficients and ideal
physics limit conclusions about operational prevalence or calibrated value.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
An electric-bus operator assigns mandatory services to vehicles and decides
when to recharge them. Continuous charging offers flexibility, but a vehicle
cannot cover a fraction of a service. Assigning two services to the same bus
can therefore impose an intermediate energy requirement that disappears when
two buses are used. A tariff may reward the resulting change in charging
without compensating the operator for its additional vehicle.

We ask a precise question. A supplier has a convex incremental cost for
serving aggregate fleet demand. A tariff is calculated from a candidate
fleet's load and posted before the operator chooses its complete schedule.
Holding that tariff fixed, does the candidate minimize the operator's bill
plus its intrinsic operating cost? This is an own-load price-support test.
Its participant is one operator controlling the complete fleet. It is not a
strategic model in which that operator anticipates a new tariff after each
alternative it considers.

The connection between price support and convexification is established in
nonconvex market theory. We develop its physical implications for a class of
heterogeneous services in two simultaneous batches. Every assignment allowed
by the declared successor graph is retained, with one fixed shared connector
in each charging window.
An exact joint energy--vehicle-cost projection reduces the complete fleet set to
early-energy thresholds indexed by matching cardinality; standard matching
convexity then identifies the hull and a precise support criterion. The
energy-only feasible set is an interval; the relevant nonconvexity concerns
its cost-labelled assignment layers. Full
terminal replenishment is central: an initially full battery must not provide
free net energy over a finite horizon. Every plan buys the same total service
energy, yet its assignment changes \emph{when} that energy must be purchased,
coupled with the fixed cost of a used bus.

Bidirectional charging changes this frontier through both available battery
inventory and connector time. Charging one bus while discharging another
does not cancel the time spent on a single connector: its resource is gross
throughput. We derive the resulting signed-load thresholds and show exactly
how the obstruction can persist or disappear. Under a total fleet cap,
identical paid idle buses add no projected alternatives beyond those obtained
by separating matched service pairs. This statement concerns the declared
ideal model, not the value of spare buses in general V2G operations.

The heterogeneous class makes the assignment set exhaustive under explicit
timing and resource assumptions. A two-service specialization provides an
accessible illustration and an exact connector-capacity and vehicle-fee
characterization. We then distinguish two kinds of enlargement. Additional
energy-consuming movements under unchanged equipment and charging access
cannot improve either the physical or hull value in the stated construction.
An added battery type changes the equipment set and can restore support. Its
fee matters: a newly admitted plan cheaper than the old hull optimum need
not restore support because the enlarged hull can improve further. Finally,
an exact replication calculation shows why a small planning gap and a small
whole-owner incentive are different statements.

These are application-specific results in a synthetic model. We do not
claim that nonconvex demand or convex-hull price support is a new concept.
The public timetable evidence supplies a separate exact certificate beyond
the two-batch class. A feasible convexified value below an independently
verified lower bound for every physical fleet proves a positive gap for the
declared 37-service model. The separation is small; practical magnitude and
prevalence remain separate research questions.

\section{Implications and limits}
The examples identify a conditional failure of a specified posted-tariff
institution. The supply function is convex; the indivisibility lies on the
fleet side. Continuous energy decisions and terminal energy accounting do
not eliminate that indivisibility. Conversely, an indivisible fleet need
not lack supporting marginal prices: the exact parameter regions include
zero-gap cases, and an added battery choice can physically implement a
supported load. The correct claim is a possibility with characterized
conditions, not inevitable failure of marginal tariffs.

The expansion results are deliberately explicit about resource changes.
The movement-only stability theorem preserves charging access, equipment,
service requirements and nonnegative movement consumption. It says nothing
about a new charging location, regenerative energy, altered travel energy,
service cancellation or a more capable battery. Those changes can alter the
relevant energy projection. The equipment result studies one such change
directly rather than describing an arbitrary projected point as an omitted
feasible duty of the original model.

The participant definition is equally consequential. A whole-fleet deviation
can replace every service assignment. Independent operators need compatible
resource rights before their unilateral alternatives are well defined.
The replication theorem uses reserved service pairs and connectors to make
that comparison exact. It does not infer incentives for independently owned
buses competing for the same unreserved depot connector. Heterogeneous fixed
service pairs cannot generally reproduce the efficient complete-crossmatching
outcome by relabeling; that step in the replication comparison relies on
identical services.

All analytical models omit stochastic delays, battery degradation,
temperature effects, charging taper and switching time. Monetary and supply
coefficients are synthetic. The positive-gap neighborhood admits a reserve,
constant losses and one connector, but remains conditional on those
abstractions. The analytical formulas and exact implementation comparisons
support the declared mechanisms, not estimates of an operator's savings or
an assessment of a deployed market design.

The two-batch theorem allows fixed shared charging resources but assumes
two simultaneous service batches and no travel.
The public timetable has varied service times, movement and waiting energy,
and one shared connector, so it is outside that theorem's scope. Its exact
branching certificate establishes the same qualitative obstruction without
claiming that the two-batch threshold formula applies. Extending the readable
projection to varied timing and movement remains open; one internally checked
case cannot establish operational prevalence or economically significant loss.

\section{Conclusion}
An electric-bus fleet can lack marginal-price support even when charging is
continuous and every used battery is fully replenished. For heterogeneous
two-batch services with allowed successor matchings and positive vehicle fees,
cardinality-matching thresholds determine exactly which hull-optimal loads
are physically supported. Bidirectional charging preserves that structure
with different thresholds: it can leave a positive gap intact or eliminate
it. Gross connector time and terminal replacement remain binding even when
net exports are allowed. The two-service model shows how connector
capacity, vehicle fees and battery options change that answer.
Energy-consuming movement additions preserve the
obstruction under unchanged resources; a sufficiently capable battery can
remove it at an appropriate fee. Neither a cheaper newly admitted plan nor
a vanishing planning gap alone resolves the relevant incentive question.
The public case provides an exact existence certificate in a more detailed
declared model, while leaving practical prevalence and calibrated value open.

\section*{Evidence and reproducibility}
The analytical claims concern the stated ideal models. Exact rational
implementation comparisons use independently formulated physical enumeration
and analytical formulas; agreement is an internal cross-check, not external
replication. The connector/fee qualification contains twenty fixed cases,
and the support-extension qualification fourteen fixed cases, with explicit
boundary and corruption controls. These cases qualify implementations;
the proofs establish the continuous-domain statements. The heterogeneous
adequate-resource specialization matched independent raw SOC enumeration
and threshold formulas on all eight fixed cases; eighteen negative controls
and a smoke anchor passed, including tie and continuous-response checks.
The fixed single-shared-connector extension separately matched raw SOC
enumeration and serial witnesses against the sorted-threshold formulas on all
fourteen prospectively fixed cases, with twenty-four negative controls and a
smoke anchor passing. These include physical collinear-threshold and clipped
lower-boundary optima, assignment ties and response continua. Both are internal
qualifications, not external replication; the eight-case run concerns the
separate $n$-connector resource policy.
Archived outcome and
resource records distinguish exact arithmetic from tolerance-qualified
native computations. This evidence update reads stored qualification results
without rerunning the scientific calculations. The public positive-gap result
uses a source-bound feasible mixture and an independently reconstructed exact
303-node coverage certificate, with 353 classical native proposal calls.
Neither a finite plan bank nor a native solver status alone establishes that
conclusion. Failed strict replays and earlier unsuccessful certification stages
remain archived with their costs. This verification is internal, not external
replication; the public timetable's energy and economic assumptions are synthetic.
