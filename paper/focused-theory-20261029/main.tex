\author{Nathan Cho}

\begin{abstract}
We characterize when a complete electric-bus fleet minimizes its operating
cost and electricity bill at the marginal supply prices associated with
its own load. For heterogeneous services in two simultaneous batches,
a declared successor graph and fixed shared charging resources, we derive
the exact joint load--vehicle-cost frontier with full battery replenishment.
Cardinality-matching thresholds determine where a convexified optimum has
a physical implementation. In this lossless class the load-only set is an
interval; its discrete
vehicle-cost layers cause the obstruction. A bidirectional extension accounts
for gross connector throughput and signed electricity settlement. Vehicle-to-grid
operation can preserve or remove a positive gap, and identical paid idle
buses are redundant under a stated fleet cap. With common conversion losses,
the efficient joint early/terminal load--cost frontier consists of at most
two line segments per matching cardinality, permitting a finite reduction
for coordinatewise nondecreasing supply objectives. Exact examples and parameter
regions distinguish useful flexibility from mere enlargement of the duty set.
The matching and price-support machinery is classical; the contribution is
the exact lossless load--cost classification and common-loss efficient
frontier under these explicit assumptions.
For one public 37-service timetable, an independently checked rational
branching certificate separates every fleet in its declared movement graph from a feasible
convexified plan, proving a conservative positive gap of at least $0.390674$
in the stated objective units. The physical value lies in the outward interval
$[441.532912,441.532913]$; the hull optimum remains unknown.
With a separately defined exact additive consumption law, fresh certificates
and a dominance argument extend the positive gap to finite walks over the
specified paired directed arcs and continuous waiting.
Synthetic coefficients and ideal
physics limit conclusions about operational prevalence or calibrated value.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
An electric-bus operator assigns mandatory services to vehicles and decides
when to recharge them. Continuous charging offers flexibility, but a vehicle
cannot cover a fraction of a service. Assigning two services to the same bus
can therefore impose an intermediate energy requirement that disappears when
two buses are used. A tariff may reward the resulting change in charging
without compensating the operator for its additional vehicle.

We ask a precise question. A supplier has a convex incremental cost for
serving aggregate fleet demand. A tariff is calculated from a candidate
fleet's load and posted before the operator chooses its complete schedule.
Holding that tariff fixed, does the candidate minimize the operator's bill
plus its intrinsic operating cost? This is an own-load price-support test.
Its participant is one operator controlling the complete fleet. It is not a
strategic model in which that operator anticipates a new tariff after each
alternative it considers.

The connection between price support and convexification is established in
nonconvex market theory. We develop its physical implications for a class of
heterogeneous services in two simultaneous batches. Every assignment allowed
by the declared successor graph is retained, with one fixed shared connector
in each charging window.
An exact joint energy--vehicle-cost projection reduces the complete fleet set to
early-energy thresholds indexed by matching cardinality; standard matching
convexity then identifies the hull and a precise support criterion. The
energy-only feasible set is an interval; the relevant nonconvexity concerns
its cost-labelled assignment layers. Full
terminal replenishment is central: an initially full battery must not provide
free net energy over a finite horizon. Every plan buys the same total service
energy, yet its assignment changes \emph{when} that energy must be purchased,
coupled with the fixed cost of a used bus.

Bidirectional charging changes this frontier through both available battery
inventory and connector time. Charging one bus while discharging another
does not cancel the time spent on a single connector: its resource is gross
throughput. We derive the resulting signed-load thresholds and show exactly
how the obstruction can persist or disappear. Under a total fleet cap,
identical paid idle buses add no projected alternatives beyond those obtained
by separating matched service pairs. This statement concerns the declared
ideal model, not the value of spare buses in general V2G operations.

Common conversion losses require keeping both early and terminal grid loads:
net early energy alone hides the replacement energy lost through exports.
We derive a finite efficient-frontier description using the same matching
values, with at most two pieces per cardinality. This preserves physical and
convexified planning values for monotone supply costs, while deliberately
retaining a narrower scope than the full lossless support classification.
The reduction also admits a common compulsory transition-energy task per
matched pair. When that energy varies by edge, a small counterexample shows
why minimum early recharge alone loses an efficient assignment: pairing
can change total replacement energy as well as its timing.
An explicit family then retains a positive support gap despite exponentially
many efficient physical branches at a fixed fleet size. This boundary concerns
physical alternatives, not exponential hull complexity or computational hardness.
A further extension admits a complete assignment graph with every perfect
matching physically feasible and preserves the same strictly positive gap.

The two-batch assumptions make the assignment set exhaustive. A separate
37-service public timetable supplies an exact existence certificate beyond
that class: a feasible complete-plan mixture lies below a checked lower
bound on every physical fleet in its declared movement graph. The energy
and economic coefficients are synthetic, so this establishes a mechanism,
not operational prevalence. Equipment and ownership examples explain why
additional alternatives and aggregate planning accuracy must be interpreted
carefully; their complete analyses are retained in the appendices.

\section{Implications and limits}
The obstruction concerns one posted-tariff institution with a complete-fleet
participant. It is a possibility under characterized conditions, not an
inevitable failure of marginal tariffs, a strategic tariff-reset equilibrium
or a convergence claim. Convex-hull pricing and general support duality are
established theory. The physical contribution is the explicit assignment-
dependent frontier under the stated timing, resource and loss assumptions.

The analytical models omit stochastic delays, degradation, temperature,
taper and switching time. Common windows and efficiencies enable the finite
reduction; arbitrary timetables, vehicle-dependent losses and negative-price
economics require further analysis. The nominal reserve/loss neighborhood
does not validate those abstractions empirically. The public case uses varied
times and movement energy, but remains charge-only within fixed declared
movement modes and charging windows. Its certificate does not prove
completeness for arbitrary retiming or unlisted access. A separately defined
exact additive law admits a wider result: under its explicit triangle
and charging-access premises, a separate certificate covers arbitrary finite
walks in the paired-arc model. Neither law certifies all empirical road options.
Synthetic coefficients and one base timetable leave calibrated magnitude
and prevalence open.

\section{Conclusion}
Service assignments change the joint load--vehicle-cost set even when its
load-only projection is simple. For the stated common-window class,
cardinality-matching values determine an exact lossless support classification
and, with common losses, a finite efficient frontier for monotone planning.
Gross connector use and full replacement energy are essential to that
description. A separately checked public-timetable certificate establishes
the same qualitative obstruction in a richer declared charge-only model.
These results identify when continuous charging flexibility does and does
not remove assignment-induced price-support failure, while leaving empirical
significance and more general scheduling classes open.

\section*{Evidence and reproducibility}
Independent physical enumeration qualifies the finite analytical
implementations; constructive proofs establish their continuum statements.
Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} preserves all case/control denominators and
their scopes. The public result uses classical native proposals followed by
exact physical admission and rational coverage certificates. One fresh
standalone standard-library Python process rechecked the retained complete
proof without a solver in a construction-and-check phase of about 33 seconds.
This is internal author-run verification, not external replication. The
certificate index binds sources, inputs, exact constants and command.
Earlier failures and costs remain archived. The exported package stays local
pending redistribution review; no public release or substantive learning
contribution is claimed.
