\section{An exact positive gap on one public timetable}\label{sec:public}
For the stated charge-only movement-graph model of one public 37-service timetable, an
independently checked physical lower bound strictly exceeds a feasible
complete-plan mixture's hull upper. The outward bounds are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:publicbounds}
 \CH_{15}\le441.142238<441.532912\le D_{15}\le441.532913,
 \qquad D_{15}-\CH_{15}\ge0.390674.
\end{equation}
The gap lower is the conservative difference of the down-rounded physical
lower and up-rounded hull upper shown here; it is not an exact gap value.
The upper on $D_{15}$ rounds upward the qualified exact witness value
$441.532912\ldots$. Neither $D_{15}$ nor $\CH_{15}$ is known exactly.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:support}, no physical fleet is supported at its
own marginal prices under this declared model and tariff convention.
This is an exact sign certificate with a conservative separation; it
is not evidence of a practically large loss or operational prevalence.

\subsection{The declared case and objective}
The case uses the Hildenbrand timetable from the Sistig data
\citep{sistig2025dataset,sistig2025electrification}: 37 mandatory services
and the complete directed deadhead matrix. Depot 15 and depot 16 are
separate one-depot modifications of the same base timetable, not independent
operational samples. The nonlinear sign certificate concerns depot 15.
Source timetable offsets, including next-day services, are retained; the
source data do not supply the energy or economic calibration below.

The opportunity set is the explicitly declared finite movement graph and
its descriptor's fleet cap.
Its pullout, direct service-to-service, via-depot and pullin modes have
fixed leg times and specified depot charging gaps. Services may be assigned
to any compatible complete path in this graph; charging allocations within
the declared gaps remain continuous and share the connector. The complete
directed deadhead matrix supplies movement data, but does not by itself
authorize arbitrary retiming, extra depot visits or unlisted movement modes.
The certificate quantifies over all fleets admitted by this graph. Extending
that claim to a larger continuous-routing opportunity set requires an
additional coverage or dominance argument, not merely the same timetable.

Service consumption is 1.20 kWh/km plus 16 kWh per service hour; directed
deadheads use 0.96 kWh/km plus 16 kWh per travel hour, and explicit off-depot
waiting uses 16 kWh/h. Depot-parked auxiliary draw is zero. Each used bus
has 400 kWh usable inventory, zero reserve, unit efficiency and 360 kW
acceptance. One 360 kW connector is shared. Every used bus starts full
and returns full by a synthetic 30:00 service-block boundary. There is
no taper or switching time. Monetary movement cost is zero, but movement
time and energy remain physical constraints. The used-bus fee is 100.
This finite block is not a certificate of repeating daily operation.
The public model remains charge-only; the preceding V2G classification
is a separate analytical model and was not applied to this timetable.

For its 30 hourly market intervals, the convex supply objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:publicF}
 F(e)=\sum_{t=1}^{30}\left(0.2e_t+\frac{e_t^2}{1800}\right).
\end{equation}
These familiar decimal coefficients denote the original stored binary
values used in every exact certificate.\footnote{The linear coefficient is
$3602879701896397/18014398509481984$ and the quadratic Hessian coefficient is
$5124095576030431/4611686018427387904$; the quadratic term is half the latter
coefficient times $e_t^2$.} The coefficients are synthetic. The result
therefore concerns a specified ideal scheduling and supply-cost instance,
not calibrated market behavior, a strategic pricing equilibrium or an ML
experiment.

\subsection{What establishes the strict separation}
The physical upper is a rational charging allocation on selected routes,
independently replayed against every service, movement, battery bound,
shared connector and full terminal inventory. A separate rational dual
certificate establishes its charging optimum conditional on those routes,
not global fleet optimality.

A different strictly admitted physical response has a higher nonlinear
planning cost but a lower bill at this incumbent's fixed marginal prices.
It therefore supplies a genuine deviation, rather than a raw floating
candidate. An exact two-plan mixture of these admitted plans, checked by
direct quadratic evaluation and one-dimensional optimality conditions,
supplies the hull upper in \eqref{eq:publicbounds}. A mean complete-plan
load is not a physical daily dispatch; the mixture upper is not asserted
to be the global hull optimum.

The missing global step is supplied by a new physical branch proof.
An independently reconstructed minimum-fleet obstruction rules out one bus
within the declared movement graph.
For every fleet with at least three buses, the vehicle fee and only the
linear mandatory-service-energy term already exceed 477.82; omitted
movement consumption and quadratic supply cost are nonnegative. These
facts permit searching the two-bus physical domain while covering every
other fleet count separately. They do not exclude larger-fleet components
from arbitrary hull mixtures or from response problems at other tariffs.

Binary branching retains both values of each selected movement variable.
For every fringe region, exact tangent planes, signed row multipliers and
finite-box residual compensation give a valid lower; an infeasible region
requires an exact positive contradiction. Independent semantic reconstruction
and complete branch coverage make these bounds exhaustive. A bounded
refinement retains the original partition and strengthens its leaves under
their inherited decisions, yielding \eqref{eq:publicbounds}. The outward
physical interval has width at most $10^{-6}$, but equality of the exact
bounds is not claimed. Appendix~\ref{app:certificates} gives the checkable
algebra and Appendix~\ref{app:publichistory} records the search and
qualification stages. This is a global physical lower, not a hull lower;
a native OPTIMAL status, finite plan bank or small solver gap would not
establish it.

\subsection{Stability to the vehicle fee}
Holding physics and the supply objective fixed, every used-bus fee
$f\ge100$ retains the positive gap guarantee in \eqref{eq:publicbounds}.
Both admitted mixture components use two buses, so their fee shift cancels
that of the two-bus lower; a separate nonnegative-fee floor covers larger
fleets. An independently checked exact interval calculation also gives an
open sufficient range below 100. Appendix~\ref{app:publicfee} gives the
derivation and endpoint qualifications. This varies one economic parameter,
not losses, battery capacity or station resources.

\subsection{Interpretation and limitations}
Classical native planning already found an objective near 441.532912;
the contribution of the certificate chain is exact admission and exhaustive
coverage, not a better heuristic objective or an ML advantage. Earlier
failed physical screens and multiplier recovery remain failures in
Table~\ref{tab:publichistory}; later certificates do not relabel them.
The earlier continuous relaxation's valid hull lower $434.17$ remains
distinct from the branched physical lower. Appendix~\ref{app:publichistory}
also separates flat-tariff comparisons, internal qualifications and the
new portable retained-evidence check. The physical value is tightly
enclosed, but the hull optimum and exact gap remain unknown.
One timetable with synthetic energy and economic assumptions cannot
estimate prevalence, operating savings or welfare effects in bus systems.
The result extends the analytical possibility to this fully specified
varied-time charge-only instance; it does not validate the simplified
common-window model empirically or establish a learning contribution.
