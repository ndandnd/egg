\section{Relation to existing work}
\paragraph{Operational coupling.}
Grid-aware scheduling, capacitated charging and scalable route-and-recharge
models already combine bus duties with charging access
\citep{wu2022grid,devos2024capacity,parmentier2023fleets}; integrated flow
and set-partitioning models also choose assignments and charging slots
\citep{borndorfer2024integrated}. \citet{wu2022grid} include endogenous
vehicle-to-trip assignment and full overnight charging. Integer bus-charging
aggregation/disaggregation around auction bids is studied by
\citet{zoltowskaLin2021}, while \citet{wu2019gridinteractive} couple service
planning to market clearing and distribution marginal prices in a bilevel
model. Thus assignment, full charging and endogenous electricity prices are
not sufficient novelty claims. We instead ask whether a fixed posted
marginal tariff supports a complete fleet over alternatives that change
both used-bus costs and duty-created charging opportunities.

Transport and V2G are also established: \citet[Sections 2.1--2.2]{blatiak2022v2g}
optimize commercial-fleet trip timing and charging against energy and
balancing prices. The author manuscript of \citet[Section 2]{zhou2025v2g}
chooses bus use, connections and partial charging/discharging under input
purchase/export tariffs, including vehicle fees, deadheading, charging,
export revenue and degradation, with full-depot inventory/overnight
recharging. These materially overlap
our physics; the manuscript's imperfectly extracted mathematical glyphs
preclude detailed claims about its connector or terminal constraints.
Our distinction is an exact cost-labelled threshold and own-load support
classification, not richer V2G operations, first transport--V2G coupling
or first use of spare buses for grid services.

\paragraph{Joint loads and vehicle cost.}
\citet{mukhiAbate2023exact} characterize fixed-population charging polytopes
with prescribed availability and full departure charge using permutahedra.
\citet{mukhiLohoAbate2025exact} give generalized-polymatroid representations
and exact Minkowski aggregation for convex individual flexibility sets.
\citet[Section II, Example 1]{altaha2024flexibility} likewise aggregate
lossless bidirectional batteries with initial/final requirements.
Our load-only projection is an interval, not a new nonconvex flexibility
set. Theorem~\ref{thm:sharedprojection} instead retains every attainable
load--vehicle-cost layer as successor matching and fleet size change;
Corollary~\ref{cor:sharedsupport} tests hull-optimum physical attainment.
Fixed-matching interval aggregation and ordered matching remain classical.
The V2G thresholds and capped paid-idle elimination concern this restricted
class with shared gross throughput; persistence/restoration examples do
not establish a first signed-aggregation or V2G support result.

\paragraph{Common losses.}
\citet[Theorems 1--3]{altahaBitar2024lossy} establish a piecewise-affine
grid-power/SOC bijection, convex single-store SOC feasibility and sufficient
monotonicity conditions for convex reformulation. We do not claim these
transformations or conditions as new. Theorem~\ref{thm:lossyfrontier}
combines service-dependent vehicle counts, successor choices and shared
gross connector use in an efficient joint early-load, terminal-load and
vehicle-cost frontier with at most two segments per cardinality. Common
windows and monotone economics are essential to discarding dominated
points while preserving planning values.

\paragraph{Support, lost opportunity cost and ownership.}
Nonconvex pricing and uplift are established
\citep{oneill2005,gribikHoganPope2007}; convex-hull pricing supplies the
convexification and lost-opportunity-cost framework
\citep{schiro2016,huaBaldick2017}. Complete linear descriptions address
support with integralities \citep{huembs2022complete}, and whole-participant
schedule columns with fixed-price self-scheduling oracles have established
decomposition treatments \citep{andrianesis2022hull}. Our support equivalence
and complete-fleet unit apply these tools, not new pricing duality or
column-generation machinery. Classical aggregation
\citep{starr1969,hreinsson2021} explains small aggregate nonconvexities;
our replication calculation distinguishes the planning gap, a composite
owner's absolute fixed-price incentive and independently reserved pairs'
incentives. Its rates do not contradict approximate aggregation results.

The contribution is a physical classification under explicit assumptions,
not a richer operational model or faster general scheduling algorithm.
Matching integrality, value-function convexity and price-support duality
remain the underlying tools. Capacity, fee and equipment examples explain
the classification; the public certificate establishes existence within
its declared movement graph, without extending the closed-form theorem
to arbitrary timetables.
