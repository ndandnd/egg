# Distribution pricing and indivisible bus schedules

Independent source check, 2 November 2026. The question is whether a depot price can both clear a radial feeder and support a physically operable fleet schedule, and whether iterative price discovery reaches such a point. We checked the six search families in the brief, chased citations in both directions and compared the original texts where available. This is a targeted adversarial search, not an exhaustive database census. Access labels distinguish examination of relevant original sections in a complete paper from an abstract or a surrogate. No full-text copies are added here.

## What changes the interpretation

Shaojun Huang, Qiuwei Wu, Shmuel Oren, Ruoyang Li & Zhaoxi Liu, “Distribution Locational Marginal Pricing Through Quadratic Programming for Congestion Management in Distribution Networks,” IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 30(4), 2170–2178 (2015), DOI 10.1109/TPWRS.2014.2359977. Full author text, introductory counterargument and KKT derivation. A strictly convex aggregator QP for divisible EV/heat-pump demand resolves ambiguity in load selection. This is a substantive control when diagnosing fleet ties, but regularizing genuine schedule costs changes the economic question. [Full text and DOI](https://oren.ieor.berkeley.edu/pubs/DLMP.pdf)

Zhaoxi Liu, Qiuwei Wu, Shmuel Oren, Shaojun Huang, Ruoyang Li & Lin Cheng, “Distribution Locational Marginal Pricing for Optimal Electric Vehicle Charging Through Chance Constrained Mixed-Integer Programming,” IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid 9(2), 644–654 (2018), DOI 10.1109/TSG.2016.2559579. Full author text, §§III–IV, especially (17)–(25). Binary indicators select which driving-pattern scenarios can violate a probabilistic requirement. The DSO uses DC OPF with distribution constraints. Forecast-and-post aggregator scheduling differs from a full-market welfare certificate for indivisible timetabled duties. [Full text](https://oren.ieor.berkeley.edu/pubs/126.pdf)

**Nearby fleet and grid models.** Yetkin, Augustino, Lamadrid & Snyder, “Co-optimizing the smart grid and electric public transit bus system,” Optimization and Engineering 25, 2425–2472 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s11081-023-09878-w. Full text §§2–4, 6 and Appendix B. A social planner assigns off-duty vehicles to stations, charges/discharges them, and dispatches DC-grid generation. Mixed-integer and stochastic formulations explicitly use LMPs. Procedure 2 computes calibration prices excluding added transit demand in that step. The paper investigates pricing and fleet cooperation without a global convergence claim for our price update. [Publisher text](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11081-023-09878-w)

Lingming Kong, Hongcai Zhang & Ningyi Dai, “Spatial and Temporal Optimal Scheduling of Electric Buses for Active Distribution Network with Soft Open Point,” EVS34 (2021), author preprint posted 2025, DOI 10.36227/techrxiv.174320017.79124533/v1. Network voltage, bus mobility and power exchange are already present. A centralized solve demonstrates neither recurrent posted DLMPs nor a full-fleet support-gap sign. [Author paper](https://doi.org/10.36227/techrxiv.174320017.79124533/v1)

Rui Yao, Xuhang Liu, Anna Scaglione, Shlomo Bekhor & Kenan Zhang, “Integrated equilibrium model for electrified logistics and power systems” (2025), arXiv:2505.04532, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2505.04532. Full author text, §§II–IV and appendix. One logistics operator selects incentives that induce truck movements and charging. Action flows solve a perturbed-utility problem, the PSO uses DC OPF, and a fixed point exists under their regularity conditions. Anderson acceleration in the Hawaii study is a numerical method. The continuous action flows and reward-response construction differ from covering every scheduled service with individual buses. A single fleet operator responding to grid prices is precedented. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04532)

## Dynamic coordination and closest coupled models

Access records dated 2 November 2026. “Full text” means the paper itself was inspected from an open author or publisher copy, or from a user-supplied local copy, including the relevant model, proof or numerical section. The paper-specific claims below use the cited versions. This search cannot establish priority by exclusion.

**Alizadeh, M., H.-T. Wai, M. Chowdhury, A. Goldsmith, A. Scaglione and T. Javidi (2017).** “Optimal Pricing to Manage Electric Vehicles in Coupled Power and Transportation Networks.” *IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems* 4(4), 863–875. Full journal text on an author's site. Individual drivers choose energy-feasible paths with charging arcs. The social planner assigns continuous path flow and dispatches generation with DC shift-factor line limits. Road and station tolls matter to the traffic response. Section V-A posts a price for the preceding charging pattern and then optimizes traffic at that price. Section VI, Table II gives alternating even/odd charging, traffic and prices in a numerical case with 22,600 vehicles per epoch. At Winters, for example, charging alternates between 7.227 and 15.87 MWh. Separately, Prop. V.1 and (22)–(26) treat convex social-cost minimization via partial dual decomposition on balance and transmission limits. The authors invoke small enough steps for convergence of that residual-based multiplier iteration. Intermediate generation and flow may violate coupling. The argument gives no guarantee for their greedy update. Since their aggregate operating set is convex, a price cycle in a system with an efficient supporting price already follows from their model. The specific physical-versus-hull gap for a joint integer bus operator is a different observable. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCNS.2016.2590259) · [full journal paper, pp. 869–873](https://web.ece.ucsb.edu/Faculty/selected_pubs/Alizadeh-mp/1.pdf)

**Wei, W., L. Wu, J. Wang and S. Mei (2018, online 2017).** “Network Equilibrium of Coupled Transportation and Power Distribution Systems.” *IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid* 9(6), 6764–6779. **Full journal article, available in the user-supplied repository.** Equation (39) defines the two-network equilibrium. Algorithm 2 updates a continuous mixed-fleet traffic assignment at posted prices and then uses marginal prices from a radial branch-flow SOCP. Equations (33)–(34) prohibit reverse flows. The paper invokes exactness under its own feasible network assumptions. The DLMP is the multiplier of the active nodal balance. In §IV-A, Proposition 3 proves existence on a compact convex link-flow polytope by Brouwer, conditional on a continuous traffic-equilibrium response (Assumption 2), OPF feasibility for every represented charging load and LMP continuity (Assumption 3). Eq. (44) is a stated local stability condition at the equilibrium involving a positive own-station price slope and the product of that slope, absolute own-station flow sensitivity and energy per EV below one. The text offers a contractivity explanation. It does not bound a full multi-station Jacobian. Eq. (45) and the ensuing uniqueness statement are explicitly conjectural. §IV-B makes convergence local and initialization-dependent and explicitly allows failure despite an existing equilibrium. It suggests damping when the product is near one. The test in §V-B starts at zero-charging OPF prices and for 50 kWh per EV reaches the stopping tolerance of 0.5 after four iterations from a residual of 700.26. Figure 9 describes monotone decline. Table VIII generally sees extra iterations as energy per EV grows. No observed period-two case is reported in these experiments. The conditional existence theorem and experiment do not cover an indivisible single operator, discontinuous price/response, or V2G reverse flows. Local read-only copy: `ref/papers/Network_Equilibrium_of_Coupled_Transportation_and_Power_Distribution_Systems.pdf`, printed pp. 6773–77. [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2017.2723016) · [Stevens record](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/network-equilibrium-of-coupled-transportation-and-power-distribut/)

**Author's subsequent account.** W. Wei, D. Wu, Q. Wu, M. Shafie-khah and J. P. S. Catalão (2019), “Interdependence between transportation system and power distribution system: a comprehensive review on models and applications,” *Journal of Modern Power Systems and Clean Energy* 7, 433–448. Full open article. Section 2.3.2 explicitly attributes the price→traffic→charging→OPF fixed-point framework to their 2018 ref. 19. Equation (28) and steps 1–4 stop when consecutive LMPs are close. The authors describe convergence as likely when equilibrium exists and is stable, and describe the 2018 paper as discussing existence and stability. **Xie, S., Y. Xu and X. Zheng (2022).** “On Dynamic Network Equilibrium of a Coupled Power and Transportation Network,” *IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid* 13(2), 1398–1411. Full author manuscript. Dynamic continuous path departure/route/charging flows with point queues and multi-period conic distribution OPF/DLMP. Definition 3 and (54) use a composite self-map. Theorem 2 invokes Brouwer on compact convex domains with continuous maps. In Lemma 2 the authors use local price continuity over a small load range and claim unique prices from strong duality. Strong duality alone does not establish dual uniqueness, so this cannot be borrowed at a kink. Algorithm 2 adds extrapolation and projected traffic-flow updates. Remark 1 expressly restricts strong-convergence claims in the absence of monotonicity and presents numerical convergence. It would be wrong to label these results a general proof of the original Wei best response, of an integer response, or of diminishing price steps. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2021.3130384) · [author-posted full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356511792_On_Dynamic_Network_Equilibrium_of_a_Coupled_Power_and_Transportation_Network)

**Roozbehani, M., M. A. Dahleh and S. K. Mitter (2012).** “Volatility of Power Grids Under Real-Time Pricing,” *IEEE Transactions on Power Systems* 27(4), 1926–1940. Full author preprint. Aggregate supply, price-responsive demand, information asymmetry and successive retail/wholesale prices. Assumption I imposes regular producer cost/consumer value. Corollary 3, (57) gives a sufficient stability condition using maximal generalized relative price elasticity below one for some exponent. Amplification/instability of the market-demand feedback is possible. Their dynamics evolve in market periods and concern prices, demand and uncertainty. Observing an offline sequence of trial DLMPs oscillate would not itself establish real-time physical grid or voltage instability. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRS.2012.2195037) · [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1401)

**Li, R., Q. Wu and S. S. Oren (2014).** “Distribution Locational Marginal Pricing for Optimal Electric Vehicle Charging Management,” *IEEE Transactions on Power Systems* 29(1), 203–211. Full journal text. Model (1)–(9) uses DC OPF/PTDF limits, elastic household demand and charging aggregated by location with divisible energy and cumulative SOC bounds. The DSO computes welfare-optimal day-ahead prices. Local rational price-taking optimization reproduces the central decision through KKT conditions. The paper illustrates a congested RBTS distribution case with Danish driving data. The result concerns implementation at a clearing price under their information and aggregator model. It does not give a global attraction basin for the naive fleet response. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRS.2013.2278952) · [full journal paper](https://oren.ieor.berkeley.edu/pubs/new115.pdf)

**Gong, X., A. De Paola, D. Angeli and G. Strbac (2019).** “Distributed Coordination of Flexible Loads Using Locational Marginal Prices,” *IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems* 6(3), 1097–1110. Full institutional postprint. One of the closest overlooked references. Devices have divisible fixed-energy tasks over availability windows and bounded rates. An approximate linearized network OPF retains active/reactive and voltage constraints and polygonal line ratings. Equations (3)–(4) use one-sided marginal optimized generation costs at kinks. The iterative scheme shifts an admissible amount between time slots sequentially and recomputes prices, allowing multiple update choices. Theorem 1 gives asymptotic set convergence to variational Wardrop equilibria under their model and feasibility assumptions. Theorem 2 identifies global optimality when the optimized cost is differentiable at that equilibrium. Neither the one-sided price diagnostic nor using an OPF-derived set-valued signal is new. Whole-duty replacement can be discontinuous even at a smooth unique OPF price, and their divisible swaps need not be feasible for buses. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCNS.2019.2920587) · [open article manuscript](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1179777)

**Gan, L., U. Topcu and S. H. Low (2013).** “Optimal Decentralized Protocol for Electric Vehicle Charging,” *IEEE Transactions on Power Systems* 28(2), 940–951. Full author journal manuscript (plural “Protocols” in the preprint). Continuous rate-limited and fixed-energy EVs with charging windows. Aggregate strictly convex valley-filling cost and no OPF. Algorithm A1, (8), adds a squared penalty for changing one's previous schedule. Under nonempty feasibility, Lipschitz derivative with constant beta and 0 < gamma < 1/(N beta), Thms. 3–4 give convergence to the optimal profile set and convergence of aggregate load/price. Thm. 5 treats delayed/asynchronous updates with bounded delays/frequent updates and adjusted step. Individual profiles can be nonunique. These guarantees concern a specifically regularized continuous algorithm. ## Feeder physics, reference systems and data

Read on 2 November 2026. “Full text” means that a legal complete copy was opened and the indicated technical sections were checked. It does not mean every appendix was independently reproved. These sources concern continuous network variables except for the topology search in Baran–Wu. None proves convergence of the proposed integer-fleet price loop.

**Masoud Farivar and Steven H. Low (2013), “Branch Flow Model: Relaxations and Convexification,” Parts I and II.** IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 28(3), 2554–2564 and 2565–2572. DOIs 10.1109/TPWRS.2013.2255317 and 10.1109/TPWRS.2013.2255318. Full text, both parts. Part I §§III–V, Theorems 1, 2 and 4, separates eliminating phase angles from relaxing the branch-current equality to a cone. A tree resolves angle consistency. Exact conic recovery uses objective monotonicity and the ability to increase loads without upper bounds in their proof. A fixed background load or fleet with bounded hours and SOC does not supply that freedom. Part II concerns phase-shifter convexification of meshes outside a spanning tree. It gives no price-response convergence result. [Both papers, authors’ copy](https://smart.caltech.edu/papers/relaxconvex2parts.pdf)

**Lingwen Gan, Na Li, Ufuk Topcu and Steven H. Low (2015), “Exact Convex Relaxation of Optimal Power Flow in Radial Networks.”** IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 60(1), 72–87, DOI 10.1109/TAC.2014.2332712. Full text, §§II–IV. Equations (1a–d) are the AC branch equations in their child-to-parent orientation. Theorem 1 assumes strictly increasing substation objective, componentwise upper injection bounds, path-product positivity C1, and C2, requiring every SOCP optimal injection to lie in the voltage-safe set. C1 is computable from parameters. C2 is generally solution-dependent. Section IV imposes an additional linear injection restriction (11) so C2 holds by construction; this changes the feasible set. The result is about this network formulation. New current limits, taps, shunts, storage and nonsmooth/negative prices require explicit applicability analysis. If the upstream price is negative on part of the feasible range, extra losses can improve the relaxed objective. Do not floor observed prices or append a penalty and then claim exactness for the original model. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.7170)

**Steven H. Low (2014), “Convex Relaxation of Optimal Power Flow,” I. Formulations and Equivalence, II. Exactness.** IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems 1(1), 15–27 and 1(2), 177–189. DOIs 10.1109/TCNS.2014.2309732 and 10.1109/TCNS.2014.2323634. Full texts. Part I §§IV–VI relates bus and branch variables, rank-one and cycle consistency, and SDP, chordal and SOCP relaxations on trees. Part II §III and Table I distinguishes sufficient conditions on power injections, voltage bounds and phase differences. Relaxations can agree with each other yet have a common nonzero gap to AC. Uniqueness of a primal optimum does not alone identify a unique vector of prices; redundant constraints can also have nonunique multipliers with an unchanged economic price. [Part I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0766) and [Part II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0814)

**Javad Lavaei and Steven H. Low (2012), “Zero Duality Gap in Optimal Power Flow Problem.”** IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 27(1), 92–107, DOI 10.1109/TPWRS.2011.2160974. Full text. Theorems 1–2 relate an SDP dual, rank-one recovery and a sufficient nullspace condition. The numerical transmission-network tests and transformer perturbations illustrate the hypotheses. The “gap” here concerns continuous nonconvex AC physics. A gap due to discrete bus duties is a separate mathematical object. [Authors’ paper](https://smart.caltech.edu/papers/zeroduality.pdf)

**Zhao Yuan, Mohammad Reza Hesamzadeh and Darryl R. Biggar (2018), “Distribution Locational Marginal Pricing by Convexified ACOPF and Hierarchical Dispatch.”** IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid 9(4), 3133–3142, DOI 10.1109/TSG.2016.2627139. Full text, §§II–IV. Three network levels transmit generalized convex bid/cost curves. SOC and envelope relaxations represent continuous AC dispatch. Theorems 1–2 concern tightening the specific loss constraint (2). They assume a convex objective, a unique AC global solution and a condition on inexact relaxed losses. Theorem 2 assumes convergence of a decreasing bound to the correct loss. The authors say the further angle/voltage relaxations need not tighten. They report agreement to within 1% of centralized cost after three iterations on the tested modified 342-node system. No general best-response theorem for fleet scheduling follows. [KTH manuscript](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1082030/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

**Daniel K. Molzahn and Ian A. Hiskens (2019), “A Survey of Relaxations and Approximations of the Power Flow Equations.”** Foundations and Trends in Electric Energy Systems 4(1–2), 1–221, DOI 10.1561/3100000012. Full monograph available; consulted the formulations and relevant portions on relaxations and §5.2.3. DC discards resistance, reactive power and voltage variation and uses small angle differences. This removes mechanisms that set a marginal depot price on an MV feeder. A local nonlinear solver produces a candidate feasible upper bound only after an AC constraint and objective check. Comparison with a relaxation lower bound quantifies the remaining uncertainty. [Authors’ monograph](https://molzahn.github.io/pubs/molzahn_hiskens-fnt2019.pdf)

**Mostafa Nick, Rachid Cherkaoui, Jean-Yves Le Boudec and Mario Paolone (2018), “An Exact Convex Formulation of the Optimal Power Flow in Radial Distribution Networks Including Transverse Components.”** IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 63(3), 682–697, DOI 10.1109/TAC.2017.2722100. Full manuscript consulted on model and theorem. Models radial AC with line shunts, terminal currents and voltage ceilings. Their augmented, conservative constraints yield a sufficient exactness result under their stated conditions. Line series-current magnitude need not equal either terminal current in a cable with charging. For SimBench, carry charging and transformer model into both SOCP and AC comparator, or identify the simplified feeder and separately benchmark its approximation to the native one.
