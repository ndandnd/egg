# Should EGG add a distribution OPF?

**Decision, 2 November 2026. Proceed to feeder and price qualification.** A matched test can still answer a question for a transit operator. If a quoted feeder price keeps sending buses between charging plans, would *any* efficient price support a whole-fleet dispatch? Would committing to a conventional duty cover first change the answer? An oscillogram alone cannot distinguish the remedies. Failure of a price update might call for a better clearing algorithm. Failure of support makes changing only that update insufficient for implementing the efficient dispatch.

## The test worth doing

Hold the same compulsory trips, fleet/terminal obligations, depot equipment, feeder, price day and welfare objective for all methods. Compare a separately acquired and paid fixed-cover plan with joint whole-fleet optimization and the convex hull of *complete* permitted fleet schedules. Evaluate their physical costs, voltage and ampacity, the certified welfare interval, and the best-response residual at the implemented price. Quantify what an operator stands to lose by honoring the dispatch versus changing duties. The planner gap measures a different quantity from that operator regret at a specified price. Grid model uncertainty, optimizer tolerances and alternative optima must be small enough to separate the proposed effects. Include an LP/convex fleet relaxation, centralized physical baseline and conventional coordination. Label randomized mixtures as counterfactual across days and never claim their average voltage is an operated day.

**An analytic negative control.** For one slot with a smooth strictly convex feeder cost V, suppose three feasible menu entries have loads 0, 1 and 2. Pick strict thresholds V'(0) < a < V'(1) < b < V'(2). Set private costs c(2)=0, c(1)=a and c(0)=a+b. At V'(0) the operator strictly chooses 2. At V'(2) it strictly chooses 0. Undamped prices alternate. Yet at V'(1) it strictly chooses 1. The supporting tangent to V and the three private-cost inequalities certify that 1 minimizes even the convexified objective. The gap is exactly zero, with unique prices and responses on the cycle. Positive resistance alone provides a power-flow realization of the convex cost: on a unity-voltage two-bus resistive feeder with positive constant purchase price and loss parameter r, high-voltage import is (1−sqrt(1−4rL))/(2r), with r small enough to supply the whole interval. This is a finite-menu mechanism check, not a constructed empirical bus case. It rules out assigning all zero-gap cycles to a tie or kink.

**Evidence that would change the paper.** Find prospectively matched cases where the certification changes the inferred remedy relative to fixed-cover dispatch and classical price coordination, with absolute operational stakes exceeding numerical and AC model error. If the gap cannot be separated from solver/relaxation uncertainty, report unresolved. If price cycles persist with valid supporting prices but effects on duties, welfare and operations are negligible, report that the grid extension adds realism and a negative result. No harder-case shopping. This review found a defensible question and no priority guarantee. It supplies no new ML contribution on its own.
