# Learned route proposals: focused literature and experiment design

Prepared 28 September 2026. This is a bounded primary-source review and proposal, not an implementation or EGG efficacy claim.

## Current evidence and prior work

The reuse-frontier run covers three synthetic fixtures and is explicitly incomplete: 44/45 comparison cells certified, with one failed cell preserved. In matched cells, retaining columns and retaining plus shifted-price proposals used the same clean-pricing-call counts; shifted proposals yielded three projection-novel columns without observed clean-call reduction. This is motivation for a better proposal test, not evidence for or against ML. See the [frozen summary](../../result/reuse_frontier/20260927-attempt1/summary.json) and [independent review](../../result/reuse_frontier/20260927-attempt1/review/REVIEW.md).

The literature distinguishes learned column selection from learned pricing/route proposals. Shen et al. sample graph-coloring columns guided by predicted pricing solutions; Chi et al. learn selection among already-generated CG columns; Abouelrous et al. (2025 preprint) directly construct VRPTW pricing routes. MIP warm-start work predicts partial assignments and uses an optimizer to complete or repair them; neural routing work similarly learns route-repair operators. The repo's manuscript already cites Sugishita et al.'s learned dual warm starts. These works establish method families, not validation on complete electric-bus fleets with SOC, shared charging, and terminal replenishment.

## Six primary references and what they establish

1. Shen, Sun, Li, Eberhard & Ernst (2022), [ML pricing heuristic for graph coloring, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/21230): a predicted pricing solution guides sampling of multiple columns inside CG/branch-and-price. Candidate generation is demonstrated; certificate replacement is not.
2. Chi, Aboussalah, Khalil, Wang & Sherkat-Masoumi (2022), [deep RL for column generation, NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/3ecfe5c632afb7d96a2337b18ff99b1f-Abstract.html): learns sequential selection among existing columns on cutting-stock and VRPTW; it is a useful selection ablation, distinct from route generation.
3. Abouelrous, Bliek, Gabor, Wu & Zhang (2025), [RL pricing for vehicle routing, arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02383): an attention policy constructs VRPTW pricing routes and reports faster relaxations with a nonzero objective gap versus a DP heuristic. It is a close route-pricing analogue, but not bus/SOC/shared-charger validation; failure to find a negative reduced-cost route is not proof none exists.
4. Nair et al. (2020), [neural diving for MIPs, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13349): predicts partial integer assignments and solves residual MIPs with SCIP; separate learned branching handles bounds. Predictions are starting guidance, not accepted feasible/optimal solutions.
5. Huang, Ferber, Zharmagambetov, Tian & Dilkina (2024), [contrastive predict-and-search, ICML/PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/huang24f.html): predicts/fixes some integer variables and solves the reduced MILP. This gives a concrete predict-then-repair baseline, without full-model optimality certification.
6. Hottung & Tierney (2022), [neural large-neighborhood search for routing, Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2022.103786): learns a repair operator in LNS, evaluated on CVRP, split-delivery VRP, and team orienteering. It supports learned route repair, not bus-duty feasibility guarantees.

## One controlled experiment

**Question and current-model unit.** In the existing EGG hull, a column is a *complete feasible fleet plan*: its replayed witness produces operating cost and the fleet's full 30-hour hourly-load vector. The restricted master mixes these plan points against convex supply cost; its pricing oracle takes hourly grid prices and returns a best-response full-fleet plan. There are no per-service cover rows or route-level columns. Test whether learned complete-plan proposals or retrieval reduce work to a fixed valid enclosure on held-out base timetables. Inputs may include timetable/time windows, leg energy, charger/terminal structure, and hourly prices; outputs must be a complete fleet-plan witness under the current case identity, or a partial prediction sent to repair.

**Arms and gates.** Run identical frozen cases, solver, cap, initialization, and certificate loop in: (A) cold iterative exact pricing with no prior plan pool; (B) retained complete-fleet plans from the previous tariff on the same physical case; (C) nearest-neighbor retrieval from training timetables by declared timetable/energy/price features, reconstructed under the current case; and (D) a learned complete-plan proposal. Any imported/proposed witness must be rebuilt or tagged for the exact current case and replayed before entering the full-plan pool. Replay checks all services, times, SOC, charge windows/power/connectors, shared charger limits, terminal replenishment, and hourly loads. For D, separate raw complete-plan prediction from predict-then-repair, where a fixed-budget fleet sub-MIP converts a partial prediction into a complete feasible plan. Optionally compare learned selection from a common plan pool.

**Split and evaluation.** Split by source/base timetable or operating day, keeping all perturbations, prices, route fragments, and repeat scenarios from one source in one partition. Fit model/retrieval and normalization on train, tune on dev, freeze before unseen test timetables; include a prespecified shift in energy or charger/terminal conditions. Report all frozen cases, including infeasibility, no feasible prediction, repair failure, timeout, and certificate failure. Measure first feasible fleet time, replay/repair rates, coverage before/after repair, duplicates and nonnegative-value proposals, calls/time to the same predeclared certified enclosure, final valid lower/upper bounds, and anytime gap curves under a common cap. Account separately for offline training/indexing, online inference/retrieval, replay, repair, and global pricing/certification; show online and amortized totals over a fixed repeat count. Compare at equal enclosure quality, not just equal pricing-call counts.

**Certification rule and scope boundary.** In the current hull, ML is only a full-plan proposal/seed mechanism; its score or failure to find another plan is not a lower bound. Continue exact complete-fleet pricing at current hourly prices and stop only when the oracle's global bound closes the fixed tolerance. A different, optional research direction would reformulate the problem as route-level Dantzig–Wolfe: the master would need service-cover and shared charger-hour constraints, and pricing/certification would need to account for their duals and the full SOC/terminal state. That is an architecture redesign, not the current baseline. If gains disappear after whole-plan repair and exact pricing, that is a useful negative result; until the full gates pass, make no fleet-scale or certificate-acceleration claim.
