## Computational chapter ready for review — 28 September 2026

The computational chapter is now readable as a six-page LaTeX document with four tables and two vector figures. An independent numerical review and visual inspection of all six pages are complete. This is a finished component of the expanded paper; the full first draft still needs the theory, physical and economic model, and literature integrated.

The chapter brings together the completed feasible-pool pilot (24 cells), ordered solver-baseline comparison (32 cells), and fixed-price starting-fleet pilot (16 calls). These campaigns overlap in cases and source evidence. The two public depots share one Hildenbrand timetable, so neither the depot variants nor the combined cell count are independent public replications.

The positive finding is that reusing feasible fleets and previously obtained pricing bounds helps particular cases. In the two completed public cache runs, inherited evidence raises the lower bound by 98.82 and 69.83 compared with each run's fresh-pricing lower bound. Their final numerical hull intervals remain open at [405.83, 471.52] and [420.45, 491.46], with about 352 seconds of paid source-plus-target work each. These bounds retain native-solver tolerance qualifications.

The figures place interval quality beside paid computation and show every public starting-fleet comparison. Failed targets retain missing intervals and their spent time. Starting-fleet effects are mixed under equal 160-second public pricing caps, so the chapter claims neither public optimality nor an equal-quality speedup. Its evidence map separates supported findings from questions still open, including independent-network performance and learned proposals.

No new optimization was run to prepare this chapter. The failed six-case retrieval attempt supplies no algorithm result, and the proposed single replacement remains pending; no replacement was launched. Manuscript integration can continue using the reviewed evidence while that decision is open.
