# Prospective charge-response source selection, 30 September 2026

This is one bounded developmental batch. It asks whether a small CPU ridge model can choose which **already acquired, replayed source fleet** should receive a single target-tariff charging LP. It does not train a route generator or claim a global fleet optimum. The fixed-source charging pilot found that source ranking can change after charging: among the four earlier development groups, the lowest direct target bill was source0 throughout, but the better recharged fleet was source1 in 2016 and 2018. All eight earlier fixed-route LPs returned feasible plans. Those groups are motivation only and are excluded from fitting and from this batch's evaluation.

## Frozen design and split

The stage-2 deterministic generator and its three market definitions remain unchanged. New independent timetable seeds 2022–2027 form six training groups: two each at 12, 20, and 28 services. Seeds 2028–2031 form four development groups: two at 20 and two at 28 services. Source0, source1, and target variants of a timetable always have the same `base_group`. Seeds 2004, 2005, 2020, and 2021 remain reserved and unmaterialized. No case, market, label, or feature from those seeds is accessed. The old stage-2 `EdgePrior` is frozen by its model, frozen-design, and training-receipt hashes; it is a separate comparator, not the model being fit here.

The exclusive output directory is `result/learning_campaign/20260930-charge-response-attempt1`. Freeze records the execution commit, source hashes, old-model input hashes, runtime/backend identity, every generated case and market identity, split, full cell order, and budgets. A single controller lock, append-only catalog, per-child launch/receipt, and no-overwrite output files allow a receipted run to resume. An unreceipted interrupted cell is preserved and blocks automatic retry. No second attempt or changed profile is authorized by this protocol.

There are 44 serial cells. First, all ten groups acquire source0 and source1 through independent native hull solves (20 cells). Next, the six training groups receive a fixed-source charging LP for each source fleet (12 cells). **Before any development target LP or cold solve**, the controller writes all four development groups' cheapest-direct, nearest-price, and old frozen-EdgePrior source choices, then makes one ≤60-second training/inference call. That call fits the new model only to independently replayed feasible training LP outcomes and writes all four model choices and predictions. Finally, the four development groups receive both source-specific target charging LPs (eight cells) and a cold target native solve (four cells). The second dev LP is paid for evaluation; a deployable one-LP chooser pays only for the LP it selected before either target LP result was known. The post hoc best-of-two LP is an evaluation oracle that pays for both LPs, never a free online baseline.

## Model and labels

The fixed feature vector for each source fleet uses only its pre-target physical replay and declared target/source markets: direct exact curved target bill per trip, operations cost per trip, target linear-price load bill per trip, energy load per trip, source-to-target price distance, source identity, and timetable size, plus an intercept. The target is `(replayed post-LP exact curved cost − direct exact curved cost) / trip count`. Training-only means and standard deviations standardize nonconstant features; an intercept is unpenalized. Ridge penalty is fixed at 1.0 with no tuning, cross-validation, or dev-label refit. **All 12 charged labels from all six independent training groups must replay** before a model is fit. Training coverage is written even when incomplete; an incomplete or failed fit receives a durable failure receipt. Independent development controls continue, and fitting is never retried after any development outcome. Six independent training pairs are exploratory data, not evidence of a generally calibrated model.

The charging subproblem keeps the source fleet's **exact movement set** fixed and solves its charging schedule under the **linear** target `market.a` objective (GRB, one thread, 45-second phase and 55-second wall cap). The returned plan must pass independent native replay and fixed-route identity checks. Its label is the *exact curved target cost returned by this pinned linear-tariff procedure*. It is neither the minimum curved cost for that route topology nor a globally optimal fleet cost. Distinct degenerate linear optima may have different curved costs. No quadratic charging optimization or route repair is added here. Each source/cold native solve uses the existing stage-2 bounded hull policy; any independently replayed incumbent and verified lower bound is retained separately. Nonclosing bounds, partial feasible plans, solver failures, and paid elapsed time remain visible. A replayed plan from a failed or timed-out child is provisional, not certified.

## Comparisons and endpoint

For every development group, the prospectively frozen one-LP choices are: cheapest **direct exact curved bill** among the two source plans, nearest source tariff by squared distance, old frozen `EdgePrior` projected topology (minimum symmetric-difference, then score, nearest-price distance, and row ID as tie keys), and new charge-response ridge predicted post-LP bill. The projected topology and each source's distance/score are saved with the choice. A missing source or failed learner is recorded, not silently replaced. Both source LPs are run to obtain unbiased paired evaluation labels. Report each chooser's replayed post-LP curved cost, feasibility, LP paid time, and **excess curved cost over the paid best-of-two source LPs** when both plans replay. This is candidate-pool excess, not global regret. Report source acquisition paid time once per timetable, offline fit and inference separately, and cold native feasible cost/bounds/time. The cold solve is a contextual global-search control; it is not used to select or train the source chooser. Do not claim speedup from historical controls or ignore source acquisition and training amortization.

The falsifiable result is whether the ridge choice improves paired development excess cost and paid online one-LP time against the simple pre-target choices, especially cheapest-direct. Failure to improve means this batch supports the cheap charging control, not a learned-selection advantage. Four development groups cannot establish broad transfer. No held-out test group is opened in this attempt.

## Resource and execution controls

One serial Slurm job requests one CPU, 8 GB, one native/BLAS thread, and 100 minutes, excluding `scaglione-compute-01`, with no requeue. Each of 44 children has a 100-second hard cap, giving 4,400 seconds maximum child time; the one training/inference call has a 60-second cap. Controller cap is 5,400 seconds and wrapper cap is 5,700 seconds. The job writes an outer receipt even on failure. There are no hidden retries or additional optimization calls. Local tests are pure or mocked; no local GRB/full-fleet solves are part of preparation.
