# Tariff-response transfer results

The frozen ridge selector responded to the new tariffs, but the saved choices did not improve paired cost relative to the simple controls. In each of four timetables, ridge selected source 1 for the `late` and `day` tariffs and source 0 for `flat`; nearest-price made the reverse choices. Across the 12 correlated tariff variants, ridge's mean paired excess was **0.534212 cost units**, versus **0.066973** for the frozen EdgePrior, **0.153972** for nearest-price, **0.260736** for cheapest-direct/source 0, and **0.427448** for always-source-1/training-majority. This is evidence of price-responsive switching in these cases, not an ML advantage.

The analysis independently recomputes each selected curved-cost excess from the exact `Fraction` objective fields in the frozen catalog. All 84 method-by-tariff outcomes, 28 groupwise means, and 28 switch indicators match the saved summary. The 24 charging labels reference the expected source-plan hashes and have matching source/charged movement sets. This checks route preservation from the saved labels; it is not a new physical feasibility replay.

The best-source entries show which archived fleet had the lower exact cost after its target charging LP; `S0=S1` indicates a tie. Group means average the three tariffs within each timetable. The paired benchmark is only the better of the two paid fixed-route LP outputs, not a globally optimal fleet or minimum curved-cost route. The LPs optimize the linear tariff component; the reported curved cost is a post-LP rescore.

All **44/44 catalog cells** are marked returned and feasible, with no missing artifacts or child timeouts: 8 source acquisitions, 24 fixed-route charging LPs, and 12 cold target hulls. The fixed-route LPs reported `OPTIMAL` for their linear objectives. Cold target hull status was `certified` for two cells and `budget_exhausted` for ten; those labels do not establish global physical optimality. Every cold cell retained a feasible plan. Reserved test groups remained unmaterialized.

All 12 ridge choices and their two source candidates were frozen before target outcomes. Baseline-choice preparation took 5.696 seconds and model inference/validation took 9.329 seconds. Source acquisition was shared once per timetable group and took 44.416, 130.501, 60.450, and 129.877 seconds for seeds 2032–2035. Each fixed-route LP child took 3.790–5.046 seconds; the paid two-LP comparisons took 7.627–9.890 seconds. Cold hull calls took 21.325–65.846 seconds. Slurm elapsed time was 1,104 seconds on `snavely-cpu-01`, with one CPU, 8 GB requested memory, and 285,268 KiB maximum RSS.

There are four independent synthetic timetables; their three tariffs are paired variants, not twelve independent cases. Costs are in uncalibrated cost units. All passed. This supplies a fresh feasibility/cost check, not a new global-optimality certificate.
