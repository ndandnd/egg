# TRAIN pool 128: coverage and remaining breadth

This is a read-only coverage summary of the frozen 128-group TRAIN pool. It reads only `cases.jsonl`, `source_inputs.jsonl`, and the current physical-learning direction/protocol notes; it does not inspect target-outcome tables, raw traces, development/test data, or run native replay or fitting. Machine-readable values and file hashes are in [TRAIN128_COVERAGE_NOTE.json](TRAIN128_COVERAGE_NOTE.json).

The 128 distinct base timetables cover the complete 54-cell factorial of battery profile × service count × drive-consumption rate × minimum depot-gap threshold. Each cell appears two or three times (34 cells twice; 20 cells three times). Duty-start stride is balanced marginally at 64 cases per value, but every primary cell uses only one stride: just 54 of the possible 108 five-factor cells occur. Stride therefore remains aliased with the primary design; 20-service cases use stride 25/30 in counts 43/22, while 28-service cases use 21/42. Other marginals are 43/43/42 groups across the 80, 174.24, and 232.32-kWh operating spans; 65 20-service and 63 28-service cases; 43/43/42 cases at 1.20/1.45/1.70 kWh/km; and 44/43/41 cases at 0/15/30-minute minimum depot gap. There are 128 unique case identities, 255 observed source-plan inputs, 127 two-source groups, and one single-source group (base 10037).

The pool therefore has real controlled physical breadth, but it remains repeated sampling within one narrow synthetic generator, not 128 varied real-world systems. The modeled operating spans are 80, 174.24, and 232.32 kWh, with nameplate/upper/reserve values 100/100/20, 290.4/232.32/58.08, and 290.4/261.36/29.04 kWh. These are profile assumptions; the two Elec City-scaled SOC windows are not manufacturer-calibrated usable-capacity distributions.

Charging structure remains fixed: every case has one shared connector, 90-kW grid and per-bus limits, 0.9 charging efficiency, and the same terminal-open/deadline window (minutes 1080–1800). The movement graphs contain generated depot alternatives (108–334 per case; median 158) as well as direct alternatives; these are candidate arcs, not a count of independently usable charge sessions. In the 255 source plans, 162 use at least one positive charge stop on a `depot_` movement (0–13 such stops per plan; median 2). That describes archived plan usage only, not a fresh feasibility or availability test.

Two justified gaps for a later version are: (1) cross the 25/30-minute duty-start stride within each primary factorial cell, so its effect is separable; (2) broaden physical realism by varying charging topology/power and timetable/route geometry, and validate usable-capacity/SOC assumptions rather than treating the nameplate-scaled spans as field-calibrated profiles. The current fixed 36-km/h timing/energy rule, three scalar consumption levels, two service counts, one depot/resource, and single terminal window delimit the claims this training bank can support.

No outcome quality, model benefit, or physical-optimality conclusion is made here.
