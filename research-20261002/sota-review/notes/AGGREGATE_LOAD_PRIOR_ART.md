# Aggregate-load outer models: bounded primary-literature check

2 October 2026. Five primary papers; mathematics and source inspection only. No cluster, solves, evaluation data, modelcode, or sealed DEVTESTA6B3GIRO access. This note does not establish EGG-specific physical assumptions or performance.

## Inspected sources and guarantee direction

1. **Suhail Barot and Josh A. Taylor**, *A concise, approximate representation of a collection of loads described by polytopes*, International Journal of Electrical Power & Energy Systems **84** (2017), 55–63, [DOI 10.1016/j.ijepes.2016.05.001](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0142061515302489). Inspected the [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.0939), Section III, Proposition 1 and its proof, and Section IV-B, Propositions 2–3. For individual sets \(P_i=\{u:A u\le b_i\}\), the set \(\{z:A z\le\sum_i b_i\}\) contains their Minkowski sum: adding valid inequalities proves **outer** containment. Different normals can be reconciled through redundant support inequalities. Exactness is proved for hypercubes and the stated simplex-like deferrable loads; these special cases do not establish general battery exactness. Publisher metadata and preview were available through indexed primary text; direct publisher opening failed. The supporting appendix is available on request and was not inspected.

2. **Lin Zhao, He Hao, and Wei Zhang**, *Extracting Flexibility of Heterogeneous Deferrable Loads via Polytopic Projection Approximation*, [arXiv:1609.05966 (2016), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.1609.05966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05966). Inspected the [full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.05966), Section II, Theorem 1/Remark 1, and Sections III–IV, including Theorem 2. Individual charging windows, rates, and energy bounds are prescribed. Theorem 1 gives an **exact** characterization using inequalities over subsets of vehicles and times, with exponential enumeration in general. Their tractable projected homothet/battery is a **sufficient**, hence inner, approximation, accompanied by individual scheduling policies. It is suitable for guaranteed dispatch, but its containment direction cannot justify an unrestricted oracle lower bound. Bibliographic identification here deliberately uses the verified preprint DOI; conference-version equivalence was not checked.

3. **Feras Al Taha, Tyrone Vincent, and Eilyan Bitar**, *A Multi-Battery Model for the Aggregate Flexibility of Heterogeneous Electric Vehicles*, American Control Conference (2023), 1243–1250, [DOI 10.23919/ACC55779.2023.10156328](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10156328/). Inspected the [author-hosted paper](https://bitar.engineering.cornell.edu/papers/ACC23.pdf) and [HTML preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.06769v1), Sections II-B, IV, and V-A. Section II-B explicitly states that adding RHS vectors of the common virtual-battery matrix \(H=(L,-L,I,-I)\) produces an **outer** approximation. The proposed multi-battery model instead enforces **inner** containment; Proposition 2 gives sufficient decomposable containment conditions. Experiments prescribe workplace arrival/departure windows. This directly establishes prior art for aggregate power and cumulative-energy envelopes, while illustrating that “virtual battery” alone says nothing about guarantee direction. IEEE indexed publication metadata was available; technical inspection used the accessible full author/preprint texts.

4. **Karan Mukhi, Georg Loho, and Alessandro Abate**, *Exact Characterization of Aggregate Flexibility via Generalized Polymatroids*, [arXiv:2503.23458v2, revised 17 June 2025, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2503.23458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23458). Inspected [Sections II–IV and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23458v2). Definition 1 specifies fixed individual power and prefix-energy bounds with additive storage dynamics. Theorem 2 identifies these sets as generalized polymatroids; Theorem 3 gives their **exact** aggregate by adding the associated supermodular/submodular set functions. Section III-C covers heterogeneous, prescribed EV arrival/departure times. These are subset-of-time descriptions, not merely a summed prefix envelope. Section IV develops optimization without explicit facet enumeration. This strong recent precedent narrows novelty claims about exact fixed-device aggregation; it does not make endogenous routing, shared charger access, or integer vehicle schedules separable. Only the identified preprint version is claimed here, not a verified journal version.

5. **Federico Rossi, Ramon Iglesias, Mahnoosh Alizadeh, and Marco Pavone**, *On the interaction between Autonomous Mobility-on-Demand systems and the power network: models and coordination algorithms*, [arXiv:1709.04906v4 (8 June 2019), DOI 10.48550/arXiv.1709.04906](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04906). Inspected the [full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.04906), Section II-A and the fractional-output discussion on manuscript p. 14. The time/location/charge network models travel-energy consumption, charging/discharging edges, customer and rebalancing flows, and station capacity (Equation 4). Charging availability is determined jointly with transport flows, rather than supplied as independent EV windows. The authors explicitly note fractional optimization output and sampling for individual control. This is relevant coupling prior art, not an exact aggregate polytope for integer schedules. Relaxing a faithfully embedded integer model to fractional flows would give an outer set; that containment requires a separate proof for EGG's conventions. Technical claims use the inspected preprint; final journal-version equivalence was not checked.

## Why summed virtual-battery facets can be insufficient

The following is an original illustrative counterexample, not a reported experiment. Use four unit-duration slots and charging rate at most one per vehicle. A needs one energy unit and is connected in slots 1–4. B needs one unit and is connected only in slots 2–3. Each individual's tight single-slot power lower bound is zero. Summing their tight power and prefix-energy bounds gives aggregate power upper bounds \((1,2,2,1)\), prefix lower bounds \((0,0,1,2)\), and prefix upper bounds \((1,2,2,2)\). The aggregate profile \((1,0,0,1)\) satisfies all these bounds, yet supplies nothing during B's connection window. The non-prefix constraint \(z_2+z_3\ge1\) excludes it. An outer certificate remains valid despite such fictitious profiles, but may be weak. This version was corrected in independent review so that implied individual power lower bounds do not already reject the profile.

## Endogenous fleet and useful conditional facets

For EGG, fixed-window exactness cannot be imported: routes determine traction, timing, location, and charging access, while shared sites couple vehicles. A route-conditioned set need not contain loads from other routes. An outer model must cover every unrestricted schedule and admissible fleet size.

Conditional standard resource reasoning is promising. If \(N\) vehicles are chosen, mandatory simultaneous service requires at least \(m(t)\) vehicles, service vehicles cannot charge, and each remaining vehicle draws at most \(P\), then
\[
0\le z(t)\le P[N-m(t)],\qquad z(t)\le C(t),
\]
where \(C(t)\) is a proven installed-site power bound. Integrating gives interval energy upper bounds. With full terminal replenishment, battery-side mandatory withdrawal \(W_{\min}\), and efficiency at most \(\eta_{\max}\), necessary grid energy satisfies \(\int z\,dt\ge W_{\min}/\eta_{\max}\). Combining yields a necessary charging-time/fleet-count inequality. Retain \(N\) or project over its entire admissible range; substituting an incumbent fleet count can invalidate containment. No inspected paper proves these EGG-specific bounds. Initial/terminal energy, export, regeneration, losses, and changing membership require explicit accounting.

## Recommendation and novelty boundary

Use a proven outer polytope with total/prefix/selected-interval energy and physical power/access facets as a candidate certificate component. Energy conservation, resource counting, RHS summation, and virtual batteries are established mathematics and prior art. The plausible contribution is an inexpensive **valid** relaxation for endogenous fleet operations combined with reusable complete-oracle certificates, with a measured gain in certified accuracy per total effort. Inner dispatch models cannot substitute for this requirement. Tightness, construction cost, and value across many prices remain untested; no first-virtual-battery or first-aggregation claim is supported.
