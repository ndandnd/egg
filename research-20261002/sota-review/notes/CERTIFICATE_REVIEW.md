2026-10-02. Reviewed `ECONOMIC_CERTIFICATES_AND_NOVELTY.md` by algebra and assumptions only; no solves, cluster work, or source-file edits.

**Verdict:** every displayed inequality has the correct direction under the stated finite-value assumptions. I found no invalid restricted-oracle lower bound or novelty assertion in the derivations. Four qualifications should be made explicit in the implementation/report.

1. **Domain qualification.** Replace “for every admissible finite price” with “for every price with finite `V(p)` and `F*(p)`.” At a schedule's marginal price also require `F(e(x)) < infinity` and `p in subgradient F(e(x))`. Proper closed convex `F` need not have a subgradient at every feasible boundary point. A pricing-feasible schedule is a column, but supplies a finite physical-objective upper bound only if its load lies in `dom F`. Specify whether the institution's response set includes supply feasibility; use that same `X` throughout.
2. **Stopping consistency.** Require certified `L <= U_CH` before accepting `U_CH-L <= epsilon`. A negative reported gap is a contradiction/error diagnostic, not successful termination. Native solver `ObjBound`, replayed load, approximate master weights, and floating conjugate calculations are tolerance-qualified until their uncertainty is enclosed. “Unpruned” alone does not turn floating output into a rigorous lower bound; its formulation must itself be a valid original-model relaxation with matching costs, units, and outer-approximation direction.
3. **Conjugate direction.** The note correctly subtracts an *upper* enclosure of `F*`. Maximizing `p·e-F(e)` numerically produces an incumbent **lower** bound on the conjugate, which cannot be substituted for that upper enclosure. An exact formula, verified optimization upper bound, or rigorous enclosure is needed. Retain this warning near the algorithm, because an apparently good conjugate incumbent can otherwise raise `L` invalidly.
4. **Cache compatibility.** Cache reuse requires unchanged feasible set and intrinsic objective, including energy-coordinate convention, efficiency, discretization, and embedded tariff terms. If tariffs enter `c`, the suggested tariff reuse fails. Hardware caps must cover *all* unrestricted schedules, not just retained routes or incumbent bus counts. Convert charger power to interval energy when `e_t` is energy; allow signed bounds if export is permitted. Supply changes require the new conjugate as stated.

Algebra checks: continuous linear `c+p·e` is bounded below by `V(p)` on the generating set and hence its closed convex hull; Fenchel inequality then gives `q <= CH <= D`. Restriction raises the pricing infimum, so its incumbent is an upper bound and its solver lower bound is unusable globally. A valid finite mixture yields an upper hull value without master optimality. The regret interval follows from `ell <= V <= v`; at a genuine subgradient, Fenchel equality gives `r=J-q >= J-CH`. Consequently `D_L>CH_U` excludes zero regret for every supply-compatible schedule at every available marginal price. This is neither an arbitrary-price result nor a strategic-price-impact result.

For cached prices, `c(x)+p_k·e(x)>=ell_k` and `(p-p_k)·e(x)>=inf_E (p-p_k)·z` prove the bound directly; taking the maximum preserves validity. The box formula's `min(0, price difference)` sign is correct.
