# Final contribution challenge

2 October 2026. Bounded conceptual review of the main report and the existing OR positioning note only. No protocols, bibliography, data, models or experiment artifacts were audited; no experiments or new source searches were performed. This note challenges the decision logic rather than repeating the prior novelty critique.

The report generally separates established mathematical facts, project-reported observations and prospective hypotheses well. In particular, it labels E11 interim, distinguishes restricted candidates from global certificates, and does not promise theorem transfer or journal acceptance. The remaining weakness is that its ordered engineering program can look like an evidence-backed path to the proposed unified contribution when the evidence currently supports several competing explanations.

## 1. Test whether learning is necessary before optimizing its recovery

E3/E5 demonstrate project-reported improvements over particular solver pipelines, while the report itself identifies incumbent loss, poor cold initialization and round allocation as possible explanations. Rank 1 appropriately repairs the common infrastructure. But rank 2's recovery comparison could then establish the best way to use a fixed learned score without establishing that learning earns its acquisition and integration costs. Simple widening of the same learned ranking is a recovery baseline, not a nonlearned challenge to the ranking.

**Action:** make the post-repair continuation decision conditional on a matched inexpensive nonlearned candidate policy: for example the closest applicable greedy arc/depot/charging-access rule, with the same fallback, round policy, construction and replay accounting. Historical LP/random comparisons remain evidence, but do not automatically survive the changed pipeline. If that policy matches learned performance, retain recovery as engineering and stop treating improved neural labels as the next research priority. Whether this happens cannot be inferred from the existing summaries.

## 2. Rank allocation versus oracle strengthening conditionally

The proposed allocation work is cheaper to engineer than route pricing, which explains its earlier rank. Yet E9's evidence concerns improved feasible pools; it does not isolate whether unresolved enclosures are dominated by poor primal columns, weak pricing lower bounds or outer price selection. If the lower bound barely moves even at useful queries, redistributing candidate effort cannot close that bottleneck. Conversely, an intrinsically loose route relaxation may be cheap without helping the desired certificate.

**Action:** explicitly describe ranks 3 and 5 as a conditional branch after a bounded bottleneck diagnosis, rather than an unconditional queue. Separate changes in feasible-mixture upper bounds, unrestricted pricing lower bounds and query quality. Advance allocation when both candidate and certification effort have demonstrated useful marginal returns; advance oracle strengthening when certification dominates. This decision needs measured trajectories and cannot be settled by the report's prospective CPU totals.

## 3. Demand a decisive economic endpoint for the unified paper

The unifying insight—primal search and economic certification require different information—is correct but already familiar from decomposition. Faster hull narrowing alone does not show that the fleet's support status becomes decidable, especially where all large-instance planning-gap intervals still contain zero. Nor does a profitable deviation from a particular candidate establish that every optimal fleet lacks supporting prices. A unified title risks combining two successes without demonstrating the economic bridge.

**Action:** define the candidate contribution around earlier attainment of a specified decision-relevant enclosure, and state which conclusion it licenses: planning non-support, bounded own-price regret, or merely unresolved uncertainty. Choose tolerances in physical monetary units and relate them to numerical margins before outcomes. If no meaningful economic conclusion becomes available at feasible cost, pursue a focused operational scheduling paper rather than using certification as its distinguishing headline. The present evidence cannot establish that such conclusions are obtainable.

## 4. Separate advancement screens from contribution gates

The 10%, 20% and 25% screens are transparent proposed choices, but passing them on exploratory cases is not evidence of a fleet-specific OR contribution. The main report already acknowledges this; its recommended order should make the consequence operational. Label-pool gains or a more reliable known recovery method can be useful without supplying the required new mechanism.

**Action:** retain reliability as the mandatory foundation, then require a contribution gate before broader spending: identify the precise coupled-charging mechanism or structural certificate improvement, the closest algorithmic comparator, and a falsifying outcome. Stronger alternative: pursue one bottleneck-led branch at a time, allowing a nonlearned price-support method or a purely primal method to win. This is a sharper research strategy than treating all five drafted pilots as a cumulative route to one paper.
