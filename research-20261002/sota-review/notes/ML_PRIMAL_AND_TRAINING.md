# ML primal heuristics and training signals for EGG

Research date: 2026-10-02. Research only: no training, solver experiments, cluster actions, sealed-data reads, commits, or pushes. Scope: Section 5 of `doc/GPT_HANDOFF_20261002.md`, its result summary, and the permitted assessment `doc/CLAUDE_ASSESSMENT_20261001.md`. The assessment predates E3–E11; the later handoff supersedes its claims about missing matched-time evidence. All EGG results below are exploratory development evidence, not confirmation evidence.

## Recommendation for integration

The best immediate extension is **recoverable predict-and-search around the existing movement scores**, followed by **cost-weighted pools of replay-valid complete fleet solutions**. Test simple incumbent-centered LNS and online neighborhood selection before paying for a learned destroy policy. This ordering follows EGG's already working scorer and solver, rather than conference recency. No reviewed paper demonstrates the best method for EGG's particular 300–10,000 movement binaries and approximately 300,000 continuous charging variables.

The relevant EGG evidence is unusually informative: E3/E5 show that deleting movement options can improve complete-fleet time-to-quality; E4 shows tariff-diverse labels can improve economic outcomes; E7/E8 show that gains on synthetic cases and more aggressive pruning can reverse on public schedules. E10's replay failures mean invalid plans cannot be credited as objective improvements. v8's AP improvement is a ranking result, not a bill improvement. These facts motivate solver correction, richer labels, and explicit tests of public-schedule tails.

### ML1 — Neural Diving: foundation with real mixed-variable evidence

Vinod Nair, Sergey Bartunov, Felix Gimeno, Ingrid von Glehn, Pawel Lichocki, Ivan Lobov, Brendan O'Donoghue, Nicolas Sonnerat, Christian Tjandraatmadja, Pengming Wang, Ravichandra Addanki, Tharindi Hapuarachchi, Thomas Keck, James Keeling, Pushmeet Kohli, Ira Ktena, Yujia Li, Oriol Vinyals, Yori Zwols. **Solving Mixed Integer Programs Using Neural Networks**. Verified as arXiv:2012.13349 (first 2020; inspected v3, 2021); no peer-reviewed venue verified. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.2012.13349](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.13349). [Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13349), [full text, §§6.1–6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.13349v3).

Learns objective-weighted distributions from deduplicated feasible incumbents, samples partial integer assignments, and completes them with a solver. Continuous decisions are optimized conditional on integers. Selective prediction learns assignment coverage. Six datasets include production and electric-grid problems; large mixed-variable examples make this relevant. Reported large headline gap gains also involve Neural Branching and should not be attributed to diving alone. Models are trained per dataset; energy weighting is sensitive to objective scale, and independent marginal predictions can combine incompatible solutions. EGG mapping: several movement proposals plus charging optimization, using full fleet cost as the label quality.

### ML2 — Predict-and-search: highest-priority solver-side adaptation

Qingyu Han, Linxin Yang, Qian Chen, Xiang Zhou, Dong Zhang, Akang Wang, Ruoyu Sun, Xiaodong Luo. **A GNN-Guided Predict-and-Search Framework for Mixed-Integer Linear Programming**. ICLR 2023. Conference DOI unavailable; verified preprint DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2302.05636](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.05636). [Primary metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05636), [full paper, §§3–5 and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05636), [conference PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=pHMpgT5xWaE) (OpenReview retrieval challenged; arXiv inspected).

Uses cost-weighted solution pools to predict binary marginals, then restricts disagreement with selected predicted zeros/ones using an L1/Hamming trust region. Four datasets, 100 test instances each, 1,000-second cutoff; Table 1 reports mean gap reductions of 51.1% versus SCIP and 9.9% versus Gurobi 9.5.2. Gurobi gains vary: IP −9.5%, WA 6.7%, IS 0%, CA 43.2%. Neural Diving comparisons use a tuned reimplementation with acknowledged limitations. EGG mapping: replace irrevocable deletion with a limited escape budget for low-score movements; retain charging optimization. This is a primal heuristic, not unrestricted-model certification.

### ML3 — ConPaS: economic negatives; final paper inspected

Taoan Huang, Aaron M Ferber, Arman Zharmagambetov, Yuandong Tian, Bistra Dilkina. **Contrastive Predict-and-Search for Mixed Integer Linear Programs**. ICML 2024, PMLR 235:19757–19771. DOI unavailable. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/huang24f.html), [final PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/huang24f/huang24f.pdf); retrieved copy: `tmp/conpas-final.pdf`.

**Full final text inspected**, §§4–5, Appendices C–D; supersedes the earlier workshop-only reading. Uses up to 50 positives collected under one-hour Gurobi budgets, ten negatives per positive, quality-weighted InfoNCE, and PaS search. Mixed-variable negatives evaluate continuous completion; worst-topology generation is a heuristic maximin procedure. Four main families, 100 test instances each, 1,000-second cutoff; only IP has continuous variables (33). The 9.82–86.02% gap-reduction range concerns the best SCIP-based baseline. The added Gurobi 10 appendix tests MVC/MIS/CA and favors the method on graph families; region parameters differ across methods. Final additions include 50%-larger-instance tests, loss ablations, and an explicit warning that accuracy/AUROC need not predict downstream search performance. EGG mapping: cost-aware topology labels after charging optimization; large continuous-block and public-transfer effectiveness remains unverified.

### ML4 — Learned LNS: preserve an incumbent and leave charging free

Nicolas Sonnerat, Pengming Wang, Ira Ktena, Sergey Bartunov, Vinod Nair. **Learning a Large Neighborhood Search Algorithm for Mixed Integer Programs**. arXiv:2107.10201 (first 2021; inspected current PDF); peer-reviewed venue not verified. DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2107.10201](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2107.10201). [Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10201), [full text, §§3–5, Appendix A/C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.10201), [author code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/neural_lns).

Neural Diving supplies an initial solution; imitation of a local-branching expert learns which integer variables to unfix. All continuous variables remain free at every step. Five datasets include mixed production and electric-grid instances; reported 2–37.8× final gap advantages on three datasets are at long runtimes. Random neighborhood selection is already strong in some families. Expert collection uses up to three hours per step and ten steps per training instance; training can take 48 GPU-hours per dataset. EGG mapping: improve an accepted fleet while refitting charging. The expensive expert makes a faithful reproduction a poor first pilot.

### ML5 — CL-LNS: contrastive learning of neighborhoods, not edge imitation

Taoan Huang, Aaron M Ferber, Yuandong Tian, Bistra Dilkina, Benoit Steiner. **Searching Large Neighborhoods for Integer Linear Programs with Contrastive Learning**. ICML 2023, PMLR 202:13869–13890. DOI unavailable. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/huang23g.html), [full text, §§4–5 and Table 1/2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/huang23g/huang23g.pdf).

Learns variable-unfixing subsets from local branching, with multiple improving actions as positives and poorly improving perturbed subsets as negatives. Tests four binary ILP families, small-to-large transfer, 100 test instances per family, and 60-minute anytime metrics. Table 1 spans 1,000–12,000 variables; comparisons and ablations favor CL-LNS across several metrics but not every cell. Training data require costly neighborhood solves. EGG mapping: learn which trip/route exchanges improve the full bill after obtaining a valid seed. Its largely pure-binary evidence does not establish performance for EGG's large continuous block; neighborhood scores differ semantically from current edge-use scores.

### ML6 — Balans: newer, low-label-cost adaptation against shift

Junyang Cai, Serdar Kadioğlu, Bistra Dilkina. **Balans: Multi-Armed Bandits-based Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search for Mixed-Integer Programming Problems**. IJCAI 2025, pp. 2566–2574. DOI [10.24963/ijcai.2025/286](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2025/286). [Proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/286), [full text, §§3–4](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0286.pdf).

Selects among destroy/repair neighborhoods using an online bandit, with no offline training. Evaluates distributional and hard MIPLIB subsets using SCIP 9 and a Gurobi 11 ablation, single-thread solves, 20-second initialization, minute-scale neighborhoods, and a one-hour budget. The Gurobi ablation improves D-MIPLIB, but explicitly says Gurobi performs much better than LNS methods on H-MIPLIB. Neighborhood sequencing can outperform committing to the best standalone operator. EGG mapping: online selection among movement expansion, route exchange, LP-disagreement, and random neighborhoods. This addresses adaptation cost, not an OOD guarantee, and its long-horizon results may not survive EGG's 60-second budgets.

### ML7 — BTBS-LNS: correction mechanism useful; binarization lower priority

Hao Yuan, Wenli Ouyang, Changwen Zhang, Yong Sun, Liming Gong, Junchi Yan. **BTBS-LNS: Binarized-Tightening, Branch and Search on Learning LNS Policies for MIP**. ICLR 2025. DOI unavailable. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7eca17ef54789b0663cab421f2e9dbf5-Abstract-Conference.html), [full text, §§3–4, Tables 4/7/8](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/7eca17ef54789b0663cab421f2e9dbf5-Paper-Conference.pdf).

Binary encoding/bound tightening supports general integer variables; a tripartite attention graph and a branching policy release wrongly fixed variables. Tests synthetic families, mixed benchmarks, and MIPLIB. Table 4 is worse than Gurobi on Item and Anonymous MIPLIB aggregate gaps despite outperforming SCIP/LNS; other MIPLIB results favor the method at a 300-second cutoff. Includes LP reduced costs and dual features. EGG mapping: borrow correction of bad fixing and solver-state features. General-integer binarization adds little when the key movement decisions are already binary; the full learned architecture is a larger integration than an explicit recovery rule.

### ML8 — TLNS: verified 2026 publication, with preprint full-text evidence

Wenbo Liu, Akang Wang, Wenguo Yang, Qingjiang Shi. **Mixed-Integer Linear Optimization via Learning-Based Two-Layer Large Neighborhood Search**. LION 2025 proceedings, LNCS 15744, pp. 215–236, published 2026-01-02. DOI [10.1007/978-3-032-09156-7_15](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-09156-7_15). [Publisher metadata](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-09156-7_15), [full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08206) (2024); final chapter behind access control.

Solves LNS subproblems by another LNS layer, using a lightweight graph transformer and contrastive training. The preprint trains on smaller instances and tests ten large instances per family; large binary instances have 16,000–100,000 variables. Table 4's up-to-96.1% gain is a primal-integral result, not a certified-gap reduction; final objective gains are smaller. The implementation uses SCIP presolve, noting Gurobi's presolve operator is inaccessible for its workflow. EGG mapping: relevant only if neighborhood MILPs themselves dominate cost; expensive continuous reoptimization could persist across both layers. Do not prioritize a direct port before one-layer timing diagnostics.

### ML9 — RoME: explicit cross-domain evidence; first borrow training discipline

Tianle Pu, Zijie Geng, Haoyang Liu, Shixuan Liu, Jie Wang, Li Zeng, Chao Chen, Changjun Fan. **RoME: Domain-Robust Mixture-of-Experts for MILP Solution Prediction across Domains**. NeurIPS 2025, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 38. DOI [10.52202/085713-1131](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-1131). [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3071ed272658c4418309962ba7b94ec8-Abstract-Conference.html), [full text, §4, Appendices A/C/D](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/3071ed272658c4418309962ba7b94ec8-Paper-Conference.pdf).

Combines expert routing with group-robust training and task-embedding perturbations; trains on three families and evaluates five, with curated zero-shot MIPLIB tests. WA includes 1,000 binaries and 60,000 continuous variables: a closer mixed-variable precedent than pure-binary studies. Uses Gurobi 11.0.3; PaS/ConPaS region settings differ across methods. MIPLIB is curated rather than exhaustive; Appendix Table 8 contains losses/ties despite broad aggregate claims. EGG mapping: domain-balanced training across tariff/topology/scale and worst-group validation are lower-cost first steps than a new MoE architecture. No calibrated coverage or safety guarantee follows from robust training.

## What transfers to EGG, and what does not

The structural analogy is strong: movement binaries define topology; the solver determines physically feasible continuous charging and tariff response. A solver-backed partial assignment or neighborhood can exploit predictions without asking the network to produce a complete charging schedule. The **performance** analogy is weaker. EGG's time-indexed continuous block, charging availability, nonlinear-cost outer approximation, and exact replay create costs and failure modes absent from most binary benchmarks.

In particular, leaving continuous variables free preserves useful economic flexibility but may leave almost the whole expensive LP/QP work. ML4's Appendix C does include a maximum post-presolve continuous count of 335,783 in Production Planning, so large continuous models are not entirely outside the literature. That is a maximum size statistic, not evidence of matched ratio/structure or successful 60-second electric-fleet repair. Measure model construction, presolve, first relaxation, node processing, charging/tangent rounds, and replay separately before choosing a more complex learner. Compare the presolved model's continuous count and nonzeros, not just the movement count. A full variable-constraint GNN is a substantially larger representation than the present 18k-parameter movement graph; transferring the search idea does not require transferring the graph representation.

Let E be candidate movements, p_e the existing score, S0 selected predicted-zero edges, and S1 selected predicted-one edges. The proposed EGG adaptation is

`sum(e in S0) x_e + sum(e in S1) (1 - x_e) <= Delta`.

If S1 is empty and S0 contains all excluded edges, Delta=0 reproduces hard exclusion. Delta>0 admits some excluded edges. Unlike physical deletion, retaining them can preserve model size and cost more in presolve. This tradeoff is the principal reason to test both a full-model trust region and staged reinsertion into a physically compact model. Use predictions of ones cautiously: a high marginal edge score is not a jointly consistent fleet. Warm starts should come from replay-valid complete fleets; a vector of individually plausible movements is not automatically a valid MIP start.

A neighborhood around a valid incumbent is different from a neighborhood around raw predictions: the former contains a known feasible topology, provided charging/tangent/replay settings are compatible. Keep the accepted incumbent outside the neighborhood solver so a timeout or failed replay cannot discard it. For fleet-count changes, destroy coherent route segments or several linked trips, including source/sink/depot arcs and charging opportunities; merely freeing isolated edges can lock fleet count or suppress useful midday returns.

Primal improvement can supply complete-fleet columns or seeds to hull calculations. **It cannot certify a global pricing lower bound.** Bounds from a pruned/fixed/neighborhood pricing MIP apply to the restricted search, and must not be used as unrestricted Fenchel bounds. An eventual certification stage needs the unrestricted oracle or independently valid bounds.

## Training signal proposal (our adaptation, not a published EGG result)

1. **Pools before a new model.** Store several deduplicated route topologies per TRAIN timetable/tariff, with optimized charging, exact replay status, bus count, and complete-fleet objective. Deduplicate on the integer topology rather than continuous charging jitter. Preserve several high-quality modes: labels from alternative fleets should not become arbitrary “wrong edges.” A solver solution pool is a biased search sample, not an unbiased probability distribution over all feasible fleets.
2. **Cost weights with controlled scale.** Test instance-normalized weights `w_s ∝ exp(-(J_s-J_best)/(tau * scale_i))`, with stable log-sum-exp and a validation-chosen temperature; include a uniform-pool ablation. This is an EGG modification, not an exact reproduction of ML1/ML2. Use the same physical bill accounting for all labels, not incomparable tangent-objective values from different rounds. Record energy purchase timing separately so cheap-period opportunities can be diagnosed.
3. **Hard economic negatives.** Construct route-feasible alternatives that differ in depot-return/charging opportunities, optimize their charging, and contrast plans with meaningfully different costs. Infeasible topology and feasible but expensive topology need distinct tags. A native incumbent that fails roundoff projection is not automatically a physically infeasible negative: repair or quarantine it. Avoid investing in a general maximin negative generator first; bounded solver-repaired alternatives are enough for a pilot.
4. **Solver information as an ablation.** Add root-LP movement values, reduced costs, and summaries of binding charging-capacity/SOC constraints only after timing their collection. Compare with an LP-only ranking/search arm. A reduced cost for the current relaxation/cuts is neither a safe edge-deletion certificate nor a universal measure of final physical cost. Dual values in degenerate charging LPs can be unstable.
5. **One self-improvement cycle, with external exploration.** Freeze a scorer; generate TRAIN proposals with cold/LP/random/learned arms; collect accepted solutions; retrain once; evaluate on a timetable-group split fixed in advance. Pool-only relabeling of learned-pruned solutions risks self-confirming omissions. Keep unrestricted or expanding proposals in the collection mix and retain earlier diverse labels. This is a proposed EGG protocol; no claim is made that the reviewed sources establish its convergence or improvement.

For all these changes, measure log loss/AP as diagnostics and choose configurations by replay-valid complete-fleet cost and time-to-quality. Split by timetable group before tariff expansion: otherwise the same topology can enter training and validation under different tariffs. Do not tune on sealed DEV/TEST/A6/B3/GIRO. Public evaluation already used exploratorily cannot be represented as untouched confirmation; call it a transfer stress test and freeze choices before running it.

## Defer / do not pursue first

- **Full Neural Diving/learned-LNS reproduction:** data-generation cost, full MILP graphs, and new training machinery outweigh the cheap solver-side pilot. Reuse the current movement model first.
- **A new MoE architecture because RoME is newer:** first test tariff/topology group balance, worst-group selection, and recovery; EGG's supervised data are small and public shift is not identical to its benchmark domain shift.
- **TLNS before profiling one-layer repair:** additional search layers can repeat the large continuous problem, and the inspected implementation relies on SCIP presolve access.
- **General-integer binarization:** already-binary movement decisions are the target; BTBS's bad-fixing correction is the more useful idea.
- **End-to-end charging prediction or solver-free complete fleets:** retain the optimizer and exact replay. No source here verifies replacement at EGG's physical scale.
- **A “safe” or calibrated pruning claim from sigmoid confidence, empirical coverage, or robust training:** none establishes complete optimal-fleet coverage under synthetic-to-public shift. These are heuristic search controls until an appropriate theorem/protocol is supplied.
