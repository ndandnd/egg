## Reprioritize the question

Supersede the **unfrozen** nine-arm factorial proposal explicitly. Ask whether
frozen learned ranking adds practical finite-budget value over credible
nonlearned search assistance, rather than whether restoration helps one ranker.
The factorial is useful for a later restoration interaction study, but spreads
this budget across too many cells before establishing a competitive baseline.

Use six fixed primary arms: graph-v8 recovery, CatBoost recovery, charging-aware
classical ranking with recovery, matched random ranking with recovery, full
cold8, and a constructive charging-feasible baseline. The four ranking arms
share the30%/top-three retention policy, tie rules, acquisition/restoration policy
and completion/admission rules. This policy does not match exact retained
cardinality: coverage unions can produce different movement counts. Report those
counts; the learned-value contrast evaluates the whole method and does not
isolate ranking quality at equal graph size. They restore the same full feasible set, so a learned
contrast is not merely a comparison of restricted graph feasibility. Keep the
constructive and charging-aware arms distinct: one builds a fleet/routes and
charging witness; the other ranks movements. Freeze both algorithms using
existing TRAIN/design information before new-cohort outcomes. Do not select the
best classical variant on study cases.

The constructive baseline is a prerequisite, not an optional limitation. Its
construction, full physical replay, charging completion and warm-start loading
pay the common clock. Cold8 remains the grounded unrestricted comparator; do
not substitute full4. Dropping static arms means this study cannot estimate the
causal benefit of restoration itself.

Primary: graph versus the frozen charging-aware recovery method. Direct
secondaries are graph versus CatBoost and constructive; use a prespecified
hierarchy and Holm across those two if inferential claims are pursued. Define

net-win contrasts, per-size validity differences and cluster-level intervals.
All eight bases are development groups; four per size give wide exploratory

envelope is descriptive, not a deployable matched-time baseline.
Both-valid percentage gains and fleet/deadhead/electricity decomposition are
secondary; conditional equal-fleet subsets are descriptive, not causal mediation.

The mechanism contrast holds target tariff, physics, seed and solver policy fixed
while changing only ranking price input. Recompute both scoring conditions inside
the clock; cached day scores are not free preprocessing. Report score/retained-
graph changes before bill changes for every prespecified mechanism base. Retain
zero-exposure/unchanged-feature cases; never replace them. A separate feature audit reports both learned
models receive linear prices but not nonlinear coefficients; therefore shifting
linear cheap windows is appropriate. Invariance to a b-only change limits

direct/nonwindow CatBoost price features fixed, subject to prespecified arithmetic

context mechanism, without guaranteeing an economic benefit. No scorer run was
performed for this review. Fresh-versus-frozen ranking contrasts
separate ranking responsiveness from native charging response. Four mechanism
bases support identification/descriptive effect sizes, not a strong tariff-
generalization claim; multiplicity-adjust the two learner mechanism contrasts
if inferential claims are attempted.
