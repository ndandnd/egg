## What maps, and what does not

A metabolic network uses reaction fluxes `v`, stoichiometric conservation `Sv = 0`, reaction bounds, and environmental resource limits. EGG has edge or route selection variables, time/location flow conservation, energy-state dynamics, charger capacity, and aggregate power constraints. Both exhibit compensation: deleting one reaction or edge can force use of a distant alternative through shared resources. A locally plausible edge score therefore does not characterize the feasibility or cost of a completed system.

The strong analogy concerns **constraint propagation and alternative feasible completions**, rather than literal biology. Steady-state molecular balance is not a vehicle's state of charge; charging stores energy over time and requires vehicle presence. FBA's continuous flux can split arbitrarily, whereas one bus cannot fractionally follow incompatible paths. A flux-consistent network means each retained reaction can carry flux in some distribution; it need not mean all reactions operate together in one solution. EGG requires one jointly feasible schedule. These distinctions invalidate naive transfers of convex combinations, reaction-wise tests, and shadow-price guarantees.

Safe control supplies a second mapping: state becomes time, location, battery and outstanding service obligations; proposed control becomes an edge or restricted candidate graph; a backup trajectory becomes a verified route-and-charge completion. Unlike a physical controller, an offline candidate generator does not execute its first action immediately. The architectural lesson is still useful: retain the old completion until the new one has passed the actual coupled feasibility checker. Recursive feasibility alone does not certify global objective quality or completion before a solver deadline.

## Classical baseline, prices, and labels

A repair baseline needs no new ML: given selected graph `E_hat` and full admissible graph `E`, minimize `sum(w_e a_e)` over restored edges `a_e`, subject to the **existing full physical route/charging model**, with edges outside `E_hat` enabled only by restoration. Use unit weights first; learned scores can later define weights. Report restoration count and completed objective separately: minimal additions can preserve an expensive schedule. If graph repair is expensive, an LP relaxation can screen candidates or furnish a bound, followed by the integral verifier. No theorem here makes the relaxation's completed solution physically usable.

For a minimization model, a verified feasible incumbent gives an upper bound `U`; a valid full-model relaxation gives a lower bound `L`. A restricted graph's optimum or its branch-and-bound lower bound need not be a lower bound on the original problem. Pruning may remove the original optimum. Preserve that distinction when reporting gaps. With charging power coupling `sum_i p_it <= P_t`, relaxation multipliers quantify marginal resource value within the continuous model, subject to sign convention and degeneracy. Re-optimizing after a capacity perturbation is a useful sensitivity check. These multipliers alone neither decentralize integral schedules nor prove an equilibrium: indivisibilities and an integrality gap may require additional payments or a different market design. This is an optimization inference, not a theorem imported from a biological paper.

For label design, use the incumbent correctly. Edge inclusion proves that at least one known completed schedule uses that edge. Exclusion proves neither infeasibility nor inferiority. For cost ceiling `B`, define the feasible quality set `F_B = {z: full constraints, cost(z) <= B}`. For a selected edge, ask whether feasible solutions exist with `x_e=1` and with `x_e=0`. Classify as required-in-band, optional-in-band, impossible-in-band, or unresolved according to certified outcomes. A time limit is unresolved. With `B=U`, this is an incumbent-quality band, not a near-optimal band; only a valid global lower bound can support a quantified optimality statement.

This yields a cheaper intermediate target: retain multiple diverse incumbent witnesses and train positive-unlabeled or ranking targets, recording provenance, objective and solver status. Include route-level features for residual power demand, slack and service obligations. Full variability analysis can be costly; sample edges near the pruning threshold, charger bottlenecks and disagreement cases. Evaluate calibration and false exclusions on physical instance families, with family/size/topology holdouts rather than random edge splits that leak graph context. These are proposed EGG adaptations. They do not assert that existing feasible solutions have become optimal labels.

## Ranked practical tests

**1. Verified backup plus minimum-addition repair.** Planning estimate: 3–5 engineering days if existing verification and candidate-graph hooks are reusable; 1–2 weeks if charging completion must be exposed. Retain a verified baseline completion and ensure its edges remain available, or explicitly restore them when the learned graph fails. Compare deterministic threshold pruning, equal-size random/diverse pruning, witness retention and weighted repair on identical held-out instances and wall-clock budgets. Record time to first verified completion, feasibility rate, incumbent cost, additions, memory and full-model bound provenance. Stop accepting improvements at the common deadline and return the stored witness. Pass criterion: lower completion latency without lost feasibility or worse returned cost than the retained baseline under the same total budget. This requires a baseline witness to exist in time; fallback cannot create one after the deadline. Check every charging event against vehicle presence and coupled power constraints.

**2. Quality-band labels versus single-incumbent imitation.** Planning estimate: 1–2 weeks for data/status plumbing and a bounded diagnostic; full retraining/data generation is additional, unestimated work. Start with existing public or explicitly authorized instances and sampled edges; enumerate or certify small cases before scaling. Compare original labels against multiple-witness labels and certified quality-band categories, holding model capacity, split and inference budget fixed. Measure recall of edges supporting any admissible quality-band completion, actual downstream feasibility/cost, abstention, and labeling time. Evaluate on distinct physical regimes as well as synthetic holdouts. Pass criterion: fewer catastrophic exclusions and better completed cost/latency, not merely higher classification accuracy. Adopt optional-edge labels only where feasibility evidence supports them; retain unresolved cases explicitly.

The useful synthesis is modular: ML ranks exploration, constraint optimization validates completions, stored witnesses preserve a feasible answer, and bounds/prices retain their precise mathematical scope. Biology supports sparse repair and alternative-solution analysis; control supports fallback architecture; robotics supports learned proposal distributions. None removes the need to certify EGG's coupled integral physics.
