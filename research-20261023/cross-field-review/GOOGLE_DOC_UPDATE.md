Cross-field literature review: what changes next — 23 October 2026

The broad literature review strengthens the research plan but narrows the novelty claim. Learned electric-bus connection pruning already exists; Olson, Lusby and Røpke's 2026 conference abstract also describes progressive restoration of discarded connections. Montanaro et al. (2025) supplies a serious nonlearned GRASP/pruning comparator. Wu et al. (2022) already combines multidepot scheduling, time-of-use prices, charger capacity and peak coordination. Its fixed-duration/full-charge assumptions must be compared with EGG's actual equations before claiming a new energy-coupling problem.

The strongest cross-field transfer concerns training labels. Metabolic flux-variability analysis distinguishes choices that are required, optional or excluded within an objective band. For EGG, absence from one incumbent is not evidence that an edge is bad. The proposed first label improvement is multiple diverse, replay-valid witnesses plus a bounded audit of ambiguous edges; unresolved solver outcomes stay unresolved. A band defined by an incumbent is not automatically near-optimal.

Selected configuration interaction in chemistry motivates ranking omitted choices in the context of the current solution, then restoring useful combinations. A verified 2026 chemistry paper explicitly studies learning to rank for selection. This supports a ranking-loss comparison, not an assumption that a Transformer will improve fleet decisions. Safe control motivates retaining a verified completion; sparse optimization distinguishes heuristic removal from bound-certified exclusion. Their specialized theorems do not automatically transfer to integral bus routing.

The five proposed experiments are: strong constructive baselines with matched recovery; price-information tests with fleet and energy-demand controls; alternative-solution labels; coordinated restoration; and selective full-model bound checks. Equal fleet count alone is insufficient, so the electricity test includes fixed-route charging and route-energy versus charging-timing decomposition. All studies need prospective protocols and complete online/offline cost accounting. No new solver or training run was performed for this review.

The current 80-trip gain remains mainly a fleet-search result: 91.916% of summed paired graph-versus-heuristic savings comes from vehicle-count cost. The immediate implementation priority remains common-clock recovery qualification and a strong constructive comparator. The research question is whether learning preserves valuable alternatives under shared energy constraints and improves decision quality or certification beyond strong classical methods. The review is ready for an independent reader; the model and publication claim remain unvalidated.
