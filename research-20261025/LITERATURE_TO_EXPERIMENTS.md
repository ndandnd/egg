# Three comparisons that decide the next experiments

25 October 2026. Targeted primary-paper check; recommendations below are proposed experiments, not results or execution authorization. No local project imports, numerical runs, tests, hashes, data downloads, sealed/GIRO access, or commits were performed.

**Decision:** run a strong classical candidate-pool challenge and a controlled electricity-mechanism test before expanding training or developing another recovery algorithm. Learning to prune bus connections, combining greedy and learned pools, and restoring a full pricing graph already have close precedents. A credible contribution must survive those controls and identify what the charging/resource coupling changes.

## Three precise experiment decisions

### 1. Does learning beat a good schedule pool?

**First screen:** use the current development timetables to challenge the explanation cheaply. Freeze one charging-aware randomized constructive/GRASP design and one root-LP-informed ranking before looking at arm results. Compare these with the existing GNN, CatBoost, a GNN–GRASP union, and full-model search from the start. This is a baseline challenge, not a new generalization result.

For every restricted arm, use the same physical model, protected structural arcs, verified common feasible witness, retained-candidate budget, completion procedure, and full-model recovery schedule. Choose a budget large enough to contain that common scaffold; count its arcs explicitly. Charge construction, LP scoring, inference, completion and recovery to the same online allowance. Report a second common-policy comparison if forcing identical cardinality materially changes a method. Show feasible-bill trajectories, first feasibility, failure rates, fleet count, route energy and charging cost; do not silently drop failed pairs.

**Confirmation if learning survives:** generate new independent timetable groups with nested 40/60/80-trip subsets, preserving the same source timetable within each size comparison. Hold out whole groups, not seed variants, and retain paired solver seeds. Freeze group count, total budget and a minimum useful cost/time margin before execution. These are proposed scales, not the papers' benchmarks. The statistical unit is the timetable group. If the strongest classical control matches the GNN within that prespecified margin, adopt the simpler control and stop architecture expansion. A hybrid gain alone establishes complementarity, not a new hybrid concept.

### 2. Is the surviving value really electricity information?

Pair the same trips/infrastructure across **flat, observed and time-permuted tariffs**, crossed with **slack versus binding charger/site-power capacity**. Compare frozen price-aware and tariff-blind rankings, the strongest classical control from experiment 1, and a cheap charging-opportunity score. Fix fleet count at a predeclared common feasible value, and report unrestricted total-cost results separately. Declare infeasible variants and common-fleet selection explicitly.

Use a **fixed-route charging optimization diagnostic** to establish how much price response is physically available when route energy is identical. It is a mechanism/ceiling diagnostic, not a fair route-ranking contest. For ranking arms, decompose electricity savings into changed energy demand and changed charging timing; equal bus counts alone do not remove the former. On sufficiently small cases use full-model bounds to report regret intervals, retaining unresolved cases.

Continue an electricity-aware learning claim only if price features add value beyond classical opportunity scores on new timetable groups and the effect changes predictably with binding shared resources. If the physical charging diagnostic has little value, reject this mechanism for that regime. If the diagnostic has value but tariff-blind/classical ranking captures it equally well, reject the need for learned price-aware ranking there. Do not tune on the perturbed evaluation cohort after inspecting outcomes.

### 3. Is adaptive restriction worth studying at all?

Only after a timing breakdown identifies candidate search or recovery as the bottleneck, compare frozen GNN, GRASP and LP-informed seeds under the **same unrestricted solver**, total clock and restoration schedule. Include full search from the same verified initial incumbent. Then, if justified, compare one prespecified stagnation-triggered expansion against that common schedule; hold the final full-model effort constant and charge all earlier work.

Measure time to first feasible solution and to a prespecified **valid full-model gap**, and separate primal gains from lower-bound progress. For a future genuine CG implementation, count restricted/full pricing calls and require the appropriate full-pricing termination check for an LP certificate. For the present compact MIP, only bounds from a valid full-model relaxation/search qualify. If unrestricted bounds remain weak, better ranking has not established faster certification. Do not pursue a recovery theorem merely because more restored arcs improve an incumbent.

## Already known, newly verified, and genuinely missing

- **Already known from the local strategy/reviews:** the reported 40/80-trip results are development evidence; at 80 trips, 91.916% of summed paired graph-versus-heuristic savings is fleet-count cost, not a decomposition of the median percentage gain. Different timetables confound the size comparison. Learned bus pruning, greedy–GNN combinations and generic recovery are prior art. These checks do not change those conclusions. [Existing strategy](../research-20261023/FT50_RESEARCH_STRATEGY.md), [EV review](../research-20261023/cross-field-review/EV_OR_REVIEW.md), [synthesis](../research-20261023/cross-field-review/CROSS_FIELD_SYNTHESIS.md).
- **Newly verified detail here:** the conventional GRASP chapter's exact test scales/splits and different-hardware caveat; Gerbaux's concrete greedy/GNN/hybrid trade-off; Morabit's strong reduced-cost comparator and its LP-only target. These tighten comparator selection; they are not new EGG results.
- **Genuinely missing EGG evidence:** matched strong classical pools and LP-informed controls; independent nested scale tests; price-feature value at fixed fleet and controlled route energy; a demonstrated computational bottleneck and full-model certificate benefit under common recovery. The local synthesis already proposed these questions; this note supplies more specific comparators and stopping decisions.

**Novelty boundary:** a possible contribution is a demonstrated, transferable shared-resource mechanism for which learned preservation of alternatives beats strong constructive and LP-informed controls, or a specific valid decision/certificate result exploiting that structure. Neither has been established. This targeted three-paper check does not prove nobody has already studied that narrower claim; formulation-level comparison and any theorem novelty check remain necessary if the experiments support pursuing it.
