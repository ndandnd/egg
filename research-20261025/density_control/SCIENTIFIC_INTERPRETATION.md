# What the equal-density control establishes

25 October 2026. Evidence: [master receipt](attempt01/receipt.json) and
[finalized summary](summary.json) only. Four assigned decisions completed once;
all four witnesses passed independent original-case replay and receipt-inclusive
120-second admission. No rerun, import, hash, test, solver call or raw-capture
inspection was performed for this interpretation.

Both arms retained all boundaries, used unchanged original physical rows, paid
fresh scoring/selection, and used the same pure fleet-count objective and native
seed17. The summary verifies the common B/K/identity contract. All four native
statuses were OPTIMAL, with incumbent/restricted fleet bound respectively
4/4, 3/3, 5/5 and 5/5. These are numerical bounds for the allowed-arc models,
not exact mathematical or full-space fleet-optimality certificates.

This supplies a concrete conventional competence result: the frozen CoverCA1
control meets the predeclared three/five-vehicle development targets on this
single exposed pair and seed. Ordinary CA1 meets the 80-trip target but misses
the 40-trip target. The finalized all-decisions `competence_passed=false` is
therefore preserved. A diagnostic pass and four feasible witnesses do not turn
that aggregate gate true, and successful-arm eligibility is not confirmation.

The density confound is narrowed in two ways. Within the new matched arms,
cardinality and backbone cannot explain the 40-trip difference: replacing 47
modes while protecting cover structure preserved a numerically better physical
fleet option in that restricted model. This does not identify which mode or
which SOC/resource interaction mattered. At 80 trips the pools differ by only
six replacements and both reach five vehicles; this offers little evidence of
an additional fleet benefit from cover protection there. Neither comparison
identifies a price effect or superiority across timetables.

The old sparse matching pool's 24-vehicle 80-trip result remains unchanged.
The denser controls show that conventional candidate selection can retain
five-vehicle alternatives, so sparse-pool failure is not evidence that learning
is necessary. They do not isolate density alone: the old experiment also had a
different candidate set/backbone and default rather than explicit native seed.
Historical greedy, full-search and learned outcomes additionally differ in
objective or allocation where applicable. Only the two new arms are matched
here; cross-experiment speed, objective and fleet differences are contextual.
Current fleet-first electricity costs were evaluated after solving, not
optimized, and cannot establish tariff-aware ranking value.

The highest-value next study is a separately frozen, small multi-group and
repeated-seed challenge on independent timetable groups. Retain **both** frozen
CA1 and CoverCA1 controls alongside the frozen graph/CatBoost rankings and full
search; do not select an apparent winner from these two exposed cases and then
treat it as an independently chosen final comparator. Prespecify common B/K for
restricted arms, physical constraints, solver seeds, objective, completion/
restoration policy, and one equal end-to-end clock charging preparation through
replay. Predeclare common-incumbent preparation and failures rather than dropping
difficult cases. Use timetable groups as the unit, report availability first,
then fleet and integrated-cost trajectories with electricity/energy components.
This first tests whether learned selection adds useful value beyond competent
conventional controls. A subsequent fixed-fleet, controlled-tariff intervention
is still required to identify learned electricity-information value.
