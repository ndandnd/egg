25 October 2026 — Energy demand and load shape remain mixed
The stronger fixed-route charging diagnostic made eight numerical QP calls. All reported an optimum, but only seven produced timely independently replayed charging plans; one failed physical session materialization and kept its valid prior. Its numerical optimum is not a feasible bill. In the seven successful cases the stronger optimizer improved every corresponding four-round control endpoint, but the three complete groups' median gains were only 0.077–0.160% of prior late electricity E0. The old large GNN–fixed-route contrast therefore cannot simply be dismissed as poor charging optimization in those cases; the missing eighth QP witness remains explicit.

A separate readout of all 168 stored operational endpoints found GNN used less total energy than the unchanged prior in all 24 repeated comparisons (mean reduction 9.0440%). Relative to Cover+CA1, GNN's mean energy reduction was 0.7977% of the control's energy, while its cost advantage remained 1.2319% of prior electricity E0. These use different denominators. In six of 24 GNN–Cover comparisons GNN was cheaper despite using more energy. Energy quantity alone therefore does not explain every cost ordering, but neither these signs nor the larger fixed-route contrast identifies learned tariff-information value: every downstream solver uses the target tariff.
