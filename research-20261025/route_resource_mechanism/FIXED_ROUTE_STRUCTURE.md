## What fixing a route actually fixes

For a fixed complete directed path system `r`, let `P_r(R)` be the feasible aggregate **period grid-energy vectors** under common resources `R`, and `c_r` its vehicle/deadhead cost. The ideal fixed-route value is `min_{L∈P_r(R)} c_r+a·L+½Σ_t b_tL_t²`. Distinguish this ideal projected set from tolerance-qualified stored-matrix/decoded witnesses; native numerical results alone do not establish set equality or exact minima.

The source changes the set through three linked mechanisms:

1. **Visit windows.** Only selected depot modes and terminal pullins admit charging. A depot visit is the gap at its declared `depot_split`; terminal availability begins at `max(last-leg arrival, terminal_open)` and ends at the recharge deadline. Direct/pullout modes offer no charging. Routes therefore select where/when vehicles are available, not merely a different encoding of the same timetable [windows](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_recharge.py:124).
2. **Resource intersection.** Atomic intervals split on market edges, resource boundaries and every declared window endpoint. Charging variables exist only for visit modes covering the whole interval; fixing a mode off forces its charge to zero. Interval capacity is `min(per_bus_kw,grid_kw) × hours` when a connector exists, otherwise zero, with a shared sum-of-charge cap. This is a **zero/one-connector** model; increasing connectors beyond one is outside its admitted structure [compiler](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_recharge.py:211), [charge rows](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_pathflow.py:182). Because only the minimum power enters these interval caps, raising grid power above per-bus power cannot expand that interval's capacity.
3. **SOC and allocation across visits.** Service consumes fixed trip energy. Selected movement legs consume their declared energies; depot arrival must retain reserve and charge cannot exceed battery headroom. SOC transfers to the next service after efficiency-adjusted visit energy. Terminal charge restores the bus to full. Thus the union or total duration of windows is insufficient: charging must go to the correct bus before its energy demand, within prefix reserve/headroom bounds [SOC rows](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_pathflow.py:161). Actual sessions are serially decoded within intervals, then independently checked for connector use, power and SOC; no overlapping-bus concurrency is created by fractional interval variables [serial allocation](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_recharge.py:553), [resource sweep](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_recharge.py:308).

In the ideal exact physical equations, full initial/final batteries and constant common efficiency imply:

`η Σ_t L_t = Σ_mandatory trips E_trip + Σ_selected movement legs E_leg`.

Fleet count cancels from this conservation identity. Mandatory service energy is common, but movement energy is route-dependent. Equal fleet size, membership counts or deadhead-cost coefficient zero does **not** imply equal grid demand. Conversely, charging timing cannot change ideal total grid energy for one fixed route under this efficiency model; there is no endogenous time-dependent efficiency in these constraints. Vehicle cost and declared travel-minute cost are separate fixed-route operations [operations](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_pathflow.py:242). Stored floating rows use a proved outward aggregate-energy band, while replay applies its unchanged energy tolerance; report those numerical qualifications rather than replacing them with an exact-equality rejection [aggregate rows](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_pathflow.py:225), [energy-band assumptions](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_pathflow.py:32), [physical conservation](/Users/nadan/Documents/ChatGPT/egg/ml-prediction-20261017/src/egglab/native_recharge.py:331).

## Tariff-independent equivalence and dominance: sufficient conditions

For nonempty sets and attained minima, **same projected set and same operations** (`P_r=P_s`, `c_r=c_s`) imply equal values for every common tariff. If sets agree but operations differ, values differ by that constant. Set inclusion `P_r⊆P_s` and `c_s≤c_r` makes `s` weakly dominate `r` for every common objective in this family, even if some linear prices are negative. These are classical feasible-set/projection arguments, not novelty claims. Two observed tariff ties do not establish either premise.

A concrete sufficient *special case* for equal projected sets: both routes have the same total grid demand; every bus can complete all travel without intermediate charging while respecting reserve; all required charging is permitted only in the same common terminal window after every vehicle has arrived; all buses have the same applicable rate and deadline, can split/preempt charging continuously, and resources are common with one serial connector. With all other charging opportunities absent or proved redundant, no additional per-vehicle deadlines/rate restrictions and full replenishment, vehicle demands can be serially allocated over any admissible aggregate profile in that window. The aggregate set reduces to common period/intersection capacities plus a common total-energy sum. Different route labels then need not matter. **Large total capacity alone does not establish these conditions**; an early SOC deficit, later arrival or extra useful depot visit breaks the argument.

For a weaker, **tariff-specific value** statement, use a sandwich rather than claiming projected-set identity. Suppose both routes have the same ideal total grid demand `E` and operations `c`. Let `C` be a common terminal-only aggregate set proved physically allocatable to each route's buses, and `A` a common coarse relaxation retaining nonnegative loads, common resource capacity and total energy `E` while dropping route-window/SOC restrictions. Then `C ⊆ P_r,P_s ⊆ A`. If an optimizer of the relaxed problem over `A` is attained in `C` (with a valid terminal-only allocation for both routes), both fixed-route minima equal that relaxation value plus `c` for **that tariff**. This is classical lower/upper-bound logic; neither a small returned cost difference nor nominal terminal capacity proves its premises. No relaxation solve is proposed or executed here.

This review did not open stored cases or independently expand the generator audit. Treat those as source-derived candidate bounds requiring verification on any newly admitted physical inputs, not row truth or proof of no-midday SOC feasibility. Even if all returns share that terminal window and every bus can avoid midday charging, **optional daytime depot charging can still lower quadratic supply cost** by spreading a fixed energy total across more periods, including under flat linear prices with positive curvature. Hence common terminal access/no operational need for midday charging is not sufficient for cost equivalence; the relaxation-attainability condition above or a stronger schedule-lifting proof is needed. Route energy equality is separate and essential: declared direct waiting/idle versus depot movements can change consumption `D`.

Equal energy and same window union are not sufficient for inclusion: buses may own different visits and SOC deficits. A valid schedule-lifting map must preserve each vehicle's charge availability and SOC bounds, not merely move aggregate kWh between anonymous windows. For routes with unequal demand, a coordinatewise-lower feasible-profile lifting can prove dominance only under an explicitly monotone cost family, e.g. `a_t≥0,b_t≥0` and no larger operations. The source permits finite negative `a`, so “less energy always wins” is not a tariff-independent statement over the entire supported price domain.

## Falsifiable mechanism and competing explanations

**Mechanism hypothesis:** a route-specific visit/window or SOC prefix provides access to scarce charging time that another route lacks; tightening a shared resource can then make their projected load opportunities, feasibility or reoptimized cost distinguishable, even after charging recourse. A necessary evidential chain is: actual different eligible visits/SOC timing → capacity/availability restriction relevant to those visits → changed feasible loading or credible fixed-route value difference. Many membership alternatives may instead be interchangeable where a common terminal charging region is sufficient. Both statements remain hypotheses for the exposed population.

**Counterexample to overclaiming shared scarcity:** two routes can have equal operations and total energy, but only one can charge in a cheap period; the other arrives after that period. With ample capacity and nonbinding power at the chosen schedules, the first route can still be cheaper for a positive cheap/expensive tariff contrast (`b=0` suffices). This is a schematic source-compatible window mechanism, not a constructed/replayed instance. Shared-capacity binding is therefore **not necessary** for route-price sensitivity. Conversely, a shared cap binding identically for two routes with identical projected sets does not create a route advantage.

Competing explanations to separate are: (i) unequal movement/grid energy rather than timing flexibility; (ii) unequal fleet/deadhead operations; (iii) a pure terminal-arrival/window exclusion without shared congestion; (iv) a common terminal region that attains the coarse relaxed optimum, making extra visits redundant **for the studied tariff**; (v) economically useful optional daytime charging that lowers curvature cost despite no SOC need; and (vi) numerical/qualification uncertainty rather than an economic difference. More depot modes or a larger window-area proxy alone identifies none of them. Returned optimal points at two tariffs neither prove aggregate-set identity nor identify the cause of a gap.
