**Energy conservation.** Full initial and terminal battery states, fixed nonnegative service/movement draws and constant common efficiency give `eta × total grid energy = total route draw`. The cancellation of fleet battery inventory is correct. Equal fleet or zero deadhead-cost coefficient does not establish equal movement energy. This is an ideal conservation statement, distinct from stored-row bands and replay tolerances; the notes preserve that distinction.

**Terminal construction.** The sufficient condition is sound: every vehicle can reach its final depot visit without charging or reserve violation; every final visit covers the same interval; no further energy draw remains; all vehicles share the applicable rate/deadline; and serial charging can split/preempt. Each deficit is nonnegative and fits the vehicle's available headroom. A common aggregate terminal profile can then be partitioned sequentially among those deficits, using exactly the same aggregate energy and never exceeding the common rate. In the source's finite period/resource setting, interpret the profile via its atomic resource/period intersections and a finite serial allocation, including zero capacity when no connector is available. Different deficit partitions do not obstruct this construction. Without these availability, headroom and splitting assumptions, total capacity or a union of windows would not suffice.

**Sandwich direction and scope.** `T ⊆ P_r ⊆ A` has the correct direction: minimizing over `A` supplies the lower bound, while a minimizer in the physically allocatable common `T` supplies an attaining upper bound for each route. Equal total energy and operating cost then imply equal value **at that tariff**, without equality of projected sets. The stronger projected-set equality special case correctly excludes, or requires redundancy of, other charging opportunities. The existence/attainment qualifications matter; neither note infers them from a returned numerical point.

**Optional daytime charging.** The caveat is essential and correctly stated. A route that needs no intermediate charge can still benefit from an optional earlier depot visit through a cheaper linear price or positive quadratic-cost smoothing. Thus a long common terminal interval and adequate reserve are not, by themselves, an economic equivalence proof. The common terminal subset must attain the relevant relaxed objective, or another valid lifting argument must establish the claim. The source-derived timing bound is explicitly a generator statement, not verification of stored inputs or battery sufficiency.

**Binding power and monotonicity.** Binding shared power is neither necessary nor sufficient for route-price sensitivity. A cheap-window exclusion can distinguish routes even when power is slack at an optimal schedule; this schematic counterexample concerns the supported window formalism, not a claim that the inspected generator realizes it. Identical projected sets can have identical binding caps and identical values. Reducing only effective capacity gives a nested feasible set and a nondecreasing ideal value for each fixed route, but gives no monotonicity of the difference between route values. The notes also correctly distinguish reducing grid power from changing the effective `min(bus, grid)` capacity.

**Interpretation and next experiment.** Energy/operations, window exclusion, SOC timing, optional smoothing and numerical uncertainty remain competing explanations. Two tariff ties do not prove set identity; differing route labels do not prove different useful recourse. A future resource interaction needs prospective input/physics admission and an actual relevant opportunity change, with failures retained and no outcome-driven cap selection. Such an interaction would establish neither an ML increment nor information causality. The notes make no numerical certificate, global routing optimum, novelty or launch claim. Their classical sufficient-condition reasoning is suitable for designing a falsifiable experiment, not certifying current witnesses.
