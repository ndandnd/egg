## What the components do and do not identify

Fleet count is two, vehicle/operations cost is 200 and service energy is 160 kWh throughout. Deadhead-cost contribution is zero. Thus the contrasts do not buy more buses or reduce mandatory service, and movement-minute differences are not a monetary labor saving in this model.

Unchanged and target-QP common endpoints retain movement energy 40 kWh and the same represented total grid energy. The charging-only cost improvement therefore comes from a changed charging allocation under the fixed physical path, not lower route-energy demand. After duty-preserving optimization, movement energy is 20 kWh in b0/posted/system and 18 kWh in strategic; the corresponding full endpoints have those same component values. Grid energy is correspondingly lower than the common path (200 kWh for the first three dependent contexts). The strategic endpoint has the lower recorded grid-energy value as well. These component observations are consistent with useful movement realization and charging recourse **without passenger-duty reassignment**.

They do not separate the cost effect of reduced movement energy from changed charging windows, timing or loading. Mode choice changes physical consumption and opportunities together; even linear posted prices vary over time. The strategic and system contexts may select different energy/timing tradeoffs under their respective objectives. Neither a lower quadratic component nor an ordered-duty change identifies a causal charging-access or information effect. Identical costs/component totals also do not imply identical charge sessions or period profiles. No such stronger comparison was performed here.
