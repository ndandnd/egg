# Narrow source verification — 28 October 2026

Review inspected the relevant fulltext passages, not search titles alone.
This is a small positioning check, not an exhaustive novelty clearance.

- [Hümbs, Martin and Schewe (2022)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00186-022-00775-z):
  model, complete-description definition and Theorem1 connect supporting linear
  prices with an integral optimum of a central relaxation. EGG's support/hull
  equivalence is a specialization, not new general economic theory.
- [Hua and Baldick (2017)](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~baldick/papers/convex_hull_2017.pdf):
  SectionsII–III/Theorem1 and ramping extension connect duality, participant
  convexification and lost opportunity costs. Convex-hull pricing and ramps are
  established; EGG places indivisibility in bus service assignments.
- [Andrianesis et al. (2022), author version](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.13331v2):
  SectionsII–III use entire feasible participant schedules as columns and
  self-scheduling as pricing. Whole-plan columns and their comparison with
  subgradient methods are precedents, not EGG algorithmic novelty.
- [Zoltowska and Lin (2021)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/energies/energies-14-04727/article_deploy/energies-14-04727.pdf?version=1628071003):
  aggregation/disaggregation and case-study passages model detailed bus charging
  and charger occupancy from given trip-energy/availability inputs. Their model
  permits terminal inventory down to its minimum. EGG's endogenous assignment
  and compulsory full replenishment are meaningful scope differences, not proof
  of a first-ever transport/grid integration.

[Hreinsson et al. (2021)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9376954/) was checked
only at the abstract-level IEEE record9376954. It supports acknowledging established
aggregation/approximate-convexity work. Do not claim that full paper lacks a
whole-owner/coalition-regret result: that comparison remains unverified.

Working contribution wording: fully replenished bus constructions and explicit
conditions showing when continuous charging does or does not restore marginal
price support, with separate participant-scale and admissible-price diagnostics.
No “first” claim. The new capacity/fee theorem is a small structural extension
within this project. Its publication significance is not established by code
agreement, source review or a finite benchmark.
