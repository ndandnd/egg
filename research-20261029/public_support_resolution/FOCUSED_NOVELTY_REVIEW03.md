## Consequence for this manuscript

The operational overlap is material: do not position endogenous assignment, fixed fleet fees, bidirectional depot scheduling, partial charging or spare-bus economics as the new contribution. Full replenishment should be defended as a necessary assumption for our exact claim, not advertised as absent from this work.

The plausible narrower distinction remains our **exact joint net-load–vehicle-cost projection** for the common-window, allowed-successor-graph model: the minimum-gross-throughput lemma, matching-cardinality thresholds, convex frontier, physical-attainment/support classification and conditional idle-bus elimination. Those are classifications of a deliberately restricted physical opportunity set. They are not a richer practical scheduling formulation or a new heuristic. In the inspected model/claimed-contribution sections, the economic problem is input-tariff cost minimization; our own-load support test additionally compares physical planners and complete-plan convexification. No corresponding threshold/support theorem was identified in these inspected sections. This is a bounded formulation comparison, not a claim of absence throughout V2G literature.

Strongest rejection remains: the restriction to simultaneous batches and ideal serial connector physics may reduce the result to elementary aggregation/matching plus classical nonconvex pricing, while richer V2G bus operation is already modeled elsewhere. The new public strict-gap certificate strengthens the existence evidence at scale, but synthetic economics and a small certified separation do not establish practical importance or V2G prevalence.
