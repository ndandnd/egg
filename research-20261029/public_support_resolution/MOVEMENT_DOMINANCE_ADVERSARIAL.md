# Independent adversarial pass: metric-path canonicalization

**Verdict:** separate directed triangle inequalities for time and distance support a plausible componentwise-purchase dominance theorem under carefully shared access rules. Battery upper bounds alone do not defeat it: curtailment is essential. However, “single-depot-only charging” does not by itself supply the required access rules. In particular the descriptor's special terminal-opening rule can invalidate unrestricted-path coverage if an intermediate post-service visit is allowed to charge before that opening. Do not silently promote the current certificate without resolving that policy and canonical-mode availability.

## A concrete terminal-policy counterexample

Suppose B=20, unit efficiency, zero reserve, a20kW single depot connector, terminal opening1 and deadline3/2 (hours). A mandatory service starts with the full battery and ends at depot D at time0 with zero inventory. There is one other finite location X. Directed times D→X=X→D=1/10hour and distances D→X=X→D=1km, with zero diagonals. Both two-location matrices satisfy triangle inequalities. Use exactly the proposed travel energy0.96kWh/km+16kWh/hour and off-depot waiting16kWh/hour; depot waiting is free.

If an unrestricted intermediate depot visit after the last service can charge before terminal opening, the bus buys20kWh during[0,1], takes D→X→D during[1,6/5], consuming128/25kWh, and refills that amount at20kW by182/125hours, which is less than3/2. It finishes full with one bus and the same mandatory service order. Every action is feasible and every purchase is at the only charging depot.

The canonical immediate terminal pull-in at time0, when charging is restricted to[max(arrival,1),3/2], can buy only10kWh. It is infeasible, let alone componentwise dominating. This does not depend on a nonmetric route, extra charging location, loss, reserve or negative price. It exploits a **visit-label-dependent access distinction**, not travel geometry. It is an illustrative analytical instance, not a claim about the actual public descriptor or its terminal-opening value.

If the proposed broad model instead prohibits ALL post-last-service charging before opening, including intermediate visits, the counterexample is excluded. If terminal opening is0 or equals a global resource opening that binds every visit, it is likewise excluded. A full dominance theorem must state one of these common-policy conditions, or add an appropriate earlier-charge canonical terminal mode and recertify the enlarged model. Simply deleting the excursion while keeping early charges does not fit the old terminal window.

## What survives the other adversarial checks

Fix one bus's mandatory service order and the same finite time-independent location set. Between consecutive services:

* **No depot visit:** replace the path by the direct OD arc. Its duration and distance are no greater. The saved travel time becomes off-depot waiting; because travel auxiliary and waiting auxiliary rates are identical, auxiliary consumption over the fixed service-to-service gap is unchanged. Distance consumption decreases. There is no intervening charging, so inventory is monotone and the smaller total consumption preserves feasibility even if travel and waiting are reordered.
* **At least one depot visit:** replace travel before the first depot arrival and after the last departure by the direct inbound/outbound arcs. Use immediate inbound travel and latest feasible outbound departure. Triangle inequalities make this depot interval contain the original first-arrival/last-departure span. Delete all intervening excursions; parked auxiliary is zero. Inbound consumption and total outbound-plus-off-depot-waiting consumption do not increase. Every original charging time remains a physical depot-presence time, provided the access policy does not change with the new visit label.
* **Shared connector and hourly loads:** retain the original bus's charging power only where needed and only at its original times. Dropping purchases preserves the shared connector calendar, each hourly load weakly decreases, and used-bus fees are unchanged. No energy is shifted to a different hour. This requires continuously divisible charging with no positive minimum session, ramp, taper, switching time, minimum dwell or payment for unused sessions.

The capacity issue cannot be handled by blindly replaying every old charge: less consumption can cause overfill. Instead curtail an original charge whenever it would exceed B. While parked, compare the canonical inventory with the original inventory at the same retained charge events. Saturating at B cannot place it below an original feasible inventory; deleted excursions and off-depot waiting only improve that comparison. At the latest outbound departure the direct remaining travel consumes no more than the original remainder, preserving the next service's initial inventory. Repeat this argument gap by gap. The original full terminal inventory supplies enough retained charging to reach B by the deadline; stop exactly at B, subject to the common terminal-access condition above. Thus upper bounds are a proof obligation resolved by curtailment, not a counterexample by themselves.

Latest direct pullout is also safe when every bus is initially full at the same depot, there are no relevant pre-service mandatory tasks, and no earlier availability/departure restriction is violated: initial gratuitous excursions and their refills can be removed. Earliest direct pull-in reaches the depot no later than any allowed terminal path, but dominates only if its permitted charging times include those of the original post-service visits. A terminal horizon must be retained, not silently extended.

## Exact assumptions needed before a coverage claim

1. Time and distance triangle inequalities must both hold in the exact coefficient interpretation, separately for every allowed directed intermediate location. Nonnegative distances/times are also needed. No actual-matrix check is supplied here.
2. The same nonnegative distance coefficient and common travel/waiting auxiliary coefficient must generate leg energy consistently. A triangle inequality for rounded times/distances alone does not automatically prove one for separately rounded/stored leg energies.
3. Canonical direct/via-depot/pullout/pull-in modes must actually exist for every relevant endpoint/order in the broad model. Geometry cannot supply a missing source mode or change a declared fleet cap.
4. All original depot charging times must remain permitted after canonicalization, including terminal/pre-service rules. Site calendars, initial full charge, final full charge and ownership/resource rights must be identical.
5. No alternate depot, intermediate charger, mandatory relocation, location-dependent waiting draw, time-varying OD duration, inventory-dependent energy or other benefit of discretionary visits is included.

Componentwise purchase dominance is physical and does not require nonnegative prices. Its **economic** implication does: at a negative hourly purchase price the more consumptive excursion can be privately preferable, so deleting it need not preserve minimum private bill. Similarly a nonmonotone supply objective can prefer a dominated purchase vector. For the declared charge-only increasing supply objective and nonnegative operating costs, those objections do not apply, but no negative-price response-oracle claim follows.

The sensible theorem is therefore conditional canonicalization under metric OD data **and matching depot-access policies**, not “triangle inequalities alone make the current descriptor complete.” Within those conditions I found no counterexample from SOC upper bounds, waiting placement, curtailment or shared-hourly-load accounting. The terminal-policy counterexample remains the main gap to resolve before extending the certificate's stated opportunity set.
