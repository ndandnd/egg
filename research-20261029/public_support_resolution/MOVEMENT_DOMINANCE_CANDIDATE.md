# Conditional canonical-movement dominance under componentwise triangle inequalities

Source-only prospective theorem. It extends the narrow fixed-arc reasoning in the frozen MODEL_TO_CERTIFICATE_REVIEW without editing that review, the paper or consumed sources. No actual matrix, case values, evidence, numerical property check, execution, imports, tests, hashes or native work. **No assertion is made that the public matrices satisfy the hypotheses.**

## Precise hypotheses

There is one selected depot D and a fixed finite set of declared locations. Every directed OD arc is an available jointly specified travel-time/distance pair(tau_ij,d_ij), constant over the horizon. Both matrices are nonnegative, have zero diagonals, and satisfy their directed triangle inequalities separately. Thus a direct OD arc uses no more time AND no more distance than any finite path with those endpoints. This requires the OD pair to describe an admitted realizable leg, not incompatible time/distance minima from different routes.

Services have fixed positive-duration time windows, endpoints and nonnegative prescribed battery consumption; a feasible bus has a fixed ordered service sequence. Every counted bus serves at least one service, starts full at D and must finish full at D by the same terminal deadline. The original fleet cap is retained. Additional trips between services perform no mandatory task. Charging is continuous, charge-only and lossless, occurs ONLY at D, and has no minimum session/power, taper, switching overhead or inventory-transfer feature. The single shared connector and all individual acceptance/resource periods are unchanged.

Travel energy is EXACTLY kappa_d*d_ij+rho*tau_ij in the physical arithmetic, with kappa_d>=0 (the stated coefficient0.96) and rho>=0 (16 per hour). EVERY nondepot waiting period consumes the SAME rho per hour as travel auxiliary draw; parked time at D consumes zero. There are no other zero-draw parking locations, charging locations, traffic-dependent arc times, negative/regenerative energy, location-dependent waiting rates or unlisted tasks. The condition concerns the selected depot; a source-labelled but inactive second depot cannot silently receive free waiting or charging access. Independently rounded stored leg-energy totals do not automatically satisfy this exact linear identity: distance/time triangle checks alone cannot cross that binary-arithmetic seam. A later application must verify the actual energy-dominance relations or justify the exact consistent energy law; intended decimal formulas are insufficient.

The canonical opportunity set includes every chronologically feasible direct mode and one-via-D mode between each pair of services, plus all feasible pullouts/pullins, using these same OD pairs. Via-D inbound leaves immediately after service; outbound leaves as late as possible to reach the next service exactly. Direct travel is immediate with the remaining gap spent at the destination. Initial pullout is latest feasible; terminal pullin is immediate. If a service endpoint is D, a zero-length leg/gap must preserve any depot charging access rather than discard it through a “direct” label. ALL charging after a bus's last mandatory service follows the SAME opening/deadline policy, regardless of the visit's mode label; additional visits cannot obtain earlier charging by being called internal. A zero terminal opening is a sufficient special case, not an actual public-case value verified here. No minimum off-depot dwell or other constraint rewards retiming/excursions.

These canonical inclusion conditions are necessary in addition to matrix inequalities. A complete OD matrix alone does not guarantee that a mode generator actually emits every required candidate/window.

## Claim

For any feasible fleet using arbitrary finite paths/retiming over those SAME locations, with the stated charging access, there is a canonical fleet with the SAME buses and service order, unchanged vehicle fee and componentwise no larger grid-energy purchase in EVERY market period. Battery capacity/reserve, service feasibility, connector constraints and full terminal inventory remain satisfied. This is domination/equality of optimal values for monotone economics, **not equality of physical projections or all response sets**.

## Construction by consecutive-service gaps

Proceed along each old bus's service sequence, maintaining that its new inventory immediately after each completed service is at least the old inventory. Start with a genuinely full battery; the initial leg is addressed below.

**A gap with no depot presence.** Let its fixed duration be T. The old path's total travel time plus off-depot wait is T, so energy is kappa_d*(sum distances)+rho*T. The canonical direct trip fits because tau_direct<=sum travel times<=T and uses kappa_d*d_direct+rho*T, no larger by the distance triangle inequality. With no charging, inventory is nonincreasing. New end inventory is at least old end inventory, and is the new minimum over the gap; thus reserve holds everywhere and capacity cannot increase. Both service endpoints are nondepot in this branch; depot presence even at an endpoint belongs to the next branch. Redistributing waiting between nondepot locations does not change auxiliary energy under the common rho assumption.

**A gap containing depot presence.** Let a be the first old arrival at D and b the last old departure before the next service, counting zero-length/endpoint visits. Canonical direct inbound arrives at a'<=a: its time is no greater than the old inbound path plus waiting. Canonical latest outbound leaves at b'>=b: its time is no greater than the old outbound path plus waiting. Hence the canonical visit[a',b'] contains EVERY original depot charging session in the gap, including sessions separated by excursions. Its inbound/outbound energies are individually no larger than the old prefix/suffix energies, by the distance/time inequalities and nonnegative waiting consumption. The canonical visit exists whenever that old connection does.

The new inventory at a is at least the old first-arrival inventory. During[a,b], the canonical bus stays parked with zero draw; the old bus may travel/wait outside D between visits and has nonnegative draw. Keep the old instantaneous charging function at the SAME times until the new battery reaches capacity B, then suppress further charging during this canonical gap. If W is the new inventory at a and Q(t) the old cumulative charge since a, the new inventory is exactly min(B,W+Q(t)). The old inventory is no greater than both B and W+Q(t), since it starts no higher and subtracts nonnegative consumption. Thus the new inventory dominates it at b. This construction is physically charge-only, never exceeds capacity and uses instantaneous charging no greater than the old rate.

Between b and canonical departure b' there is no old charging session left in this gap, and the new bus remains parked. Its canonical outbound energy is no larger than old post-b consumption, so its next-service arrival inventory is at least the old one. Nonnegative inbound/outbound consumption makes their endpoint inventories the relevant minima, proving reserve in transit as well. Service consumption is unchanged, hence the induction continues. No triangle inequality is needed to delete an excursion that leaves and returns to D; the triangle hypotheses are used for replacing the inbound/outbound endpoint paths by direct OD legs.

## Initial and terminal connections

For the initial connection, replace every pre-first-service excursion by staying full at D and taking the direct latest feasible pullout. The old bus's last depot-departure inventory is at most B, and its remaining travel/wait consumption is at least the canonical direct consumption. The latest canonical departure is no earlier than that old last departure, so it is inside the horizon and reaches the first service with no less inventory. No initial charging is needed; zero purchase is no greater than the old purchase.

After the final service, direct immediate pullin reaches D no later and with no less inventory than the first old return. Retain old charging times after terminal opening, curtailing once full, and delete later unnecessary excursions. The old final inventory is B, so the new inventory, maintained at least as high and at most B, also ends at exactly B. If terminal opening is positive, both schedules must obey that same policy; no early recharge may be manufactured by classifying a terminal visit as internal. This equal-access assumption is indispensable to the claimed containment of every old charging clock.

The independent MOVEMENT_DOMINANCE_ADVERSARIAL.md identifies precisely this obstruction to triangle-only reasoning: an extra post-service depot visit may exploit pre-opening power that the canonical terminal mode cannot access. Triangle inequalities constrain travel, not the charging-access semantics. Such a schedule is outside this candidate's equal-access hypotheses; without that hypothesis the proposed dominance conclusion is false. No actual public opening value is inferred by this analytical note.

## Shared connector and periodwise load dominance

Apply the constructions to all buses, KEEPING their old charging clocks. Each bus's new rate is pointwise between zero and its old rate; newly gained depot time is not needed for extra purchases. Consequently no original connector conflict, aggregate power limit or individual acceptance constraint is worsened. Integration over each unchanged market period yields e_new,t<=e_old,t separately, not just a lower total energy sum. The fleet count and service order are unchanged; the public monetary movement coefficient is zero, so intrinsic cost is unchanged. Nonnegative travel-cost variants would additionally need their particular cost model checked.

A counted bus with no mandatory service falls outside the “same service-covering paths” premise. In charge-only operation its pointless excursions/charging can instead be deleted along with its nonnegative fee; this gives weakly lower cost, but not the claimed same-count representation by canonical service paths. Keep these two statements distinct.

## Economic and support consequences

Let X be the canonical set and Y the broader path/retiming set under these hypotheses. X is a subset of Y and every y has a canonical x with e(x)<=e(y),c(x)<=c(y). For coordinatewise nondecreasing F, replacing each plan or each finite whole-plan mixture component proves equality of physical and hull infimum values between X and Y. With the usual compactness/attainment assumptions these are equal D and CH, so a positive canonical gap survives in Y. This is a VALUE result; dominated additional physical points still enlarge the literal projection.

For ANY fixed nonnegative posted price vector, V_X(p)=V_Y(p); a retained canonical plan's regret at that unchanged price is the same. Extra plans may tie if their added purchases have zero price. For differentiable monotone supply with an admissible nonnegative own gradient, a supported canonical optimum remains supported in Y; a strictly positive common gap excludes own-load support in Y by the classical criterion. No assertion of all tied responses, negative-price value preservation, tariff-reset equilibrium or unrestricted price-support equivalence is made.

## What remains unproved for the public case

The frozen generator source uses the stated maximal via-depot gap policy, but this note has not checked actual OD triangle properties, zero diagonals, canonical mode completeness, selected-depot-only zero-draw interpretation or joint OD-leg realizability. Without them the existing public certificate remains restricted to its finite declared modes. Arbitrary detours can beat a direct OD arc in a nonmetric matrix; additional low-draw parking or different charging locations also break the simple dominance argument. A later prospectively frozen property check could test the explicit finite hypotheses; it must not be described as this note having already established them or as a calibrated operational result.
