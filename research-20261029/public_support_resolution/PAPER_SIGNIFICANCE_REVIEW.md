# Focused paper: adversarial significance and readability review

29 October 2026. Read the new `paper/focused-theory-20261029` main narrative, framework, construction, expansion, ownership, public-case and related-work sections. This is a source-only editorial/scientific-positioning review, not a repeated proof audit or literature search. No execution, numerical work, source hashes or data inspection. The withdrawn Hao comparison is irrelevant to this assessment.

## Judgment

The draft now has a coherent question and credible claim discipline. It is a focused analytical paper about a deliberately specified bus/tariff model. Its main unresolved weakness is **why the exact physical characterization is important enough beyond illustrating established nonconvex support theory**. The new equipment region is more substantive than the general expansion proposition or movement-only stability result. Correctness and transparency are necessary; neither establishes sufficient significance.

The current abstract accurately separates the old framework from new physical results, but lists too many equal-weight contributions. A reader can finish it without knowing which theorem supplies the paper's main new insight. Lead with the capacity/fee and equipment characterization, use the nominal example to explain it, and present replication as a separate implication rather than another coequal paper.

## Strongest rejection arguments

1. **A familiar theorem is embedded in a tiny application.** The complete physical projection is two service partitions with a continuous charging segment. Convexifying their projected operating-cost boundary and minimizing a quadratic is elementary. A reviewer can accept every formula and still conclude that the resulting phase regions are a worked example rather than a substantial new result. The draft's explicit attribution appropriately prevents overclaiming but does not answer this objection.

2. **The preservation class is economically unchallenging.** Additional nonnegative-consumption movements cannot improve the old energy/cost frontier under the fixed windows/equipment. Dominance is exactly why the obstruction survives. Calling this “stability under richer alternatives” without immediately stating domination would exaggerate its scope. The draft currently discloses this, but gives the theorem enough narrative prominence to invite the objection. Treat it as a boundary clarification or lemma; foreground the changed-equipment region, which actually changes the useful frontier.

3. **The institution is possible but not yet motivated.** A large operator generates its own marginal tariff, then treats that tariff as fixed across complete-fleet alternatives. This is a coherent support test, but the draft offers no concrete tariff/coordination practice for which this is the decision facing an operator. Full replenishment makes finite-horizon accounting sound; it does not establish that operating institutions use this pricing experiment. Explain the diagnostic role without claiming a market-design failure or strategic result.

4. **The public case is a bound, not validation of the mechanism.** Its interval includes zero; two depot variants are one timetable. The source is public, but economics and energy assumptions are synthetic. The section currently spends substantial space on archival exact-versus-native details before giving the open sign question. A reviewer could read this as computational inability added to an otherwise small analytical paper. Even a resolved sign under this scenario would establish scale, not prevalence or calibrated importance.

5. **Replication changes the participant and supply scaling.** The result is insightful only if the reader keeps absolute whole-owner gain, per-bus gain and reserved-owner gain distinct. It does not refute standard atomization intuition. The draft correctly says this. Its novelty relative to the complete aggregation/coalition literature remains uncleared; do not make it the headline solely because the nonvanishing limit is striking.

## Evidence or exposition improvements feasible within the authorized work

These are bounded priorities, not promises that any result will be obtained.

- **One decisive public certificate would add more than another favorable toy scan.** Report a new exact sign certificate only if it actually closes the global question. If it does not, preserve the old interval and explain the precise missing oracle/certificate. Do not promote a finite-bank optimum or positive incumbent regret into an optimal-fleet conclusion.
- **Explain the parameter mechanism before presenting the formulas.** State which frontier changes when K grows, why f trades an extra bus against a different charging window, and why B changes the minimum necessary early purchase. A simple physical-branch/hull diagram and two parameter-region diagrams would make the main theorem inspectable without treating implementation checks as evidence of generality.
- **Close or explicitly bound the closest institutional source gap.** Wu2019 currently remains abstract/preview-only. Its full formulation would help establish the precise distinction between strategic coordination and the present fixed-tariff test. An unsuccessful bounded retrieval must remain a limitation, not an invented absence claim.
- **Add a brief “why terminal accounting changes the question” explanation.** Compare the logical role of initial depletion with full replacement, without asserting that earlier models are invalid. Both assignments here buy30kWh; timing and vehicle fee alone drive the result. That is the useful isolation, more precise than generic statements that full charging is new.
- **Add a theorem-to-question table.** Nominal example: possibility. Capacity/fee theorem: exact regime. Movement dominance: which additions are harmless. Equipment theorem: genuine erasure and fee dependence. Replication: whose incentive. Public section: what global evidence remains unknown. This reduces the appearance of a theorem collection.

## Readability changes with high return

The draft is commendably candid but repeatedly interrupts scientific exposition to assess its own originality and readiness. The abstract, draft-status block, introduction, related-work ending and implications all reiterate limitations. Keep necessary assumptions and one clear scope paragraph; move workflow judgments (“requires critical assessment,” “not a submission-readiness judgment,” “closest-prior-art assessment is remaining”) to a cover note or research-state document. The manuscript should state the claim and evidence clearly rather than repeatedly narrating its evaluation process.

Start the public section with its present mathematical outcome and significance: “The only established nonlinear interval includes zero.” Put the detailed synthetic scenario and exact/native provenance afterward, with archival details in an appendix if needed. Present the proposed certificate routes as a methodological outlook, not new results.

Notation could be easier: avoid reusing x for both a complete fleet and scalar early energy without a prominent reset; define K explicitly as terminal power and note that one-hour windows identify its numerical energy limit. In the equipment theorem use a visibly different fee symbol with one sentence connecting it to the original fee7. The current q notation accomplishes this but needs the intuitive sentence before the formulas. A learner should not need the appendix to discover that a=30−B is the one-bus minimum early charge.

The abstract's first sentence is memorable but only states the obvious indivisibility. Replace it with the actual question and answer. “No supported physical plan” must be understood as under the declared tariff, supply and complete-plan set; retain that context immediately nearby.

## Suggested title and abstract hierarchy

Preferred title: **“When Marginal Prices Support Fully Replenished Electric-Bus Fleets”**. It accommodates both failure and erasure. The current title is also accurate. Avoid “general,” “robust,” “market design,” “first,” or “scalable” unless future evidence earns those words.

Claim order:

1. Main result: exact support region as connector capacity, vehicle fee and battery options vary in the declared model.
2. Physical anchor: full replenishment and continuous charging still allow failure with equal total purchased energy.
3. Boundary insight: dominated movement additions preserve the frontier, useful equipment additions can erase failure, and beating the old hull is insufficient.
4. Participant insight: whole-owner absolute regret differs from reserved individual incentives under the stated replication/scaling.
5. Evidence limit: public support is unresolved unless the new certificate actually resolves it; no prevalence claim.

Possible concise abstract, preserving current evidence:

> We characterize marginal-price support in a two-service electric-bus model with continuous charging and full initial and terminal inventories. Although both service assignments purchase the same total energy, their charging windows and vehicle costs can prevent any physical fleet from minimizing its bill at its own marginal supply prices. We derive exact support regions as connector power, vehicle fees and available battery capacities change. Nonnegative-energy movement additions preserve the obstruction under unchanged resources, while a new battery type can remove it; a fee-dependent counterexample shows why a new plan cheaper than the old convexified optimum is insufficient. A replicated family distinguishes a whole owner's absolute deviation gain from vanishing incentives of separately resourced participants. The price-support framework is classical; the results characterize its physical realization in the stated fleet model. Existing bounds for one public37-service timetable leave optimal-fleet support unresolved.

Update the final sentence only after any new public conclusion is independently certified. This abstract does not imply submission readiness; it makes the actual scientific contribution easier to judge.
