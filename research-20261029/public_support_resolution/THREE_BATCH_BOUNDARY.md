# A classical three-batch tractability boundary

This is a prospective analytical proposition, not a numerical experiment or a new EV-scheduling hardness claim. It explains one limitation of the paper's two-batch matching reduction. No public instance, artifact or solver was inspected or executed.

## Proposition

Fleet feasibility is strongly NP-complete in the following restricted three-batch model: each batch consists of n simultaneous mandatory positive-energy services; batches are chronologically ordered and nonoverlapping; all pairwise transitions between consecutive batches are available with zero time and zero energy; at most n identical buses of battery capacity B start full; no charging or discharging is available before completion of the third batch; nonservice consumption is zero; reserve is zero; and all buses must refill to B in a common terminal window served by one connector with a fixed positive charging power. The terminal window is chosen sufficiently long in the reduction. Service-energy sum is exactly nB. Thus terminal charging is compulsory but is not the source of combinatorial difficulty.

“Simultaneous batches” means simultaneous services WITHIN each batch, not that all three batches overlap. Three mutually overlapping batches would have a different fleet requirement. Services have strictly positive duration, cannot be split or interrupted, and are counted exactly once. No extra idle buses, battery exchanges or departure-and-return reinitializations are permitted.

## Reduction and full physical feasibility

Take Numerical 3-Dimensional Matching: three labeled positive-integer multisets A, C, E, each of size n, and target B. A solution partitions them into n triples, one element of each class, each summing to B. We may restrict to total sum nB: any instance violating this equality is immediately a NO instance. A polynomial algorithm for the balanced restriction would therefore solve the unrestricted problem by this precheck. Equivalently, a many-one reduction sends such immediately rejected inputs to a fixed balanced NO instance. The same observation applies to an element exceeding B. These restrictions do not remove strong hardness.

Create one service per element, with battery consumption equal to that element, in its corresponding batch. Choose common unit service intervals [0,1], [1,2], [2,3], using the usual half-open resource convention. Consumption during each service is nonnegative, for example uniform at the prescribed total; there is no instantaneous recharge at batch boundaries. All service endpoints may be identified with the same abstract location. Transition compatibility is complete and transitions consume neither time nor energy. Being at that location does not grant charging before time 3: charger availability is terminal-only.

Each batch has n overlapping services. At most n buses therefore means exactly n counted buses, each serving exactly one service in EVERY batch. A duty is consequently a triple (a,c,e). With no intervening charging and initial SOC B, its battery feasibility is precisely a+c+e <= B. Positive consumption ensures every prefix also fits whenever the total fits. Summing these n inequalities and using total service energy nB forces equality on every bus. Hence a feasible schedule induces an exact numerical three-dimensional matching.

Conversely, assign one bus to each matching triple in batch order. Each duty consumes exactly B, stays within [0,B] throughout, and ends the third service empty. Provide one terminal connector of power 1 in compatible energy/time units, unit charging efficiency, and a common terminal window [3,3+nB]. Refill buses serially for B time units apiece. This meets the connector and per-bus power limits and restores every bus to B by the deadline. The power is a fixed constant; only the adequate window length is encoded from the input. No bus is used as an energy carrier for another bus. If a strictly positive prescribed reserve R is desired, capacity can instead be B+R with full initial/terminal inventory and the same usable energy B; the zero-reserve proposition already establishes the boundary.

The construction has polynomial size. On the strongly hard source family, B and nB are polynomially bounded, so terminal-window encoding does not hide a pseudo-polynomial blowup. This restricted scheduling problem is in NP: a pair of batch permutations specifies the duties; triple totals are checked exactly, and the explicit serial terminal schedule completes any feasible assignment. Thus its decision version is strongly NP-complete. This membership argument is for this restricted model, not a general assertion about arbitrary nonlinear continuous charging models.

## What this contributes to the exposition

The proposition is useful as a short boundary paragraph or appendix result. In two batches, assigning services produces bipartite edges and minimum-cost cardinality matchings, after which the common-window energy frontier has a small segment representation. A third batch can couple two otherwise complete adjacent matchings through a capacity constraint on the WHOLE triple. Pairwise compatibility and abundant terminal charging do not remove that coupling. Consequently, one should not suggest that the paper's cardinality-threshold classification extends unchanged merely by adding more common service batches.

It does not show that every three-batch variant is hard, that the paper's two-batch common-loss setting is maximal, or that allowing early/interbatch charging preserves this particular reduction. It does not establish a new hardness result for pricing, convex hulls, own-price support, or learning. The feasibility restriction alone gives the stated boundary; generic EVSP NP-hardness citations would be less informative about exactly what the two-batch structure buys. As a new research contribution this observation is modest and classical; as an explanation of the theorem's scope it is precise and relevant.
