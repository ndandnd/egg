# Conceptual focus after the complete-graph and curvature extensions

Source-only editorial/scientific review of current main.tex, related_work.tex, the existing critical review04 and strict_curvature_robustness.tex. No new literature, computation, data, proof audit or manuscript edit. The new constructions strengthen scope, but do not justify another parallel contribution list.

## One primary contribution

Lead with **an assignment-dependent physical frontier that reduces the joint choice of duties, fleet size and charging to one minimum-requirement matching value per cardinality under explicit common-window/common-loss assumptions**. This identifies what a reader can compute and what must be retained beyond a fixed-population flexibility aggregate. The lossless exact load–cost/support classification is its transparent specialization; common losses require two load coordinates and an efficient rather than full projection. This is one connected physical question, not a sequence of unrelated pricing, V2G, complexity and verification results.

The new complete-graph example is a sharp boundary to that reduction: once task energy depends on the matched edge, early requirement alone can discard convex-optimal assignments even though every perfect matching is feasible. Strict curvature and the relative neighborhood eliminate two convenient explanations—flat terminal cost and isolated capacity equality—but remain a boundary illustration, not a separate general robustness theory. The exact public certificate is independent existence evidence in a richer model; its strength is the exhaustive sign, not calibration or a tiny displayed number. Those two supporting roles should frame the main theorem rather than compete with it.

## Which claims are standard composition?

Support iff physical/hull values agree, lost-opportunity-cost interpretation, hull tangent certificates and rational quadratic face certificates are classical convex analysis/pricing. Minimum-cost matching convexity and interval Minkowski aggregation are classical. Finite-branch continuity gives the new relative neighborhood once explicit strict feasibility margins are proved; continuity itself is not a new theorem. Assignment potentials in the complete-graph construction are a standard dual certificate, and independent binary choices explain its many branches. Collinearity and symbolic compression make exponential branch count a caution against one statistic, not a complexity breakthrough.

The candidate original content is the **specific physical sufficient-statistic reduction and its exact scope/boundary**, including gross connector use and assignment-created vehicle-cost layers. Its ingredients are standard; assembling them into an exact physical classification may still be useful, but novelty is not established merely by multiplying extensions. Current related work appropriately acknowledges the closest aggregation/loss/pricing tools and the unresolved full-formulation comparison with Wu2019. An outside expert could still judge the physical reduction too tailored. No editorial phrasing can rule out that judgment or create an ML contribution.

## Suggested replacement abstract

> Service assignments change both an electric-bus fleet's charging opportunities and its vehicle cost. We characterize this joint physical frontier for heterogeneous services in two batches with common charging windows, a fixed successor graph and full battery replenishment. With common conversion losses and shared gross connector limits, one minimum-requirement matching value per cardinality determines an efficient early-load, terminal-load and vehicle-cost frontier with at most two segments per cardinality. It preserves physical and convexified planning values for coordinatewise nondecreasing supply costs; the lossless specialization gives an exact load–cost classification and own-marginal-price support criterion. The matching and price-support machinery is classical. Our physical reduction also has a precise limitation: edge-dependent compulsory transition consumption can retain a positive support gap on a complete assignment graph even when every perfect matching is feasible. The obstruction persists with strongly convex separable supply cost and in a relative open numerical neighborhood under fixed timing and access. Separately checked rational certificates establish a positive gap on one 37-service timetable under explicitly distinguished binary and exact-additive consumption models. These are model-specific existence results with synthetic coefficients, not evidence of operational prevalence or calibrated savings.

This removes the abstract's V2G/equipment/parameter catalog and detailed public interval. Keep those results in the paper, with their actual assumptions; the abstract need not enumerate them all. If “frontier” could imply complete projection, retain the word “efficient” every time the common-loss result is summarized.

## Suggested introduction spine and prose

Keep the opening operational paragraph and complete-fleet/fixed-posted-price definition. Replace the current multi-paragraph list of extensions with the following three paragraphs, then point readers to related work and the theorem sections:

> The difficulty is not a complicated load-only feasible set. Assigning services to the same bus can impose an early recharge requirement while reducing the number of paid vehicles. A fixed-population charging aggregate does not retain that tradeoff. We identify a restricted physical class in which it can be described exactly: heterogeneous two-batch services, common charging windows and conversion efficiencies, a fixed successor graph, shared gross connector use and full terminal replenishment. A minimum-requirement matching at each cardinality suffices to construct the efficient joint energy–vehicle-cost frontier. Standard convexification then turns that physical description into planning and own-price support decisions. In the lossless specialization the full cost-labelled projection is available; with losses, dominated schedules are discarded only for monotone value objectives.

> We delineate why this reduction works. A common transition-energy task can be included because total energy stays fixed within a matching cardinality. Edge-dependent tasks break that property. A complete-graph construction admits every perfect matching yet retains a positive gap, even with strongly convex supply cost and strict feasibility margins. Its relative-neighborhood statement keeps the discrete topology, charging access and ideal common-window physics fixed. The example therefore rules out several simple explanations for the obstruction without claiming generic routing hardness, exponential hull complexity or empirical robustness.

> The public-timetable analysis serves a different purpose. Exact physical lower bounds and independently admitted complete-plan mixture uppers establish existence beyond the two-batch class. The original binary-consumption certificate concerns its declared movement graph. A separately defined exact-additive law, fresh target certificates and a dominance argument cover finite walks over specified paired arcs and continuous waiting. These distinct models share synthetic economics and do not establish empirical road completeness or operational prevalence. Small exact qualification suites and the original-model portable verifier address implementation trust, not generalization or third-party replication.

The existing first introductory explanation that “every plan buys the same total service energy” should be explicitly confined to its lossless, assignment-independent-energy specialization. It should not be read as a claim about conversion losses or the newly added edge-dependent task model. Keep V2G, equipment, replication, three-batch hardness and the detailed many-piece construction as named consequences/boundaries with short pointers; their full arguments need not lengthen the introduction. A coherent first draft can be strong without claiming all these observations as equally primary or promising a venue fit the work does not have.
