# Decision on one prospective frontier comparison

Source-only advice, after reading the common-loss planning/support subsection and CRITICAL_RESEARCH_REVIEW04. No actual outcomes, implementation or data were inspected or computed.

**Recommendation: useful as one modest algorithmic demonstration, but optional for this first draft and subordinate to resolving the public model-scope question.** The existing exact raw-physics qualification already checks the reduction's correctness on small cases. A fresh generic formulation comparison would add value only by showing what decisions the reduction delivers, their certificate sizes and the actual end-to-end work saved. It would not resolve the novelty objection that classical tools are composed under restrictive assumptions, or establish operational prevalence. Do not run it merely to obtain a speedup table.

The non-tautological question is: given the same original two-batch physical input, can the frontier pipeline deliver physical D, convexified CH, a nonnegative own-price score and an exact support/gap conclusion with a smaller independently checkable certificate than a direct joint assignment/SOC/charging formulation? Record assignment count/fleet choice, loads, complete tie information where promised, D and CH bounds, and whether strict separation or a supported plan is actually certified. A solver incumbent or small native optimality gap is not an exact support decision.

If chosen, freeze one small cohort before any outcome: twelve synthetic instances from three predetermined batch sizes, two fixed graph rules (complete and one explicitly specified sparse rule), and two efficiency regimes (unit and one common lossy rational pair). Fix all energy-generation rules/seeds, rational economics, fees and shared-resource formulas prospectively. Sizes must be selected for an intended decision workload, not by pilot runtimes. The same source-generated physical input goes to both methods. Do not reject infeasible instances, require a positive gap, tune fees after seeing classifications, or search for a hard/easy cohort. Twelve is a descriptive demonstration, not a statistically representative benchmark; fewer cells would conflate scaling, graph and loss changes. The current ten qualified literals are correctness controls, not this independent runtime cohort.

The baseline should independently encode bus assignments, full initial/terminal SOC, charging/discharging signs, efficiencies, per-bus capacity and BOTH shared gross budgets, with all allowed matchings admitted. It must not consume r_m, frontier segments, candidate solutions or reduction-derived cuts/warm starts. Permit ordinary solver presolve and its documented default cuts. Freeze solver/version/threads, hardware, objective tolerances and one equal inclusive budget per assigned instance. Charge matching, frontier generation, physical/hull optimization and exact certificate construction to the reduction pipeline; charge model construction, baseline solve and its certificate work to the baseline. Report search and verification separately, plus combined time/RSS, without adding overlapping child CPU twice.

A direct mixed-integer physical model supplies D, not CH. Comparing its D against the frontier's CH would be invalid. For CH, either provide a separately justified generic complete-plan convexification/column-generation baseline with a globally certified pricing oracle, or explicitly report CH and support certificates as additional frontier outputs with NO claimed baseline CH runtime comparison. This second option is much smaller and more defensible for a first demonstration. At a frozen proposed price, the direct physical model can independently solve the private-response problem; native bounds remain distinct from exact certification. Generic enumeration of every full plan merely to manufacture a CH baseline is unlikely to be a fair useful comparator at the larger sizes.

Every predeclared cell remains in the denominator. Distinguish certified optimum, bounded incumbent, infeasible with certificate, timeout, resource exhaustion, formulation/error and unattempted dependency; preserve both methods' outcomes even when their counterpart fails. Report verified mismatches rather than silently harmonizing tolerances. Native timing comparisons should not be sold as asymptotic complexity evidence, external replication or empirical superiority. The strongest plausible conclusion is that the physical reduction can make these particular planning/support decisions and certificate checks compactly on a fixed demonstration suite. If implementing the independent baseline and fair accounting would crowd out scope clarification or readable exposition, defer this experiment rather than append a superficial benchmark.
