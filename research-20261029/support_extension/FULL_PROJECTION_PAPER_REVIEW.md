## Scope checks that pass

The new full theorem expressly allows repeated FINITE early directions, with no switching/session/cycle/degradation penalties, B>0 and a nonempty existing fleet. Its C/X totals are integrated sequential actions, not simultaneous physical charge/export. The constructive proof gives finite chunks and shared-power serialization; it does not merely drop complementarity. It returns the COMPLETE projected union, so arbitrary economic objectives on (E,L,c) and negative period-price VALUE queries need no monotonicity. Convex F only supplies standard convex planning within polygons/on their hull. Asymmetric purchase/export settlement must retain C/X, as lossy.tex states; arbitrary intrawindow rates and time-dependent switching objectives are excluded.

The older efficient Z deliberately removes cycles and may lower E while keeping L fixed. It therefore still needs coordinatewise monotone economics/nonnegative prices, and its supporting-gradient test remains qualified by nonnegative p. The negative-price example correctly has D=CH=−3/2 for its LINEAR objective, contrasting full value−3/2 with efficient-only−1; it is not mislabeled as a positive gap or equilibrium. The continuous-cardinality warning still applies: a complete-plan hull uses generator weights, not substitution of averaged r_m into physical constraints.

Paid-idle statements are consistent after the addition. With ZERO transition energy and total fleet<=2n, edge deletion plus an idle-to-singleton replacement preserves N,S,P,T and fee; the full finite-cycle polygon then reproduces the same C/X/load point, giving literal projected-set equality. The efficient proof alone gives only dominance. With common d>0, transition_energy.tex:94–100 correctly withholds full idle elimination: deleting an edge reduces S and terminal load. Its full common-d corollary is for SERVICE-ASSIGNED fleets, with b_j+d<=B and a separate positive-duration noncharging task interval. None of these results grants uncapped idle redundancy, a new charging location or arbitrary road tasks.

The common-d formulas replace S by S_m in BOTH refill accounting and the lower exchange bound, and retain r_m from the restricted graph. At fixed m only the matching requirement varies, so the full-polygon containment proof follows. Its monotone two-segment corollary and its arbitrary-objective polygon corollary are separate, correctly labelled claims; there is no attempted transfer of the lossless scalar support criterion.

## Contribution clarity and prior-art restraint

The revised abstract/introduction now have a clearer primary statement: one physical matching statistic per cardinality determines the full two-period load--vehicle-cost set under an explicit repeated-action law. The smaller monotone frontier is a consequence rather than a competing general claim. Related work appropriately attributes conversion accounting, time sharing, box aggregation, matching convexity, vertex counting and support duality. The substantive claim is the exact SOC-feasible replacement across duty assignments with shared resources, not a new convex-hull algorithm, generic storage model or fast scheduling method. This remains a tailored physical theorem whose significance needs expert judgment; no extra broad priority claim follows from this review.

For readability, keep the full-versus-efficient distinction adjacent to both theorem statements and avoid accumulating more examples in the introduction. The full theorem already gives a compelling decision consequence—negative-price queries need the retained cycling points—without an ML label or a runtime benchmark. Its cycle permissiveness is the price of that exactness and should remain as visible as the five-vertex count. The three wording edits above are sufficient for the present integration; no further theoretical extension or literature search is requested by this review.
