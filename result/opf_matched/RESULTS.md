## What the matched comparison resolved

We kept all 144 registered assignments (72 primary, 72 secondary). Each has a rank-fixed duty-first control, an energy-aware duty-first control at minimum fleet size, a joint whole-bus optimizer and a whole-schedule convexification. The two duty-first controls optimize subsequent charging. The original joint solver cleared every criterion on only three assignments. ## Why the duty-first baseline matters

The Energinet files contained 24 verified DE delivery hours apiece. The full German day-ahead ranges were €71.05/MWh in January and €177.15/MWh in July. These cannot clear the original homogeneous one-hour threshold of €527.78/MWh. Across the new fleets, every one of the 36 distinct feasible wholesale comparisons also chose its minimum bus count and matched the strong duty-first control. Market variants repeat these wholesale calculations three times. The rank-fixed matching cost €0.40–€6.54 more in six comparisons, all January Sistig designs. The upstream prices alone could therefore reward a different *pairing* while keeping exactly the same number of buses.

At minimum fleet size the heterogeneous peak runs changed the forced early-charge floor by **53.63–549.04 kWh** across alternative matchings, depending on the depot size. The homogeneous spread was zero throughout. The strong control captures this flexibility before observing prices by computing the matching with the lowest early-charge requirement. At a given count that matching includes every attainable early load in this two-window model. General schedules with staggered windows and transitions need not have this property.

As an additional numerical check on the 13 strict assignments where the projected rank-fixed cover missed the hull by more than one cent, we evaluated the grid-cost supporting plane from the hull's depot prices. In all 13 its slope was positive and the rank-fixed lower endpoint exceeded the hull energy. The tangent is therefore minimized over the fixed interval at that endpoint. Adding the common bus fee gives an estimate for the best possible fixed-cover cost; directly replaying its schedule gives an upper estimate. Relative to the AC-checked strong cover the tangent estimates ranged from €0.4034 to €6.5147, and the achieved differences from €0.4034 to €6.5166 (five distinct January site/size designs). An independent dual calculation replaces the hull’s minimum fleet fee-plus-price at those same prices with the fixed cover’s minimum, adds the difference to the reported hull dual objective, and gives the same range to four decimals. Repeated market rules are not independent evidence. The tangent uses the stored conic grid primal and nodal dual estimates, so these are numerical comparisons. They do not assume the grid relaxation is exact at other loads. [Underlying values](lift01/assignments.csv)

## Scope and record

For nested attainable-load intervals with a common upper endpoint and a discrete-convex lower endpoint, rounding a hull-optimal fractional count down keeps its entire load, all grid conditions and any backup unchanged and increases its fee by strictly less than one bus-day fee. The bound is sharp and applies to the entire two-window schedule. A binding cap between fleet levels alone does not force a gap; a smooth grid cost can give a positive gap at a unique price. This study compares optimized dispatches. It did not run a price-update loop, test V2G discharge or estimate real local generator bids.
