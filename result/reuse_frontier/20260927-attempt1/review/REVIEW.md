## Why the pricing check covers continuous charging

The DP is not an arbitrary integer approximation to a continuous problem.
For each fixed chain, introduce an inventory flow at every service and
charging boundary. An initial inventory of 20 enters the chain; service arcs
withdraw the fixed integer demands (15 or 0); charging arcs admit between 0
and 10 units; inventory carry arcs have capacity 20; and terminal inventory
has the integer lower bound 0 or 20. This is a capacitated flow/transshipment
polytope with integral supplies and lower/upper bounds. Its node-arc incidence
matrix is totally unimodular, so every nonempty such polytope has an integral
optimal vertex for any linear charging-price objective.

The audit consequently enumerates integer SOC states 0 through 20 and integer
charge additions through 10 without losing a continuous linear optimum.
All prices are converted to their **exact binary rational input values**;
a common denominator converts comparisons and additions to Python integers.
No floating point optimization or solver tolerance chooses the optimum.
The reported numerical comparison to saved objectives is a separate step.

All depot and direct arcs have identical zero deadhead time, energy and cost
in these frozen fixtures. Replacing a direct arc by a depot arc preserves
every direct-arc realization by choosing zero charge and permits additional
charging. Thus all-depot chains suffice to find the best linear-price value
for each independently enumerated partition. There is no shared constraint
coupling the chains, so their minimum costs add. Minimizing over all
partitions therefore gives the global pricing value for the complete
continuous feasible set. This reduction is specific to the verified
fixtures; it is not asserted for arbitrary EGG instances, partial windows,
nonintegral capacities, nonzero deadheads or shared chargers.

## A direct independent lower bound for every successful cell

The audit also bypasses the recorded master/pricing-bound formula. Let
`V(q) = min_s [c(s) + q·L(s)]`, calculated by the exact DP above. For the
nonnegative-load cost `F(L) = Σ[a_t L_t + b_t L_t²/2]`, define

`D(q) = V(q) − Σ max(q_t − a_t, 0)²/(2 b_t)`.

Fenchel's inequality gives `D(q) ≤ z_CH` for every supplied price vector.
The script evaluates this expression with rational arithmetic and takes the
best bound over **completed clean prices within each state**. Seed,
proposal, predecessor and separate-reference prices do not enter that
state's independent bound.

For all 44 certified cells, the recorded lower bound is no greater than this
independent bound within numerical guard, and the physically replayed upper
bound minus the independent bound has the recorded numerical range
**0.000025334433885859653 to 0.0009124833710245639**, below the frozen 0.01
target. These endpoints are floating-point differences of a saved numerical
upper bound and the rational lower bound converted to a float; they are not
exact rational widths of formally feasible primal witnesses. This is a stronger check than
agreement between two implementations using the same CBC backend.

The linear-pricing calculations and these dual expressions are exact for
the supplied binary rational prices/coefficients. The saved master weights,
charging witnesses and objective evaluations are floating point; their
feasibility and upper-bound interpretation remain numerical within the
explicit replay tolerances. This is not an exact-arithmetic certificate of
every physical witness, and should not be described as one in the paper.

**Physical-witness convention for manuscript wording:** JSON numeric tokens
are read as Python binary64 values. Prices and supply coefficients alone are
converted with `Fraction(value)`, preserving their exact binary values rather
than replacing 0.2 by an intended decimal rational. The physical columns,
convex weights, master loads and reference charges are not rationalized,
rounded, snapped to SOC boundaries, renormalized or repaired. They are replayed
as stored with the stated absolute tolerances. Zero-weight reference blocks
are checked in scaled coordinates. Thus use “exact rational pricing and dual
bounds with numerical physical replay,” not “exact rational physical
certificates,” for the reuse experiment. This differs from the separate
cyclic construction's rationally specified physical witnesses.

## Finding: saved master tangent lists include a later cut

At the frozen source version, the driver assigns
`tangents = rmp["tangent_points"]`, appends `rmp` to `master_events`, and
sometimes executes `tangents.append(list(rmp["L"]))` after completing the
pricing/certificate iteration. These lists alias. The recorded master can
therefore contain one tangent that was added **after that master solve**.
Blindly evaluating its saved PWL model using all recorded tangent points
can disagree with its recorded `z_model`.

The reconstruction rule is unambiguous on these completed artifacts:

1. Pair each master with its chronological clean-pricing event and, if
   completed, the matching iteration row.
2. A completed nonterminal iteration appends exactly one point when its gap
   exceeds 0.01, except the branch that tightens pricing tolerance because
   `reduced_lower < −10⁻⁶` and `reduced_upper ≥ −10⁻⁶`.
3. For precisely that branch, require the last saved point to equal the
   master's `L`, then remove that last point from an in-memory audit copy.
4. Terminal iterations and the final failed pricing attempt append none.

This rule follows the frozen control flow rather than selecting whichever
tangent set best fits an objective. No other failed branch occurred here.
The next `solve_rmp` copies the incoming tangent list, so later iterations do
not continue modifying earlier records. All 145 affected records are listed
in the audit JSON. After this reconstruction, every final PWL primal value
and primal-dual value agrees with `z_model` to at most 2.14 × 10⁻¹⁴.
The remaining column dual-feasibility and complementarity checks also pass.

**Recommended prospective repair:** deep-copy master evidence at the point
it is recorded, add a regression test for post-recording tangent mutation,
and document the repair separately. Do not edit the first-run result JSON or
silently change the identity of the frozen experiment. The independently
computed Fenchel bounds above do not require this snapshot repair.

## Reference independence and its limit

Every reference's full structure set was independently regenerated. All
scaled charge blocks—including zero-weight infeasible structures—were
replayed without dividing by their weights. The aggregate loads, operating
cost, true objective, numerical allowance and guarded interval arithmetic
match. Every successful cell's interval overlaps the corresponding
reference interval as recorded in the final summary.

The audit additionally computes an independent exact Fenchel lower bound
at each reference's recorded primal gradient. Those independent
primal-dual widths range from **0.0019611 to 0.0225984**. They are valid
but looser than the native reference's approximately 10⁻⁵ PWL stopping
accuracy. Consequently the audit **does not independently establish every
reference's tight native lower bound**. It verifies the final witnesses,
complete feasible-set enumeration and reported interval arithmetic, with
native optimality metadata remaining conditional evidence for that tighter
interval. Likewise, it reconciles all 804 inner LP call records but checks
independent KKT for the 190 returned masters, not every unsaved inner LP
primal solution. Neither limitation affects the direct independent clean
certificates for the 44 successful comparison cells.

## Accounting and interpretation for the manuscript

| Arm | Seed attempts | Clean attempts | Proposal attempts | All attempts |

| Retained | 3 | 41 | 0 | 44 |

Counts include the failed cold attempt. They are discovery/work observations,
not successful time-to-solution statistics for all 45 cells. Wall times
include the failed cell and cannot be converted into a population runtime
speedup claim. Wrapper counts include possible LP-first work and are not
the number of individual native optimization invocations.

Both reuse arms had **the same clean-call count in every matched cell**.
Across the 12 transitions after initial solves, plain retention needed 17
clean calls, so five calls remained beyond one mandatory clean verification
per transition. The shifted proposal added twelve solves while generating
three projection-novel columns; those columns yielded no observed reduction
in clean calls. This distinguishes discovery from useful saved work and is
an informative negative result for this particular proposal.

These results support a bounded claim that physical-column retention can
reduce repeated pricing work while fresh certificates are checked on these
three prospectively fixed synthetic trajectories. They do not establish
learning efficacy, operating-scale speedup, a fleet gap under replenishment,
or a universal advantage for warm starts. The depleted fixtures explicitly
credit additional buses with additional initial stored energy. The
replenished fixture forces two buses using modeled terminal markers. Both
scope restrictions should be prominent in any table or figure caption.

The fake-oracle source tests cover useful status, identity, stale-bound,
proposal and replay gates, but did not catch the mutable tangent evidence.
The independent corrupted-copy controls additionally reject cost, physical
energy, window, aggregate load, convex weight, dual multiplier, PWL value,
pricing upper/lower, proposal-bound contamination, predecessor digest,
certificate lower, attempt counts, frozen status, missing reference
structure, scaled SOC, reference objective and reference interval errors.

No further experimental retry is justified by this review. Preserve this
first-run evidence, apply the logging repair prospectively, and keep any
larger diagnostic or learning comparison behind a new frozen design.
