## Findings and claim boundaries

This is the relevant product-set factorization. Relabeling fleets cannot improve z_D, z_CH or summed fixed-price regret. Maximum per-participant regret is not invariant to regrouping, because a group's regret is its members' sum. The present assertion checks individual-versus-joint factorization at each physical profile's own price; it does not literally enumerate arbitrary intermediate partitions, which follow algebraically. Describe the check at that precision.

2. **Capacity-witness LOC must name its agent.** In lines 131–139, `fleet_loc_at_ch_price` uses `joint_value`, whose feasible set already incorporates the shared early cap. Its zero value in the witness is therefore correct for one jointly constrained fleet agent. It is **not** the sum of unrestricted individual fleet LOCs at the energy price; that sum is 2. The separate fields `unrestricted_individual_regrets` and `capacity_priced_individual_regrets` correctly expose the distinction. Avoid a caption suggesting “zero fleet uplift” without the joint-agent qualification.

3. **The alternative games are properly separated numerically.** The planner chooses load (1,1), objective 3 and energy gradient p=(1,3). The late fleet's unrestricted posted-price improvement is 2. Residual-capacity deviations forbid its move because the early slot is occupied; both local regrets then vanish. These deviations hold p fixed, as lines 175–186 show. This is a shared-constraint, price-taking equilibrium interpretation, or a generalized game at posted prices, not a verification of a strategic game that recomputes p after a deviation. The distinction matters even if this particular numerical profile also happens to satisfy another game's equilibrium conditions.

4. **The scarcity price and transfer arithmetic are coherent.** τ=2 makes consumer prices (3,3), so both physical choices are individual best responses. This supports the selected split; it does not guarantee capacity feasibility under arbitrary independent tie breaking, since both fleets could select early. The early-slot capacity complementarity condition holds and rent is τ·1=2. If the fee is collected separately, consumers pay 6: energy supplier revenue is 4, supplier cost is 3 and supplier profit is 1; the charger owner receives 2. The zero supplier LOC means optimality of the supplier's realized output at p, not zero supplier profit. This accounting does not specify rent ownership/rebates, participation compensation, strategic truthfulness or a budget-balanced mechanism. No such mechanism is established by the witness.

5. **The scaling comparison is correctly about different model families.** Fixed block energy with fixed slope, slope 1/N, and fixed total energy are not interchangeable notions of a large market. For homogeneous odd N the stored minimum total own-price regret is bE²(N+1)/2 and minimum largest local regret is bE², while Δ=bE²/4 and Δ/z_CH=1/N². Thus vanishing relative gap does not imply vanishing absolute individual regret at fixed slope. At CH prices the same homogeneous example puts all Δ in supplier LOC, with zero fleet LOC. Both price and normalization must accompany the metric. For heterogeneous cases the minima of total and maximum local regret need not occur at the same profile; they are separate statistics.

## Conditional theory addition

The Shapley–Folkman/smoothness bound in the [literature memo](market-theory-literature.md) is appropriate as a **derived classical consequence**, separate from measured results. Required premises: compact independent physical menus; additive intrinsic cost; convex F with B-Lipschitz gradient on the aggregate hull; an exact convexified optimum and its exposed local best-response faces. Define r as the dimension of the span of the union of **within-local-face load differences**, not the affine span of pooled absolute loads.

Translate each face by one of its own points and apply Shapley–Folkman. At most r local mixtures need rounding. Round within those faces; independence preserves feasibility. Their intrinsic-cost change cancels the linear term p*·δL, so the objective loss is the Bregman remainder of F and is bounded by B||δL||²/2. With active-face load diameters D_i, this gives Δ≤(B/2)(Σ of the largest min(r,N) D_i)². Shared capacity invalidates the feasibility step unless separately protected. This is an upper bound on planner gap, not an upper bound on own-price regret or cash uplift.
