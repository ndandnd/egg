## 1. Closest primary sources and what they already establish

1. **Gu and Qin, “Competitive Equilibrium for Electricity Markets with Spatially Flexible Loads,” arXiv:2510.26036v4, 9 September 2026 — A.** The live record still identifies v4 as latest. Section II-C, Assumptions 1–2 require compact convex decisions, linear load mapping and strictly convex collective preference. Remark 2 permits continuous quantities or relaxed counts. Section III-A, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 characterize equilibrium through a convex program and establish existence under their conditions. An indivisible complete-duty menu violates the convexity premise. Their collective preference also need not be EGG’s social cost. The paper distinguishes price taking from strategic price anticipation. [Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26036), [inspected full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26036v4#S2.SS3).

2. **Yao, Liu, Scaglione, Bekhor and Zhang, “Integrated equilibrium model for electrified logistics and power systems,” arXiv:2505.04532v1, 7 May 2025 — A.** The record has no newer version. The logistics response uses perturbed-utility MDP flows and couples to DC optimal power flow. Propositions 3–5 and the appendix explain continuity and the fixed-point argument. Strictly convex perturbations and locally unchanged, independent active OPF constraints differ from discontinuous atomic duty choice. Brouwer requires a nonempty compact convex invariant domain and global continuity; the printed compact-domain statement and local regularity should not be silently generalized. This is an assumption boundary, not a claim that their reported numerical solution is invalid. [Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04532), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04532v1#S2.SS4).

3. **Zoltowska and Lin, “Optimal Charging Schedule Planning for Electric Buses Using Aggregated Day-Ahead Auction Bids,” Energies 14(16), 4727, published 4 August 2021 — A; especially close new antecedent.** Sections 2.1–2.3 use mixed-integer timetable/charger models to produce aggregate bid parameters, clear a linear representation of bus demand in an energy auction, and disaggregate to minute-level charging. Equation (24) supplies energy-balance prices. Section 2.3 asserts that auction solutions can be disaggregated using the proposed aggregation. Thus bus bids, charging indivisibilities, charger scarcity and endogenous auction prices are not new. EGG can instead test exact complete-duty price support and explicitly distinguish a safe compressed bid representation from the complete projected hull. I did not independently prove their universal disaggregation assertion. [Publisher PDF, §§2.1–2.3, pp.5–8; correct DOI 10.3390/en14164727](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/energies/energies-14-04727/article_deploy/energies-14-04727.pdf?version=1628071003).

4. **Maldonado and Saumweber, “Why Do Pricing Rules Matter? Electricity Market Design with Electric Vehicle Participants,” World Electric Vehicle Journal 13(8), 143, published 2 August 2022 — A.** Section 4 discusses convex-hull, IP and extended locational marginal pricing and their economic properties. Section 5.3 models state of charge, driving requirements, continuous charging quantities and binary charge/discharge mode; Section 6 compares pricing rules with EV participants. This directly occupies EV pricing/uplift territory. It does not establish EGG’s complete mandatory-duty certificate. Its distinction between individual compensation and financial balance reinforces the need to specify who pays each transfer. [Publisher PDF, §§4–6, DOI 10.3390/wevj13080143](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/wevj/wevj-13-00143/article_deploy/wevj-13-00143.pdf?version=1659430799).

5. **Madani, Ruiz, Siddiqui and Van Vyve, arXiv:1804.00048v1, 30 March 2018 — A, carried forward from the prior review.** Nonconvex demand, minimum acceptance and intertemporal constraints are explicit. Section 3, Theorem 1, equations (29)–(31), connects the dual to minimum aggregate lost-opportunity compensation. **Andrianesis, Bertsimas, Caramanis and Hogan, arXiv:2012.13331v1, 24 December 2020 — A, prior review:** Section III already provides exact convex-hull pricing with schedule columns/Dantzig–Wolfe decomposition. Neither nonconvex demand uplift nor schedule column generation is an EGG novelty claim. [Madani full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.00048v1#S3), [Andrianesis full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.13331v1#S3).

6. **Hümbs, Martin and Schewe, “Exploiting complete linear descriptions for decentralized power market problems with integralities,” Mathematical Methods of Operations Research 95, 451–474, published online 26 March 2022 — A.** Section 2, Theorem 1 establishes price-supported equilibrium iff the centralized relaxation has an integral optimum, using complete participant hull descriptions. Their discussion explicitly warns that an ordinary incomplete LP relaxation can falsely suggest nonexistence. Their linear model is different from EGG’s convex supply cost, but this is a particularly direct precedent for the certificate’s complete-hull requirement. It also points to Harks’ earlier equivalent characterization. [Full article and Theorem 1, DOI 10.1007/s00186-022-00775-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00186-022-00775-z).

7. **Bichler, Knörr and Maldonado, “Pricing in Nonconvex Markets: How to Price Electricity in the Presence of Demand Response,” Information Systems Research 34(2), 652–675, 2023 — A for the author manuscript.** Definitions 2–4 and Section 4 distinguish price stability, budget balance, penalties and minimum make-whole payments. Minimizing losses needed for participation is different from compensating every profitable deviation. These are alternative institutional objectives, not competing algebraic identities. EGG should not label all LOC as an operator’s cash requirement or claim that a small uplift proves incentive compatibility. The accessed author PDF is a manuscript, so its page numbers differ from the final journal pagination. [Author manuscript](https://pub.dss.in.tum.de/bichler-research/2021_Bichler_Electricity_Markets.pdf), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2022.1139).

## 2. Aggregation: closest precedents and theorem assumptions

8. **Hreinsson, Scaglione, Alizadeh and Chen, “New Insights From the Shapley-Folkman Lemma on Dispatchable Demand in Energy Markets,” IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 36(5), 4028–4041, September 2021 — B.** The primary abstract explicitly applies Shapley–Folkman to aggregate convex/nonconvex demand action sets and costs, then develops reduced polytope and ellipsoid representations. This is direct prior art against “demand aggregation becomes convex” as a new claim. Publisher/institution metadata and abstract were verified; full text was not recovered, so no particular convergence rate or omitted hypothesis is asserted here. [Publisher record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9376954/), [author-institution record, DOI 10.1109/TPWRS.2021.3065913](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/new-insights-from-the-shapley-folkman-lemma-on-dispatchable-deman/).

9. **Vujanic, Mohajerin Esfahani, Goulart and Morari, “Electric Vehicles Aggregator Optimization: A Fast and Solver-Free Solution Method,” CDC 2014, 5027–5032 — B.** Indexed author-paper text includes binary fixed-rate EV charging, terminal energy requirements and distribution constraints. Its decomposable mixed-integer framework relates approximation quality to subsystem count relative to coupling dimension and contracts resources for feasible recovery. The direct PDF retrieval failed; this memo does not claim a checked numerical rate. It is enough to establish that binary EV aggregation with dual decomposition long predates EGG. [Author publication record](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~engs1373/kw_power.html), [author manuscript link](https://mohajerinesfahani.github.io/Publications/conference/2014/EV.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2014.7040174).

10. **Bi and Tang, arXiv:1610.05416v3, 22 January 2018; journal “Duality Gap Estimation via a Refined Shapley–Folkman Lemma,” SIAM Journal on Optimization 30(2), 2020 — A.** Theorem 1 states that a point in the convexified sum of sets in R^m needs convexification of at most m summands. Section 4, Theorem 8 refines duality-gap estimates through kth nonconvexity. The model uses proper lower-semicontinuous functions with bounded domains. Care is needed for finite duty sets: encoding discreteness through an extended-valued indicator can make a function nonconvexity bound infinite. A geometric decomposition is not automatically a finite monetary certificate or a feasible rounding algorithm. [Inspected v3, §§1,4](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05416v3), [journal DOI 10.1137/18M1174805](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/18M1174805).

11. **Dubois-Taine and d’Aspremont, “Frank-Wolfe meets Shapley-Folkman,” arXiv:2406.18282v2, 3 February 2025 — A.** Sections 3.3.2.1–3 explicitly distinguish sampling feasible in expectation from physical feasibility. Reconstruction uses either a downward-replacement domain assumption or constraint tightening with a Slater margin (Assumption 3.2). Theorem 3.5 retains a finite-iteration feasibility residual. Mandatory service and saturated shared chargers do not automatically supply the required slack or downward replacement. This is a useful constructive comparison, not a ready-made fleet feasibility guarantee. [Full text, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18282v2#S3.SS3).

12. **Milgrom and Watt, “A Walrasian Mechanism with Markups for Nonconvex Markets,” Review of Economic Studies 93(3), May 2026, 1995–2020; online 16 September 2025 — A from the prior review.** Section 3, Theorem 1, and Section 4/Appendix B combine rationing, budget surplus and Shapley–Folkman rounding. Buyers’ zero-consumption option and allowable excess supply are material differences from mandatory fleet service. Its guarantee cannot be transferred merely because both settings are nonconvex. [Final article, DOI 10.1093/restud/rdaf067](https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/93/3/1995/8255713).

**Recent frontier screened, not imported:** Dey and Xu, arXiv:2601.19003v1, 26 January 2026, studies improved Shapley–Folkman error bounds. Its assumptions and Theorem 3 separate a geometric error from sensitivity to changing the constraint RHS; later rates require smoothness/coercivity and other regularity. Binary fleet domains do not automatically satisfy these premises. [Selected full text — A](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19003v1). ## 3. Analytical implications for EGG

### Relabeling is not fragmentation

For independent fleet menus P_i={(c_i,L_i)}, the aggregate physical cost/load set is the Minkowski sum P=Σ_i P_i. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{conv}(\textstyle\sum_i P_i)=\textstyle\sum_i\operatorname{conv}P_i,
\qquad V(p)=\textstyle\sum_i\min_{s_i}[c_i(s_i)+p\cdot L_i(s_i)].
\]

Putting exactly the same independent fleet choices under one aggregator therefore preserves physical and convexified objectives, the existence of common price support, and aggregate regret at any fixed price. This is an algebraic consequence, not a new economic result. Individual reported regret can change when participants are regrouped: a group's regret is the sum of its members' regrets. The maximum per reported participant is therefore not invariant to labels.

Fragmenting a large indivisible block, replicating duties, adding shared chargers, allowing vehicle exchanges, or changing price-taking to strategic behavior changes the actual model. Each is a legitimate treatment; none is mere relabeling. Convexifying a whole jointly constrained system also differs from separately convexifying local menus while retaining shared constraints.

### A conditional upper bound complements PR52's lower certificate

The following is a proposed EGG specialization of classical Shapley–Folkman geometry and smooth convex analysis, not an originality claim. Assume compact independent menus, additive intrinsic costs, and convex F with B-Lipschitz gradient in Euclidean norm over the aggregate load hull. At a convexified optimum let p*=∇F(L*). Its local mixture can use only schedules on each fleet's exposed face

\[
A_i=\arg\min_{s_i}\{c_i(s_i)+p^*\cdot L_i(s_i)\}.
\]

Let r=dim span(∪_i{L−L′: L,L′∈L(A_i)}); r≤T. These are differences **within each local face**, followed by a common linear span, not the affine span of all absolute loads pooled across fleets. Apply Shapley–Folkman to their load sets after translating each by one of its own points into that common span. L* has a representation with physical face loads except at most r fleets. Round those remaining fleets to any physical member of the same face. Independence ensures feasibility. If δL is the resulting load change, exposed-face equality cancels the linear intrinsic-cost change, yielding exactly

\[
h(s^{round})-z_{CH}=F(L^*+\delta L)-F(L^*)-p^*\cdot\delta L
\le\tfrac B2\|\delta L\|_2^2.
\]

If D_i is the Euclidean diameter of fleet i's active face loads and D_(j) are these diameters sorted decreasingly, this implies

\[
0\le\Delta\le\tfrac B2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\min(r,N)}D_{(j)}\right)^2.
\]

This derivation needs exact exposed-face membership, a valid smoothness constant and no unaccounted hard coupling. Shared capacity can invalidate the rounded schedule. It controls a planner gap, not arbitrary-price LOC or maximum individual own-price regret. With bounded block diameters and fixed effective dimension, fixed B gives an N-independent absolute bound; B=β/N gives O(1/N); diameters O(1/N) under fixed total service give O(1/N²) for fixed B. These are distinct asymptotic models. The direct duty-menu bound may be more interpretable than importing extended-valued function nonconvexity constants.

### Exact two-slot predictions: small relative gap can conceal persistent individual regret

Take N independent identical blocks, each choosing (E,0) or (0,E), zero intrinsic cost and F_N(L)=b_N(L_1²+L_2²)/2. For even N a balanced physical schedule exists and Δ_N=0. For odd N,

\[
z_{CH}=b_NN^2E^2/4,\quad \Delta_N=b_NE^2/4,\quad
\min_s\max_i r_i(s)=b_NE^2,\quad
\min_s\sum_i r_i(s)=b_NE^2(N+1)/2.
\]

**Proof of regret formulas.** Any profile has imbalance d=|2k−N|≥1. The slot-price difference at its own gradient is b_NEd. Each fleet on the heavier side can improve its posted-price bill by b_NE²d; other fleets have zero regret. There are (N+d)/2 heavier-side fleets. Both regret expressions are minimized at d=1. This is a price-taking calculation: deviations evaluate the posted prices, without recomputing them.

| Odd-N scaling | Planner gap | Minimum largest individual regret | Minimum total own-price regret |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E fixed, b_N=b | bE²/4 | bE² | bE²(N+1)/2 |
| E fixed, b_N=β/N | βE²/(4N) | βE²/N | βE²(N+1)/(2N) |
| Total energy Q fixed, E=Q/N, b_N=b | bQ²/(4N²) | bQ²/N² | bQ²(N+1)/(2N²) |

In every row Δ_N/z_CH=1/N², but absolute incentive behavior differs sharply. Parity also rules out a monotone-in-N claim. At the common convexified price p*=(b_NNE/2,b_NNE/2), all fleets have zero LOC and the supplier has LOC=Δ_N at the physical balanced schedule. At its own gradient, supplier LOC is zero and fleet LOC is the much larger total above. Thus specifying **which price** matters as much as specifying the normalization.

At an odd-N balanced physical optimum, the smallest infinity-norm adjustment of its own gradient price that makes the schedule a zero-regret response is b_NE/2, attained by equalizing the two slot prices. This is a price error in currency/kWh, whereas regret is currency. For the existing approximation certificate, multiplying price error by a certified load diameter in kWh gives currency; no extra duration factor applies when L already stores slot energy. A coefficient expressed per MW must first be converted to the energy convention. These derivations are exact synthetic predictions, not new fleet measurements.

## 4. Three falsifiable tiny experiments

1. **Fragmentation, heterogeneity and the metric separation.** Enumerate rational two-slot menus with energies e_i and optional early intrinsic costs d_i. Compute the lower hull in (early load, intrinsic cost), solve the convex quadratic on every hull segment, and enumerate the physical optimum. Check the formulas above; then perturb energies/costs and test whether parity disappears without confusing that with universal convergence. Record absolute/relative gap, minimum total and largest individual own-price regret, CH-price fleet/supplier LOC, and price distance. Recombine the same menus into groups: z_D, z_CH and summed regret must remain identical. Failure of that invariance is an implementation or model-change signal. This is a precise diagnostic experiment, not a claim that aggregation theory is new.

2. **Shared capacity and omitted scarcity prices.** Two unit fleets choose early or late; impose early capacity one. Let a=(0,2), F(L)=a·L+||L||²/2. Joint planner load (1,1) has objective 3 and zero gap over the jointly feasible hull, but its energy gradient is (1,3). The late fleet has posted-energy-price regret 2 if its local deviations ignore the charger constraint. Adding charger scarcity price τ=2 to early charging gives effective consumer prices (3,3), supporting the split. Alternatively, restrict unilateral deviations by residual capacity; that is a generalized-Nash interpretation. Exact checks should distinguish the aggregate constrained agent, unrestricted local agents and resource-priced agents. A zero aggregate gap is not a universal certificate for all three models. If transfers are analyzed, separate the charger rent from the energy supplier's revenue.

3. **Compressed bids versus exact physical implementation.** On a tiny timetable/charger instance, enumerate or otherwise certify all complete duty alternatives and their projected energy set. Compare a proposed aggregate bid description with both that physical set and its convex hull. Test distinct questions: does every accepted bid disaggregate; does the bid retain all useful physical alternatives; how much planner value and price support are lost? An inner approximation may be safe yet conservative; a hull point may have no same-day physical implementation. This directly confronts the bus-auction antecedent rather than claiming its topic is absent. Do not attribute a counterexample to a published construction without reproducing its actual constraints and assumptions. This experiment remains a planned physical embedding, not a completed result.

## 6. Targeted addendum from successful Scholar discovery

**Manzolli, Eslami, Miranda-Moreno and Yu, “When Agents Meet Electric Bus Fleet Operations: Pricing Behavior, Trade-offs, and Policy Implications in an Aggregator Framework,” arXiv:2606.26400v1, 24 June 2026 — A.** Sections 3.2–3.3 and Appendix B describe Trigger, Pricing and Evaluator agents around a physical bus/V2G optimizer. Tariffs are bounded buy/sell multipliers of supplied grid prices, equation (B.20); agent policies replace the classical upper-level tariff maximization, (B.17). This is substantial prior art for agentic bus reoptimization and tariff governance. It is distinct from EGG's marginal-supply fixed point: the inspected formulation does not establish complete-duty convexification or minimum-LOC certificates. EGG's operational contribution should require controlled proposal quality and certified computational benefit; orchestration alone is insufficient. Conversely, do not treat their profit-oriented mode as a certified optimal upper bound simply because §3.2.2 uses that description: the shown agent replacement is not a global tariff optimizer. [Primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26400), [inspected formulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26400v1#A2).

**Rahimi Baghbadorani, Nikseresht, Cho and Ghiassi-Farrokhfal, “Dynamic Congestion Pricing in Distribution Networks via a Convex-Analytic Bilevel Reformulation,” arXiv:2607.03359v1, 3 July 2026 — A.** Assumption 1 requires closed convex follower sets, convex quadratic/extended objectives, attained responses and conjugate regularity. Theorem 1 encodes follower optimality with a Fenchel–Young equality. Section III-D uses penalized sequential convex approximation; Proposition 2 proves critical-point behavior with fixed penalty, compact level sets and exact subproblem solves. This avoids binaries introduced by KKT complementarity; it does **not** remove original indivisible fleet decisions. The gap/residual perspective is relevant to EGG, but the convex follower algorithm is not directly transferable to complete integer schedules. Useful comparison: evaluate realized global response regret at the actual offered price, while separately certifying physical feasibility and supply/resource consistency. The source explicitly disclaims a global arbitrary-bilevel solver. [Primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03359), [Assumption 1, Theorem 1 and §III-D](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03359v1#S3).
